# 14. Studying in Japan: choosing a school, scholarships and job hunting after graduation

<!-- zh-intro: 817c67e0 -->

This section covers the key things about studying in Japan on a student visa: how to choose a Japanese language school, scholarships open to privately financed international students, and whether you can stay on to keep looking if you have no job when you graduate. For the 28-hour weekly limit on part-time work for students, see Section 2, entry 10 (get permission for activities outside your status before working part-time); for the 14-day report and the 3-month limit after dropping out or changing schools, see Section 2, entry 3 (report after leaving a job, changing jobs or changing schools). Information is as of 2026-10-03.

### 1. Privately financed international students can apply through their school for MEXT's Honors Scholarship: 48,000 yen a month at university, 30,000 yen at a Japanese language school
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 私费留学生可以通过学校申请文科省的学习奖励费，大学每月 48,000 日元，日语学校 30,000 日元 | 416dd1d6 -->
- Cost: Free. Ask the office at your school that handles scholarships for international students and apply through the school's nomination process. You need to keep your grades up and sign in at school every month.
- In plain words: Privately financed international students with good grades and financial need can apply for the Ministry of Education's Honors Scholarship (学習奨励費), which does not have to be repaid. University and graduate students get 48,000 yen a month, and Japanese language school students 30,000 yen a month. You must apply through your school, and each school has a limited number of nominations. Your grade evaluation coefficient for the previous year must be 2.30 or higher.
- Benefit: The Japan Student Services Organization (JASSO) states that the "Program for Promoting the Acceptance of International Students (MEXT Honors Scholarship for Privately Financed International Students)" pays a scholarship to privately financed international students who are excellent in studies and character but find it hard to continue studying for financial reasons. Amounts: 48,000 yen a month for graduate and undergraduate level, and 30,000 yen a month at Japanese language education institutions. The payment period is in principle 1 year from April to the following March, or 6 months from October to the following March. Ask the person in charge at your school how to apply; the number of nominations per school is set by its number of enrolled international students, and some schools may not be able to apply. It is for privately financed students with the "Student" status of residence. Conditions include a grade evaluation coefficient of 2.30 or higher in the previous year, expected to be maintained during the payment period; each month you must sign the school's enrolment confirmation register
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 日本学生支援機構「留学生受入れ促進プログラム（文部科学省外国人留学生学習奨励費）について」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.jasso.go.jp/ryugaku/scholarship_j/shoreihi/about.html>
- Notes: The benefit is rated large at up to over 500,000 yen a year. University-level applicants also need Japanese or English proficiency, such as JLPT N2; see the JASSO page for details. Ask your school's international student office about other scholarships.

### 2. If you have a job offer before graduating but start work after graduating, switch to the "Designated Activities" status for waiting to start, so your student visa does not lapse unused
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
<!-- zh: 毕业前拿到内定、入职在毕业以后的，改成等入职用的「特定活动」，别让留学签证空着到期 | 72481e08 -->
- Cost: The application itself is free. You need the company that made the offer to sign a pledge to keep in regular contact with you and to notify immigration if it withdraws the offer.
- In plain words: If you graduated from a school in Japan and have a job offer (内定), but the company will only formally hire you some time later, you can stay on by switching to the "Designated Activities" (特定活動) status for waiting to start work. The condition is that you start within 1 year of the offer and within 18 months of graduating. While you wait, with permission you can work part-time up to 28 hours a week.
- Benefit: The Immigration Services Agency states that a person staying with the "Student" status, or with the "Designated Activities" status for continued job hunting, may change to "Designated Activities" for waiting to be hired if: they graduated or completed a course at an educational institution in Japan; they will be hired within 1 year of the job offer and within 1 year and 6 months of graduating; the activities at the company are expected to qualify for a change to a work status such as "Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services"; there is no problem with their residence record; and the company pledges to keep in regular contact with them and to promptly inform the regional immigration bureau if it withdraws the offer. After obtaining this "Designated Activities" status, if certain conditions are met, they may get permission for activities outside status and work part-time up to 28 hours a week; pre-employment training at the company may be permitted beyond 28 hours
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国在留管理庁「大学又は専門学校の在学中又は卒業後に就職先が内定し採用までの滞在をご希望のみなさまへ」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/resources/nyuukokukanri07_00013.html>
- Notes: As of 2026-10-03. If you have not found a job yet, you use a different "Designated Activities" status for job hunting; see entry 3 of this section (stay up to 1 more year after graduating to look for work). Before starting work you need to change status again, to the matching work visa. If the offer is withdrawn, the company must notify immigration, and you should also go to immigration quickly to discuss your next step. The benefit is rated large because it decides whether you can legally stay in Japan until you start work.

### 3. If you have no job when you graduate, get your school's recommendation and switch to "Designated Activities", which lets you stay up to 1 more year to look for work
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
<!-- zh: 毕业时工作还没定，拿学校推荐改成「特定活动」，最长可以再留 1 年找工作 | 4f41aa74 -->
- Cost: The application itself is free; you need enough money to live on while job hunting. Ask your school for a recommendation letter before graduating, and apply to immigration for the change.
- In plain words: International students who graduate from university, or earn the "Diploma" (専門士) title at a specialized training college, and have no job yet can switch to "Designated Activities" for job hunting with their school's recommendation. It is 6 months, renewable once, 1 year in total. Taking part in a local government employment support program allows 1 more year. With permission, you can work part-time up to 28 hours a week during this time.
- Benefit: The Immigration Services Agency states that international students who graduated from a university or the advanced course of a specialized training college, or earned the Diploma title or similar in a specialized course at a specialized training college, who have no problems in their residence record and have a recommendation from their school to continue job hunting, can change to a job-hunting status (Designated Activities, period of stay 6 months), renewable once, allowing them to stay in Japan for 1 year after graduating to look for work. Those taking part in an employment support program run by a local government that meets the agency's standards can change again to Designated Activities for 6 months, renewable once, and stay into the 2nd year after graduating. Graduates of Japanese language schools may also, as an exception, change to the job-hunting Designated Activities if they meet all conditions, including holding a bachelor's degree or higher from a university abroad, studying at a Japanese language education institution meeting the requirements, having an attendance rate of roughly 90% or more, and having started job hunting while enrolled. In each case, those meeting certain requirements may get permission for activities outside status and work part-time up to 28 hours a week; internships as part of job hunting may be permitted beyond 28 hours
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国在留管理庁「大学等を卒業後就職活動のための滞在をご希望のみなさまへ」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/resources/nyukan_nyukan84.html>
- Notes: You must apply for the change before your student visa expires; after expiry you are overstaying; see Section 2, entry 6 (apply to renew 3 months before your period of stay expires). Once you find a job, you need to change again to the matching work visa. The benefit is rated large because it keeps your residence and lets you keep looking for work.

### 4. When choosing a Japanese language school, first check that it is a Japanese language education institution certified by the Minister of Education, or a Ministry of Justice listed school still within its validity
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
<!-- zh: 选日语学校，先查它是不是文部科学大臣认定的日本语教育机构，或者仍在有效期内的法务省告示校 | ac79eb9a -->
- Cost: Free. Before paying tuition, look up the school's name on MEXT's "portal for the Act on Certification of Japanese Language Education Institutions", or ask immigration.
- In plain words: Japan now has a certification system for Japanese language schools, with certification by the Minister of Education. Schools previously listed by the Ministry of Justice still count as before until March 31, 2029, and after that need certification. Your student visa depends on the school's standing, so check before paying; it is more reliable than what an agent tells you.
- Benefit: The Immigration Services Agency's explanation on job hunting by Japanese language school graduates states: the requirement for a Japanese language education institution is a study course at an institution certified by the Minister of Education under the "Act on Certification of Japanese Language Education Institutions for Appropriate and Reliable Japanese Language Education" (Act No. 41 of 2023); until March 31, 2029, institutions listed in Appended Table 1 of the Ministry of Justice notice (Ministry of Justice Notice No. 145 of 1990) are treated as meeting this requirement. The same explanation states that immigration looks at a school's enrolment management over the past 3 years, and if the problem enrolment rate (the proportion of students who overstayed, were refused renewal, had their status revoked and so on) exceeds 5%, enrolment management may be considered inadequate
- Evidence: B
- Sources: 出入国在留管理庁「大学等を卒業後就職活動のための滞在をご希望のみなさまへ」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/resources/nyukan_nyukan84.html> ; 文部科学省「日本語教育機関認定法ポータル」. <https://www.nihongokyouiku.mext.go.jp/top/guide-japanese-language-institution>
- Notes: Why grade B: the certification system is official, but there is no figure for how much loss checking first avoids. The benefit is rated medium because choosing the wrong school may affect your residence. The requirement text above comes from the Immigration Services Agency's explanation on job hunting after graduation; for a school's own certification status, go by the MEXT portal.

### 5. If you graduated from a Japanese university and have JLPT N1, you can use "Designated Activities No. 46" for a wide range of work requiring Japanese communication, without forcing it into "Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
<!-- zh: 在日本读完大学、日语有 N1 的，可以用「特定活动 46 号」做需要用日语沟通的广泛工作，不用硬套「技人国」 | 49c098c2 -->
- Cost: The application itself is free. You need proof of graduation from a Japanese university or graduate school, and proof of JLPT N1 or a BJT score of 480 or more.
- In plain words: People who graduated from a Japanese university or completed a Japanese graduate school can apply for "Designated Activities" No. 46. Your Japanese must be JLPT N1, or 480 or more on the BJT Business Japanese test. It allows a wide range of work that requires communicating with people in Japanese. But only simple manual work is not allowed, and you must be a regular employee. If you change companies, you must apply for a change again.
- Benefit: The Immigration Services Agency's guidelines state: "graduates of Japanese universities and the like" means people who graduated from a university or completed a graduate school in Japan, who earned a bachelor's degree after graduating from a Japanese junior college or college of technology, or who completed a certified specialized course at a specialized training college and earned the Advanced Diploma (高度専門士) title; graduates of foreign universities are not covered. The Japanese requirement is JLPT N1 or a score of 480 or more on the BJT Business Japanese Proficiency Test; having majored in Japanese at a university or graduate school also counts. The activity is working as a full-time employee of the organization in work that includes tasks requiring smooth communication in Japanese, and the work must include, or in future involve, work of the "Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services" kind that requires knowledge learned at university. Work only on a production line following instructions in Japanese, or only cleaning and laundry, is not accepted. The designation document names the organization, and moving to another company requires an application for change of status
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国在留管理庁「留学生の就職支援に係る「特定活動」（本邦大学等卒業者）についてのガイドライン」（revised April 2026）. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/content/001413711.pdf>；出入国在留管理庁「留学生の就職」. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/resources/10_00015.html>
- Notes: As of 2026-10-03. For specialized training colleges, only those who completed a "certified specialized course" and earned the Advanced Diploma count; an ordinary Diploma does not. The benefit is rated medium because it adds another path to working in Japan. For the rules on part-time work during study, see Section 2, entry 10 (permission for activities outside your status).
