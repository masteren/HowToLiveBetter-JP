# 3. Emergencies: what to do first

<!-- zh-intro: a045d125 -->

This section covers emergencies such as someone collapsing, choking, earthquakes and tsunamis: what to do first and which number to call. First-aid methods are carried over from the original book's "emergencies" section, with phone numbers and sources of help replaced by Japanese ones. In Japan the number for ambulance and fire is 119 and for police 110, and calling an ambulance is free. Entries are ordered by cost-effectiveness: those costing almost nothing that can save a life come first. In entries carried over from the original book, the emergency number has been changed to 119 throughout. The original book's entries on whether to help an older person who has fallen, accidental ingestion, chemical burns, rehydration for heatstroke, being extorted, witnessing a fight, HIV post-exposure prophylaxis, dog bites and rabies, getting lost in the wilderness, and money matters after being injured while helping someone were based on Chinese laws, guidelines or services and were not carried over. Japan has no rabies, so the dog bite entry does not apply.

### 1. If you feel strong or long-lasting shaking near the coast, do not wait for a warning: run to high ground at once, and do not go back until the warning is lifted
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 在海边感到强烈或长时间的摇晃，不等警报，马上往高处跑，警报解除前别回去 | cd8dd171 -->
- Cost: Free. If you live near the sea or a river, check in advance where the nearby high ground and tsunami evacuation buildings are.
- In plain words: If you feel strong shaking near the sea or a river, or shaking that is not strong but lasts a long time, do not wait for a tsunami warning: leave the coast at once and run to high ground. When the epicentre is close to land, the warning may come too late. Tsunamis come in wave after wave, so do not go back until the warning is lifted. Even with just a tsunami advisory, get out of the sea at once.
- Benefit: The Japan Meteorological Agency states: if you see or hear a tsunami warning or advisory, or feel strong shaking near the coast, or feel long, slow shaking, leave the coast and evacuate to higher, safer ground. When the epicentre is close to land, tsunami warnings and advisories may not arrive before the tsunami, so if you feel strong shaking, or weak but long shaking, start evacuating immediately. People on the coast and along rivers should go at once to high ground or evacuation buildings. Tsunamis strike repeatedly, so after a tsunami arrives, do not relax until warnings and advisories are lifted, and keep evacuating; never go back while a warning is in effect. Where a tsunami advisory is issued, swimming and fishing from rocks are dangerous; get out of the water and away from the shore at once
- Evidence: B
- Sources: 気象庁「津波から身を守るために」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.jma.go.jp/jma/kishou/know/jishin/tsunami_bosai/index.html>
- Notes: Why grade B: this is official evacuation guidance, and no research gives a figure for how many fewer die by following it. The benefit is rated large given the scale of tsunami deaths. Tsunamis travel up rivers, so riverbanks are as dangerous as the coast. When evacuating, go up, not far away by car; roads easily become jammed.

### 2. If someone's mouth suddenly droops, one arm goes weak or their speech becomes slurred, call 119 at once; do not wait and do not drive them yourself
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 突然嘴歪、一侧胳膊没劲、说话说不清，立刻打 119，不要等、不要自己开车去 | 68fa209e -->
- Cost: Free. Three checks you can learn at a glance: ask them to smile, to raise both arms straight out, and to say a whole sentence.
- In plain words: A drooping mouth, one arm that will not lift, or slurred speech: call 119 at once. With clot-busting drugs to reopen the blood vessel within 3 hours of onset, the chance of recovering to largely independent living is about 1.75 times that without. At 3 to 4.5 hours it is only 1.26 times. Beyond 4.5 hours no difference can be seen. Note the exact time symptoms began.
- Benefit: Individual data from 16 randomized trials of intravenous thrombolysis with 6,756 patients were pooled. Intravenous thrombolysis means giving drugs to open the blocked vessel. Thrombolysis with alteplase within 3 hours of onset gave an odds ratio of 1.75 for recovering to largely independent living (modified Rankin score 0–1), 95% CI 1.35–2.27 (confidence range). This outcome was about 1.75 times as likely as without thrombolysis. At 3 to 4.5 hours, 1.26 (1.05–1.51). Beyond 4.5 hours, 1.15 (0.95–1.40), no longer statistically different
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Emberson J, Lees KR, Lyden P, et al. (2014). Effect of treatment delay, age, and stroke severity on the effects of intravenous thrombolysis with alteplase for acute ischaemic stroke: a meta-analysis of individual patient data from randomised trials. Lancet, 384(9958), 1929-1935. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(14)60584-5>
- Notes: The earlier thrombolysis is given, the better it works, and beyond 4.5 hours it is basically useless. Call 119 rather than driving yourself because the ambulance takes the patient to a hospital that can treat stroke. Note the exact time symptoms began; the doctor uses it to decide whether thrombolysis is possible. Do not give water or medicine, and do not "let them rest and see". When calling 119, say 「脳卒中かもしれません」 (it may be a stroke).

### 3. If chest pressure, pain or tightness lasts more than 15 minutes without easing, call 119; do not tough it out and do not drive yourself
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 胸口压着疼、闷、发紧超过 15 分钟不缓解，打 119，不要自己扛也不要自己开车 | d81a4137 -->
- Cost: Free.
- In plain words: Chest pressure, pain or tightness lasting more than 15 minutes without easing: call 119. Of those whose blocked vessel was opened within 55 minutes of reaching hospital, 2.8% died within 30 days. Over 55 minutes, 9.7%. Every bit of delay kills another piece of heart muscle. Do not drive yourself; losing consciousness on the way is a second accident.
- Benefit: Israel ran a national survey of acute coronary syndromes. The patients analysed had ST-elevation myocardial infarction, a type of heart attack. What was measured was the interval from arriving at hospital to opening the blocked vessel with a balloon (balloon angioplasty). With an interval ≤55 minutes, 5/178 (2.8%) died within 30 days. With an interval >55 minutes, 15/155 (9.7%) died within 30 days
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Karkabi B, Meir G, Zafrir B, et al. (2021). Door-to-balloon time and mortality in patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarction. European Heart Journal - Quality of Care and Clinical Outcomes. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ehjqcco/qcaa037>
- Notes: This study compared delay after arriving at hospital, not from onset to arriving. But the principle is the same: every moment of delay kills another piece of heart muscle. The pain may be in the upper abdomen, back or jaw, or felt as numbness in the left arm. People with diabetes and older people may feel no pain, only breathlessness.

### 4. If someone is choking and cannot speak, stand behind them and give 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts; if they collapse, start CPR
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 有人噎住说不出话，站到背后做 5 次拍背加 5 次腹部冲击，倒下就做心肺复苏 | 580625a5 -->
- Cost: Free. Learn the movement once: one fist above the navel, the other hand over it, thrust quickly inwards and upwards.
- In plain words: First ask "are you choking, can you speak?". If they can cough forcefully and make sounds, do not intervene; let them cough. If they cannot speak, stand behind them and give 5 back blows followed by 5 abdominal thrusts, repeating until the object comes out or they collapse. If they collapse, start CPR and have someone call 119. For pregnant women and very large people, use chest thrusts instead.
- Benefit: The US National Library of Medicine says: first ask "are you choking? Can you speak?". "Do not intervene if the person can cough forcefully and speak"; coughing itself often brings the object out. If they cannot speak, "give 5 back blows followed by 5 abdominal thrusts, repeating until the object comes out or the person loses consciousness". "If the person loses consciousness, start CPR", and have someone call emergency services. For pregnant women and obese people, use chest thrusts instead
- Evidence: B
- Sources: US National Library of Medicine, MedlinePlus. Choking – adult or child over 1 year. <https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000047.htm>
- Notes: Do not reach into the mouth for something you cannot see; it pushes it deeper. Do not hit someone who can still cough. Older people in Japan choke on mochi at New Year particularly often; for prevention, see Section 1, entry 22 (cut mochi into small pieces).

### 5. Sudden vertigo with inability to stand, double vision, blackness in one eye, or being unable to touch your own nose with your finger: also treat it as a stroke and call 119
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 突然天旋地转站不稳、看东西成双、一只眼发黑，或者手指点不到自己鼻尖，也按卒中打 119 | deb5b28a -->
- Cost: Free. On top of the three checks "face droop, arm raise, speech" (entry 2 of this section, mouth suddenly droops or speech becomes slurred), add two more. One is to have them stand straight with eyes closed and see if they sway, and ask whether they see double or have part of their vision missing. The other, if you suspect it yourself, is to touch your nose with your finger, then again with eyes closed.
- In plain words: Checking only "face droop, arm raise, speech" misses over 10% of strokes, because some strokes at first neither droop the mouth nor paralyse the arm. Adding inability to stand, double vision or missing vision cuts the missed proportion to 4.4%. Sudden vertigo with inability to stand, double vision, or a finger that cannot find your nose: likewise call 119, and likewise note the time.
- Benefit: The University of Kentucky stroke centre reviewed the records of 736 cases of acute ischaemic stroke (stroke from a blocked vessel) in 2014. 14.1% of patients had none of the three symptoms of face droop, arm weakness or slurred speech (FAST) at onset. Adding "unsteady walking, leg weakness and visual symptoms" to screening cut the missed proportion from 14.1% to 4.4%
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Aroor S, Singh R, Goldstein LB (2017). BE-FAST (Balance, Eyes, Face, Arm, Speech, Time): Reducing the Proportion of Strokes Missed Using the FAST Mnemonic. Stroke, 48(2), 479-481. <https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.116.015169>
- Notes: When vessels in the cerebellum and brainstem area are blocked (posterior circulation infarction), the mouth often does not droop and the arms are not paralysed. It shows only as vertigo, walking off to one side, diplopia (double vision) and choking on drinks and food, and is most easily mistaken for neck problems, low blood sugar or inner-ear crystals, causing delay. Missing the nose and being unable to stand with eyes closed are signs of cerebellar involvement. But no single check can rule out a stroke: if it comes on suddenly and does not ease, call 119 and note the exact time of onset. This study only reviewed records from one hospital, and the authors themselves state it needs further validation

### 6. Sudden severe pain like being torn apart, moving from the chest to the back and down to the lower back: call 119 and say clearly "the pain is moving"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 突然像被撕开一样的剧痛，从胸口往后背、往腰一路挪，打 119 并说清「疼在移动」 | 54b9788e -->
- Cost: Free.
- In plain words: Tearing severe pain moving from the chest to the back and down to the lower back: when you call 119, say directly "it feels like being torn apart, and the pain is moving down". Without surgery, on medication alone, this condition has a 58% death rate. Its treatment is the opposite of a heart attack: only when doctors think of it do they order a contrast CT, instead of giving you anticoagulants or clot-busters. If the blood pressure in the two arms differs a lot, or the pulse cannot be felt on one side, say that too.
- Benefit: The International Registry of Acute Aortic Dissection (IRAD) collected 464 cases from 12 centres. Aortic dissection is the condition with tearing severe pain in the chest and back. The most common symptom patients first described was sudden-onset severe sharp pain. In-hospital mortality overall was 27.4%. For type A dissection treated with medication only, without surgery, mortality was 58%
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Hagan PG, Nienaber CA, Isselbacher EM, et al. (2000). The International Registry of Acute Aortic Dissection (IRAD): new insights into an old disease. JAMA, 283(7), 897-903. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.283.7.897>
- Notes: Dissection and heart attack are treated in opposite ways. Heart attacks need drugs such as anticoagulants and clot-busters that stop blood clotting, which in a dissection would bleed out the tear. So at hospital, say up front "tearing, and the pain is moving down", so the doctor thinks of a contrast CT. A large difference in blood pressure between the arms, or no pulse on one side, is a clue. People with poorly controlled blood pressure, Marfan syndrome, or a family history of aortic aneurysm or dissection are at high risk. Again, do not drive yourself, and do not wait until the next day

### 7. After a burn, run cool water over it at once for 20 minutes, and do not apply toothpaste or soy sauce
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 烫伤后立刻用凉的流动水冲 20 分钟，别抹牙膏酱油 | a2a3aecc -->
- Cost: Free. Takes 20 minutes.
- In plain words: Running cool water over a burn for a full 20 minutes within 3 hours of injury makes later skin grafting only about 60% as likely as without. It is cool water, not ice water; ice makes the damage worse. Do not tear off clothing stuck to the wound; after cooling, cover it with a clean cloth and go to hospital. Do not apply toothpaste, soy sauce, sesame oil or flour. If you only remember after a while, cooling within three hours still helps.
- Benefit: Australia followed 2,495 children with burns. Children who had cool running water for 20 minutes within 3 hours of injury had an odds ratio of 0.6 for later needing a skin graft, 95% CI 0.4–0.8 (confidence range), about 60% of those not cooled this way. Pooling 7 studies likewise found that "20 minutes of cool running water within three hours of injury significantly reduced the need for skin grafting and surgical wound management"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Griffin BR, Frear CC, Babl F, Oakley E, Kimble RM (2020). Cool Running Water First Aid Decreases Skin Grafting Requirements in Pediatric Burns: A Cohort Study of Two Thousand Four Hundred Ninety-five Children. Annals of Emergency Medicine, 75(1), 75-85. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2019.06.028>；Griffin B, Cabilan CJ, Ayoub B, et al. (2022). The effect of 20 minutes of cool running water first aid within three hours of thermal burn injury on patient outcomes: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Australasian Emergency Care, 25(4), 367-376. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.auec.2022.05.004>
- Notes: It is cool water, not ice water; ice makes the damage worse. Do not tear off clothing stuck to the wound; after cooling, cover with a clean cloth and go to hospital. Do not apply toothpaste, soy sauce, sesame oil or flour; they interfere with the doctor's judgment of depth and invite infection. Cooling within three hours still helps

### 8. Sudden rash all over the body with difficulty breathing or faintness: treat it as anaphylactic shock and call 119 at once, saying so clearly
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 突然全身起疹、喘不上气或者发晕，按过敏性休克处理，立刻叫 119 并说清楚 | 54467bab -->
- Cost: Free. People with known severe allergies who carry their own adrenaline auto-injector pay for it, and it is a prescription medicine.
- In plain words: A sudden rash all over the body, together with difficulty breathing or faintness, should be treated as anaphylactic shock. Call 119 at once and say directly "suspected anaphylactic shock", so the ambulance brings adrenaline. Antihistamines and steroids act slowly and cannot replace adrenaline. Anyone who has had one episode should get an auto-injector prescribed, injected into the outer middle thigh, even through clothing.
- Benefit: Adrenaline (epinephrine) is the first-choice drug (first-line) for grade II and above anaphylaxis, listed in guidelines as a strong recommendation. Antihistamines and steroids act slowly and cannot take its place
- Evidence: B (strongly recommended by guidelines, but the underlying evidence is low-grade)
- Sources: China Medicine Education Association et al. (2022). Clinical practice guideline for emergency management of anaphylaxis (2020). Frontiers in Pharmacology: "Epinephrine is the first-line medicine in GRADE II to IV anaphylaxis" "Intramuscular epinephrine should be injected in the mid-anterolateral thigh", maximum intramuscular dose 0.5 mg for adults and 0.3 mg under 14, repeatable every 5–15 minutes if no response. <https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2022.845689>；Muraro A, et al. (2022). EAACI guidelines: Anaphylaxis (2021 update). Allergy. <https://doi.org/10.1111/all.15032>
- Notes: This allergy guideline (EAACI) itself states that the evidence in this area "mostly remains at a very low level". It is a strong recommendation because the benefits far outweigh the risks, not because of large trials. Adrenaline is a prescription medicine, and ordinary people usually do not have it. What you can do is call 119 and say "suspected anaphylactic shock" so the ambulance brings it. Anyone who has had a severe allergic reaction should get an auto-injector prescribed and learn to inject it into the outer middle thigh (outer, slightly forward), even through clothing.

### 9. If a person with diabetes suddenly shakes, breaks into a cold sweat or slurs their speech, first give 15 grams of sugar and recheck after 15 minutes
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 糖尿病人突然发抖、出冷汗、说话不清，先给 15 克糖，15 分钟后复测 | f0ccd373 -->
- Cost: Free. Carry a few sugar cubes or a small carton of juice.
- In plain words: First have 15 grams of sugar; sugar cubes or a small carton of juice will do. Recheck after 15 minutes, and if still low, repeat. Blood sugar below 3.0 mmol/L is medically significant hypoglycaemia. At the point where someone else's help is needed to deal with it, it can be fatal. Do not give a single mouthful to someone who is not fully conscious; it can go into the windpipe. Call 119 directly.
- Benefit: Blood sugar below 3.0 mmol/L is clinically significant hypoglycaemia. When consciousness or behaviour changes and someone else's help is needed, it is severe hypoglycaemia, which can be fatal
- Evidence: A
- Sources: American Diabetes Association (2025). Standards of Care in Diabetes—2025, Chapter 6: Glycemic Goals and Hypoglycemia. Diabetes Care: classification "Level 1: Glucose <70 mg/dL (<3.9 mmol/L) and ≥54 mg/dL (≥3.0 mmol/L)" "Level 2: Glucose <54 mg/dL (<3.0 mmol/L)" "Level 3: A severe event characterized by altered mental and/or physical status requiring assistance for treatment of hypoglycemia, irrespective of glucose level"; treatment "For most individuals, 15 g carbohydrates should be ingested" "Fifteen minutes after initial treatment, repeat the treatment if hypoglycemia persists". <https://doi.org/10.2337/dc25-S006>
- Notes: Do not feed someone who is not fully conscious; it can go into the windpipe. Call 119 directly. The same standards also recommend that people on insulin and people prone to hypoglycaemia keep glucagon. The original reads "All individuals treated with insulin or who are at high risk of hypoglycemia...should be prescribed glucagon". Nasal sprays and pre-filled injections are easier to use than those needing mixing on the spot. These measures are by default for you or a family member with diabetes, and carrying sugar is for them too.

### 10. If someone is electrocuted, cut the power first, then push the source away with something dry like a wooden stick; do not grab them with your hands
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 有人触电，先断电，再用干燥的木棍之类挑开电源，不要直接上手拉 | 1834f0ce -->
- Cost: Free.
- In plain words: Cut the power first, or push the source away with something dry like a wooden stick, before touching the person. Grabbing them with your hands turns one casualty into two. If they are not breathing once free of the source, start chest compressions at once following entry 15 of this section (press hard on the chest); do not be afraid to touch them because it is an electrical injury. Do not deal with fallen high-voltage lines or substation equipment yourself; move well away and wait for the power company to cut the power.
- Benefit: It prevents the rescuer being electrocuted too, turning one casualty into two. If they are not breathing once free of the source, do CPR following entry 15 of this section (press hard on the chest). The US CDC's first-aid steps: look first, do not touch, the person may still be in contact with the current. Cut the power if you can; if not, push the source away with a non-conducting object such as cardboard, plastic or wood. Once free of the source, if breathing or pulse has stopped, start CPR at once. A review says people resuscitated from cardiac arrest after electrocution often have a good outlook
- Evidence: B
- Sources: US CDC. What to Do to Protect Yourself From Electrical Hazards (First aid section). <https://www.cdc.gov/natural-disasters/response/what-to-do-protect-yourself-from-electrical-hazards.html>；Spies C, Trohman RG (2006). Narrative review: Electrocution and life-threatening electrical injuries. Annals of Internal Medicine 145(7):531-537. <https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-145-7-200610030-00011>
- Notes: "Cut the power before touching the person" is a purely self-interested rule: grab them directly and the second one down is you. Do not deal with fallen high-voltage lines or substation equipment yourself; move well away and wait for the power company to cut the power. Cardiac arrest from electrocution is handled with normal compressions and normal defibrillation; do not be afraid to act because it is an "electrical injury".

### 11. If the carbon monoxide alarm goes off, or everyone in a room gets a headache and nausea at the same time, get outside first, then call
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 一氧化碳报警器响了，或者一屋子人同时头痛恶心，先出门再打电话 | 5419bce6 -->
- Cost: Free.
- In plain words: If the alarm goes off, or everyone in a room gets a headache, nausea or dizziness at the same time, first get everyone outside, then call. Do not stay inside looking for the cause, and do not go back to turn off a valve. The symptoms look like a cold, or like having drunk too much. "Feeling better once outside" is the strongest clue. In the US, over 400 people die each year from carbon monoxide poisoning unrelated to fires, and over 100,000 visit emergency departments.
- Benefit: More than 400 Americans die each year from unintentional carbon monoxide poisoning not linked to fires. More than 100,000 visit emergency departments, and more than 14,000 are hospitalized
- Evidence: B
- Sources: US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Carbon monoxide poisoning: "More than 400 Americans die from unintentional CO poisoning not linked to fires" "More than 100,000 visit an emergency department" "More than 14,000 are hospitalized", common symptoms "headache, dizziness, weakness, upset stomach, vomiting, chest pain, and confusion". <https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/about/index.html>；US Consumer Product Safety Commission. Carbon Monoxide Information Center: "If you suspect CO poisoning, get outside to fresh air immediately, and then call 911". <https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Centers/Carbon-Monoxide-Information-Center>
- Notes: The symptoms look like a cold, or like having drunk too much. Everyone in the room feeling ill together and feeling better once outside is the strongest clue. Do not stay inside looking for the cause or go back to turn off a valve; get people out first, then call.

### 12. If someone in the heat is dizzy, nauseous, not sweating or confused, move them to shade at once, remove clothing and douse them with water to cool down; do not give water to someone who is not fully conscious, and call 119
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 高温下头晕、恶心、不出汗或意识不清，立刻挪到阴凉处脱衣泼水降温，意识不清的不喂水、打 119 | e2883664 -->
- Cost: Free.
- In plain words: Hot, red skin, slurred speech and confusion mean heatstroke. From dizziness to coma can take only a few dozen minutes, and the death rate is extremely high. Move them at once to a shaded, ventilated place, remove clothing, cool them with cold water and wet towels, and call 119 at the same time. Do not give a single mouthful of water to someone not fully conscious; they will choke.
- Benefit: The US National Weather Service: heatstroke is "a serious medical emergency". It shows as a body temperature above 103°F (39.4°C), confusion, slurred speech and hot, red skin. Treatment is to call emergency services at once, move them somewhere cool, cool them with cold water and wet cloths, and "do not give fluids". Heat exhaustion shows as heavy sweating, weakness, cold clammy skin and a fast, weak pulse; treatment is to move somewhere cool, loosen clothing and sip water; seek medical care if vomiting, worsening, or not easing within 1 hour. The Wilderness Medical Society's 2024 updated heat illness guideline says cold-water immersion is the most important treatment for severe heat illness and cools fastest. Without cold-water immersion, remove or loosen clothing, wet all the skin and fan, generally about half as fast as immersion
- Evidence: B
- Sources: US National Weather Service. Heat Related Illnesses. <https://www.weather.gov/safety/heat-illness> ; Eifling KP, et al. (2024). Wilderness Medical Society Clinical Practice Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Heat Illness: 2024 Update. Wilderness & Environmental Medicine 35(1_suppl):112S-127S. <https://doi.org/10.1177/10806032241227924> (the full-text site refuses automated retrieval; specific recommendations were checked against the update summary on the Wilderness Medical Society website: "cold water immersion … is the most important method of treating severe heat illness" "If CWI is unavailable, evaporative cooling is the recommended second-line treatment. … generally half as fast as CWI" <https://wms.org/magazine/magazine/1540/Heat-Illness-CPG/default.aspx>); Lipman GS, et al. (2019). The 2019 edition of the same guideline. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wem.2018.10.004>
- Notes: Do not wait to "let it pass"; heatstroke can go from dizziness to coma in a few dozen minutes. Do not pour water into someone not fully conscious; they will choke. When you only have ice packs, the Wilderness Medical Society says studies show no cooling effect from applying them only to the neck, armpits and groin. It recommends covering the whole body if you have enough ice packs, and otherwise placing them on the cheeks, palms and soles

### 13. If someone swallows cleaning products, pesticides or medicine, do not induce vomiting; bring the bottle and seek care at once. If splashed in the eyes or on skin, rinse with plenty of water for at least 15 minutes
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 误服清洁剂、农药、药物先别催吐，带上瓶子立刻就医；溅到眼睛或皮肤用大量清水至少冲 15 分钟 | 16a06135 -->
- Cost: Free. Keep cleaning products, disinfectants, pesticides and medicines high up out of children's reach, or in a locked cupboard. Never decant them into drink bottles.
- In plain words: If cleaning products, pesticides or medicine are swallowed, do not induce vomiting; bring the bottle and instructions and go to hospital at once. If splashed on skin or in the eyes, rinse with plenty of water for at least 15 minutes, holding the eyelids open, without rubbing. Mixing bleach-type disinfectant with toilet cleaner produces chlorine gas.

### 35. If you suspect a fracture, stop moving that part, support it still, apply cold for no more than 20 minutes, and do not try to straighten it yourself

- Cost: Free. Find a hard board, a rolled-up magazine or clothing for support. Apply cold things through a layer of cloth.
- In plain words: Treat every limb injury as a fracture first; being able to stand or move does not rule it out, and only an X-ray can confirm. - Notes: Graded B because these are all accepted practice. Traction splinting of femur (thigh bone) fractures before hospital is supported only by one study reviewing old records, with very low certainty.
