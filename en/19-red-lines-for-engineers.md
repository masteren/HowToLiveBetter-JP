# 19. Legal red lines for programmers and engineers: unauthorized access, malicious programs, trade secrets and code ownership

<!-- zh-intro: 168cb32f -->

This section covers the criminal and civil red lines foreigners working in development and operations in Japan most easily cross. The statutes have been checked against the 2026 versions on e-Gov. For foreigners, a criminal sentence can also affect residence; see Section 2, entry 5 (a sentence can get you deported). The original book's section on "red lines programmers and engineers easily cross" covers Chinese law and cannot be applied as is in Japan.

### 1. Do not log in with someone else's ID and password, do not access systems beyond your authorization, and do not stockpile other people's credentials
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
<!-- zh: 不用别人的账号密码登录，不越过授权去访问系统，也不囤别人的账号密码 | f4155b13 -->
- Cost: Free. When testing or troubleshooting, use only the accounts and scope you are authorized for, and get written permission before going beyond them.
- In plain words: Japan's Unauthorized Computer Access Act makes it a crime to log in with someone else's ID and password, or to get into a system by bypassing access controls. The maximum is 3 years and a 1 million yen fine. Obtaining or keeping other people's IDs and passwords for this purpose is also punishable.
- Benefit: The Unauthorized Computer Access Act provides that no one may commit unauthorized access, and violations are punishable by imprisonment of up to 3 years or a fine of up to 1 million yen. Obtaining another person's identification code (ID, password and the like) for use in unauthorized access, or keeping another person's identification code obtained wrongfully, is punishable by imprisonment of up to 1 year or a fine of up to 500,000 yen; providing another person's identification code knowing it will be used for unauthorized access is punished the same way
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 不正アクセス行為の禁止等に関する法律 第三条、第四条、第五条、第六条、第十一条、第十二条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/411AC0000000128>
- Notes: "I only looked and didn't change anything" is no defence; the law punishes the access itself. Accounts left behind by former colleagues, and permissions not revoked after you left a job, should not be used again.

### 2. Do not write, distribute or run on others' computers programs that act against the user's intent; the Supreme Court acquitted in the Coinhive case, but its test is very specific
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
<!-- zh: 不写、不传、不在别人电脑上运行违背使用者意图的程序；Coinhive 案最高法院判无罪，但判断标准很具体 | ff57b14d -->
- Cost: Free. Before adding mining, tracking, remote control or similar features to a website or app, think about whether users know and whether society would accept it. Ask a lawyer when needed.
- In plain words: Japan's Penal Code has an offence commonly called the "virus crime". Making or providing, without a legitimate reason, a program that makes another person's computer act wrongly against their intent carries up to 3 years. Obtaining or keeping one carries up to 2 years. In 2022 the Supreme Court acquitted in the case of the Coinhive mining script, because that code's effect was within what society tolerates. That does not mean all such code is fine.
- Benefit: The Penal Code provides that making or providing, without a legitimate reason and for the purpose of running it on another person's computer, an unauthorized command electromagnetic record that makes the computer not act according to the user's intent, or act against it, is punishable by imprisonment of up to 3 years or a fine of up to 500,000 yen; running it on another person's computer without a legitimate reason is punished the same way, and attempts are also punished. Obtaining or keeping one for the same purpose without a legitimate reason is punishable by imprisonment of up to 2 years or a fine of up to 300,000 yen. The Supreme Court First Petty Bench judgment of January 20, 2022 (Reiwa 2 (A) No. 457) quashed the appeal court's judgment imposing a 100,000 yen fine, dismissed the prosecution's appeal and upheld the first-instance acquittal. The judgment distinguished the two requirements of "contrary to intent" and "wrongfulness", and held that the program code, given the degree of its effect on the computer's functions and so on, could not be said to have been unacceptable to society at the time, so it lacked wrongfulness
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 刑法 第百六十八条の二、第百六十八条の三. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/140AC0000000045> ; 最高裁判所第一小法廷 (2022). 令和２年（あ）第４５７号 不正指令電磁的記録保管被告事件 判決（令和４年１月２０日）. <https://www.courts.go.jp/assets/hanrei/hanrei-pdf-90869.pdf>
- Notes: The appeal number in this case dates from 2020, and the Supreme Court only ruled in 2022. Even with an eventual acquittal, the process itself was a heavy cost. Features such as secretly collecting user data, remotely controlling users' devices or ransomware go against users' intent far more obviously than a mining script.

### 3. When you leave a job, do not take source code, design documents or customer data; taking and using them may be a trade secret infringement offence, with up to 10 years
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
<!-- zh: 离职时不带走源码、设计文档和客户资料，带走用了，可能是营业秘密侵害罪，最重判 10 年 | a7c1caa2 -->
- Cost: Free. Before leaving, delete the company's code, documents and customer lists from your personal computer and cloud storage, and do not forward them to your personal email.
- In plain words: Technical and customer information that the company manages as secret, that is useful to the business and not known outside, is a trade secret. It is a crime for an employee, to make a wrongful gain or harm the company, to copy and take trade secrets in breach of their duty of management, or after leaving to use what they agreed while employed to use. The maximum is 10 years and a 20 million yen fine, which can be combined.
- Benefit: The Unfair Competition Prevention Act provides that a trade secret is technical or business information managed as a secret, useful for business activities and not publicly known. A person to whom the holder disclosed a trade secret who, for the purpose of wrongful gain or harming the holder, in breach of their duty of management, takes it by misappropriating a recording medium, copying records, or failing to delete records that should be deleted while pretending to have deleted them, is punishable by imprisonment of up to 10 years or a fine of up to 20 million yen, or both. The same applies to current officers or employees who use or disclose it for the same purposes in breach of duty, and to former officers or employees who, while employed, agreed in breach of duty to disclose it or accepted a request to use or disclose it, and use or disclose it after leaving
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 不正競争防止法 第二条第六項、第二十一条第二項. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/405AC0000000047>
- Notes: "It's my own code, so I can take it" does not necessarily hold in law; copyright in code often belongs to the company; see entry 5 (copyright in programs written at work belongs to the company in principle). If you move to a competitor, the new company may also be held liable for using such information.

### 4. Do not copy out customer lists or member data you handle at work to sell or use yourself; the offence of illegally providing a personal information database carries up to 1 year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
<!-- zh: 别把工作中接触的客户名单、会员数据拷出来卖或者自己拿去用，个人信息数据库非法提供罪最重判 1 年 | 2c9a2337 -->
- Cost: Free. Use personal information you handle at work only within the scope of the work, and do not take it when you leave.
- In plain words: Company employees and officers, and people who have left, may not provide or misuse a personal information database they handled at work for their own or someone else's wrongful gain. Violations can bring imprisonment of up to 1 year or a fine of up to 500,000 yen. Copying part of it or reformatting it also counts. For foreigners, a prison sentence also affects residence.
- Benefit: Article 179 of the Act on the Protection of Personal Information provides: a business handling personal information (for a corporation, its officers, representatives or managers) or its employees, or people who were formerly such, who, for the wrongful gain of themselves or a third party, provide or misappropriate a personal information database or the like handled in their business (including copies or processed versions of all or part of it), are punishable by imprisonment of up to 1 year or a fine of up to 500,000 yen
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 個人情報の保護に関する法律 第 179 条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/415AC0000000057>
- Notes: As of 2026-10-03. Taking source code and customer data when leaving may at the same time be trade secret infringement; see entry 3 of this section (when you leave a job, do not take source code, design documents or customer data). For what a prison sentence means for your residence, see Section 2, entry 5 (a sentence can get you deported). The benefit is rated large because it avoids criminal liability.

### 5. Copyright in programs written at work belongs to the company in principle, so after leaving do not open-source them or sell them as your own work
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 在公司写的程序，著作权原则上归公司，离职后别拿去开源或者当自己的作品卖 | cc597459 -->
- Cost: Free. When joining, read the copyright terms in the employment contract and work rules; if you want to keep personal projects, agree on it in writing beforehand.
- In plain words: Japan's Copyright Act says that for a program an employee writes in the course of duties on the company's initiative, the author is the company unless the contract or work rules say otherwise. Unlike articles, a program belongs to the company even if not published under the company's name. So open-sourcing or selling code you wrote at the company after leaving may infringe the company's copyright.
- Benefit: The Copyright Act provides that for a program work made on the initiative of a corporation or similar by a person engaged in its business in the course of their duties, the author is the corporation unless the contract, work rules or the like at the time of creation provide otherwise. Works made in the course of duties other than programs belong to the corporation only if also made public under its own name
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 著作権法 第十五条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/345AC0000000048>
- Notes: The benefit is rated medium because it avoids claims from your former company. Whether a project written in your own time, on your own device and unrelated to work counts as a work made in the course of duties depends on the circumstances. If in doubt, confirm with the company in writing beforehand.

### 6. When taking freelance work, have the client state the work, fee and payment date in writing; the payment date must fall within 60 days of delivery, and if unpaid, call the Freelance Trouble 110 line
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 接自由职业的单子，要发包方书面写清内容、报酬和付款日；交货后 60 天内必须定付款日，拖欠打フリーランス・トラブル110番 | f1951a13 -->
- Cost: Free. When accepting work, have the client state by email or in writing what to do, how much and when it is paid. If a dispute arises, call 0120-532-110 for free consultation with a lawyer, and you can also apply for free settlement mediation.
- In plain words: Freelancers who work alone without employees are protected by the Freelance Act (フリーランス新法). The client must promptly state the work, fee and payment date in writing or by email. Clients with employees must set the payment date within 60 days of receiving the work. If you are not paid, call the Freelance Trouble 110 line (フリーランス・トラブル110番) for free consultation and mediation by lawyers.
- Benefit: The Freelance Act (Act on Ensuring Proper Transactions Involving Specified Entrusted Business Operators, in force from November 1, 2024). Article 2 of that Act provides that a "specified entrusted business operator" is an individual who does not employ workers, or a corporation with one representative and no other officers or employees. Article 3 of that Act provides that a business entrusting work to a specified entrusted business operator must promptly state, in writing or electronically, the content of the work, the fee, the payment date and other matters. Article 2, paragraph 6 of that Act provides that a "specified business entrusting operator" is an individual who employs workers, or a corporation with two or more officers or that employs workers. Article 4 of that Act provides that a specified business entrusting operator must set the payment date within 60 days of receiving the work, and as short as possible; if no date is set, the date the work is received is the payment date. The Freelance Trouble 110 line is a service commissioned by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare and run by the Daini Tokyo Bar Association, at 0120-532-110, weekdays 9:30 to 16:30; consultation is free and can be anonymous; settlement mediation is conducted by lawyers with 10 or more years of experience, also free, and is simpler to apply for, faster and non-public compared with a lawsuit
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 特定受託事業者に係る取引の適正化等に関する法律 第 2 条、第 3 条、第 4 条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/505AC0000000025>；フリーランス・トラブル110番（厚生労働省委託事業・第二東京弁護士会運営）. <https://freelance110.mhlw.go.jp/>；民事訴訟法 第 368 条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/408AC0000000109>
- Notes: As of 2026-10-03. If settlement mediation fails, recovering the fee still means going to court. Claims of 600,000 yen or less can use small claims proceedings at the summary court (Code of Civil Procedure, Article 368), and as a rule each side pays its own lawyer. The 60-day rule only binds clients with employees or with two or more officers. Whether your status of residence allows you to take side work, check Section 2, entry 10 (permission for activities outside your status) first. The benefit is rated medium because typical unpaid fees are in the tens of thousands of yen.
