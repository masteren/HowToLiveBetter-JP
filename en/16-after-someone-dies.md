# 16. After someone dies: death notice, residence card, funeral costs and inheritance

<!-- zh-intro: 5e41c445 -->

This section covers what to do, and by when, after a family member or someone you live with dies in Japan, what money you can claim, and which country's law governs the inheritance. The provisions of the Family Register Act, the Graveyards and Burials Act, the Immigration Act, the Health Insurance Act and the Act on General Rules for Application of Laws have been checked on e-Gov. If the body is to be sent back to the country of origin, follow the guidance of that country's embassy or consulate and the funeral company; this is not covered here.

### 1. An estate is inherited under the deceased's national law, including savings and property in Japan; find out the national law first, then go to the bank and the Legal Affairs Bureau
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 遗产按死者的本国法继承，日本的存款和房子也一样，先问清本国法再去银行和法务局 | 77fab386 -->
- Cost: Finding out the rules costs nothing; the actual procedures often require documents proving family relationships from the home country and translations, and complex cases need a lawyer or judicial scrivener.
- In plain words: Japanese law says inheritance follows the deceased's national law; whether a will was validly made and is effective follows the testator's national law at the time it was made. So for savings and property a foreigner leaves in Japan, who inherits and how much each gets depends first on the law of the deceased's country. Japanese banks and the Legal Affairs Bureau then process things according to that result.
- Benefit: The Act on General Rules for Application of Laws provides that inheritance is governed by the national law of the deceased. The formation and effect of a will are governed by the testator's national law at the time the will was made; revocation of a will is governed by the testator's national law at the time of revocation
- Evidence: B
- Sources: 法の適用に関する通則法 第三十六条、第三十七条、第四十一条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/418AC0000000078>
- Notes: Why grade B: the choice-of-law rule is clear, but inheritance law differs by country, so how the estate is divided cannot be written here. The benefit is rated large because savings and property are often several million yen or more. If the deceased's national law says Japanese law should govern the inheritance, Japanese law applies; the Act has a separate provision for this, and complex cases need a professional.

### 2. If the deceased had debts and the inheritance is governed by Japanese law, apply to the family court to renounce the inheritance within 3 months of learning it began, and do not touch the estate meanwhile
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 死者欠了债、继承又适用日本法的，知道继承开始后 3 个月内向家庭裁判所申请放弃继承，期间别动遗产 | 90fcb19a -->
- Cost: 800 yen in revenue stamps per applicant, plus postage stamps for communication. Apply to the family court for the deceased's last address.
- In plain words: When Japanese law governs the inheritance, heirs must decide within 3 months of learning it began whether to accept everything, accept with limited liability, or renounce. If you do nothing, after 3 months you are treated as accepting everything, debts included. Using or disposing of the estate is also treated as accepting everything. So if there are large debts, leave the estate alone and apply to the family court to renounce within the deadline.
- Benefit: Article 915 of the Civil Code provides that an heir must make a simple acceptance, qualified acceptance or renunciation within 3 months of learning that an inheritance has begun for them; the period may be extended by the family court at the request of an interested party or a public prosecutor. Article 921 of the Civil Code provides that an heir who disposes of all or part of the inherited property, or who does not make a qualified acceptance or renunciation within the 3 months, is deemed to have made a simple acceptance. The courts state that a statement of renunciation is filed with the family court for the deceased's last address, at a cost of 800 yen in revenue stamps per declarant plus postage stamps for communication
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 民法 第 915 条、第 921 条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/129AC0000000089>；裁判所「相続の放棄の申述」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.courts.go.jp/saiban/syurui/syurui_kazi/kazi_06_13/index.html>
- Notes: As of 2026-10-03. When a foreigner dies, inheritance in principle follows the deceased's national law; see entry 1 of this section (an estate is inherited under the deceased's national law). Only if that national law in turn points to Japanese law does this procedure apply, so find that out first before counting the deadline. If 3 months is not enough to investigate the estate, you can apply for an extension within the deadline. The benefit is rated large because it avoids inheriting debts that may be in the hundreds of thousands of yen or more.

### 3. When someone in health insurance dies, the family member holding the funeral can claim a burial allowance or funeral expenses; under company insurance it is 50,000 yen
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 在健康保险里的人去世，办丧事的家属可以领埋葬费或葬祭费，公司保险是 5 万日元 | c053bf5b -->
- Cost: Free. For company health insurance, apply to the health insurance society or Japan Health Insurance Association; for National Health Insurance, apply at the ward office.
- In plain words: When someone in company health insurance dies, the person they supported who holds the funeral can claim a 50,000 yen burial allowance. If there is no such person, whoever actually holds the funeral can claim actual costs up to 50,000 yen. Under National Health Insurance it is called funeral expenses (葬祭費); the amount is set by each municipality, and you apply at the ward office.
- Benefit: The Health Insurance Act provides that when an insured person dies, a burial allowance in an amount set by cabinet order is paid to the person whose livelihood the insured person maintained and who holds the burial; if there is no such person, the person who holds the burial is paid the burial costs within that amount. The Enforcement Order of the Health Insurance Act sets this amount at 50,000 yen. The National Health Insurance Act provides that municipalities and associations pay funeral expenses or provide funeral benefits on an insured person's death, under their ordinances or rules
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 健康保険法 第百条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/211AC0000000070> ; 健康保険法施行令 第三十五条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/215IO0000000243> ; 国民健康保険法 第五十八条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/333AC0000000192>
- Notes: The benefit is rated medium at tens of thousands of yen. If the deceased paid into the pension, survivors may also have a survivors' pension and the like; ask the pension office. If the deceased had already claimed the pension lump-sum withdrawal payment but died before receiving it, family members who lived with them can receive it on their behalf; see the source notes to Section 4, entry 3 (the pension lump-sum withdrawal payment).

### 4. If someone with income in Japan dies partway through the year, the heirs file a quasi-final tax return for them within 4 months of learning of the death; tax overpaid can be refunded and tax underpaid must be paid
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 在日本有收入的人年中去世，继承人在知道后 4 个月内替他做准确定申告，多扣了的税能退回来，少交了的要补 | 8affa9a8 -->
- Cost: Free. The heirs collect the deceased's income and deductions from January 1 of that year to the day of death and file with the tax office. If there are several heirs, in principle they file jointly.
- In plain words: If someone with income in Japan dies partway through the year, the heirs must file and pay the income tax for that year up to the death on their behalf. The deadline is 4 months from the day after learning the inheritance began. This is called a quasi-final return (準確定申告). If there are several heirs, in principle they file jointly.
- Benefit: The National Tax Agency states: for a person who died during the year, the heirs (including universal legatees) calculate the income and tax determined from January 1 to the date of death and file and pay within 4 months from the day after learning that the inheritance began; this is called a quasi-final return; where there are 2 or more heirs, in principle each heir signs a joint quasi-final return
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国税庁「No.2022 納税者が死亡したときの確定申告（準確定申告）」（令和 8 年 4 月 1 日現在法令等）. <https://www.nta.go.jp/taxes/shiraberu/taxanswer/shotoku/2022.htm>
- Notes: As of 2026-10-03. An employee's salary has tax withheld every month, but someone who dies mid-year has had no year-end adjustment, so filing may produce a refund or a payment, depending on the actual return. For how the medical expense deduction works, see Section 6, entry 4 (medical expense deduction). If all heirs are in the country of origin and cannot come to Japan, they can appoint a tax agent; see Section 4, entry 4 (appoint a tax agent). The benefit is rated medium because tens of thousands of yen may come back.

### 5. Relatives or people who lived with the deceased must return the deceased's residence card to immigration within 14 days
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=自由 -->
<!-- zh: 死者的在留卡，亲属或同住的人要在 14 天内交回入管 | a35be2d6 -->
- Cost: Free. Mail or hand the residence card to the regional immigration office. If it is found later, the 14 days count from the day it is found.
- In plain words: When a mid- to long-term resident dies, their residence card becomes invalid. The deceased's relatives or people who lived with them must return the card to immigration within 14 days of the death. If it is only found later, the 14 days count from the day it is found.
- Benefit: The Immigration Act provides that a residence card becomes invalid when the mid- to long-term resident holding it dies. When it becomes invalid for this reason, the relatives of or people living with the deceased mid- to long-term resident must return the card to the Commissioner of the Immigration Services Agency within 14 days of the death (or, if the card is found after the death, of the day it is found)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国管理及び難民認定法 第十九条の十四第六号、第十九条の十五第四項. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/326CO0000000319>
- Notes: The benefit is rated small because it fulfils a legal duty. The Immigration Act sets fines for failing to return a residence card on time in other cases, but the penalty provision excludes this one (relatives returning a deceased person's card).

### 6. When someone dies in Japan, file the death notice within 7 days; cremation needs a cremation permit and cannot take place within 24 hours of death
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->
<!-- zh: 人在日本去世，7 天内交死亡届，拿到火葬许可后才能火化，去世 24 小时内不能火化 | d55abd01 -->
- Cost: Free. Take the death certificate or post-mortem report written by a doctor to the ward office for the place of death or the deceased's address, file the death notice, and apply for a cremation permit at the same time. Most funeral companies can do this for you.
- In plain words: When a foreigner dies in Japan, a death notice (死亡届) must also be filed. Relatives living with the deceased must file it within 7 days of learning of the death, with the doctor's death certificate attached. Cremation or burial requires a permit from the mayor of the municipality first, and can only take place 24 hours or more after death.
- Benefit: The Family Register Act provides that a death notice is filed by the person obliged to report within 7 days of learning of the death, with a medical certificate or post-mortem report attached. Those obliged to report are, in order, relatives living with the deceased, other people living with them, and the landlord, landowner or manager; relatives not living with them, guardians and others may also report. The Ministry of Justice states that foreigners have no family register, but when they die in Japan the Family Register Act applies, and a death notice must be filed with the municipality where they were. The Graveyards and Burials Act provides that, unless other laws provide otherwise, burial or cremation may only take place after 24 hours have passed since death; anyone who intends to bury or cremate must obtain the mayor's permission
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 戸籍法 第八十六条、第八十七条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/322AC0000000224> ; 法務省「国際結婚、海外での出生等に関する戸籍Ｑ＆Ａ」. <https://www.moj.go.jp/MINJI/minji15.html> ; 墓地、埋葬等に関する法律 第三条、第五条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/323AC0000000048>
- Notes: The benefit is rated small because it gets the procedures right and saves wasted trips. If you need proof of death for use in the country of origin, you can request a certificate of acceptance of the death notice or a certificate of the recorded matters at the ward office where you filed.
