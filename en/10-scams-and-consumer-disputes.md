# 10. Scams and consumer disputes: fake police and consulates, social media scams, sales contracts

<!-- zh-intro: 48f69128 -->

This section covers the scams foreigners in Japan meet most often, how to limit losses after being scammed, and how to cancel contracts signed through door-to-door or telephone sales and who to ask. Figures come from the final 2025 data published by Japan's National Police Agency. For impersonation of authorities from your country of origin, the warnings of the Chinese Embassy in Japan are used as the example; readers from other countries and regions should check their own embassy's or consulate's guidance.

### 1. If someone claiming to be the police calls saying your account or phone is involved in a crime, hang up, look up the police station's number yourself, and call back to ask
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 自称警察打电话说你的账户或手机涉案，先挂断，自己查警察署的号码打回去问 | 1ffb1b6b -->
- Cost: Free. Hang up, look up your local police station's main number online and call it, or call #9110.
- In plain words: In 2025 Japan had over 11,000 fake-police scams, with over 100 billion yen stolen, seven-tenths of all losses to special fraud. People in their 30s were the largest group of victims. Scammers say "your account was used for a crime" and send fake police IDs and fake arrest warrants. Caller ID can be spoofed to show a real police station's number. Japanese police do not interrogate people over the phone.
- Benefit: The National Police Agency states that scams are increasing in which people claiming to be police, citing reasons such as "your account was used for a crime" or "a phone was illegally contracted in your name", say they need to "protect your assets" or "investigate your account" and get people to hand over cash or transfer money. The impersonators create panic by saying "you will be arrested", sometimes showing fake police notebooks or fake arrest warrants. Some first claim to be a phone company or the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications and then pass you to the "police", or move contact to social media or video calls. Some spoof the real main numbers of prefectural police headquarters or police stations; international numbers starting with "+" often end in "0110". Police do not conduct questioning over the phone. In 2025 there were 11,014 recognized fake-police scams, 39.6% of all special fraud; losses were 100.50 billion yen, 70.6%. Victims in their 30s were the largest age group, and losses per case were highest for people in their 70s
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 警察庁 SOS47「ニセ警察詐欺に注意！」（from 2025-11-17, accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.npa.go.jp/bureau/safetylife/sos47/new-topics/241218/02.html>
- Notes: With foreigners, scammers often use "your residence status will be revoked" or "you will be deported" as leverage. If you are unsure whether there is any problem with your residence, call immigration's helpline; see Section 2, entry 11 (immigration's Information Center).

### 2. A call from your country's embassy or home-country police saying you are involved in a case and must transfer money to a "safe account" is always a scam (this scam mainly targets Chinese nationals)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 自称本国使领馆或者国内公安来电，说你涉案、要你转账到「安全账户」，一律是诈骗（这类骗局主要针对中国籍的人） | 38b3fdda -->
- Cost: Free. After hanging up, look up the number on your embassy's or consulate's official website and call it to check.
- In plain words: Scammers first use a recorded call claiming to be the embassy, saying you have an uncollected parcel or document, or that a parcel you sent contained prohibited items. They then "transfer you" to the police back home, send a fake wanted notice, and have you pay a "deposit" or transfer money to a "safe account". The Chinese Embassy in Japan states that police, prosecutors and courts never transfer calls between each other, and anyone who offers to transfer you to them is a scammer.
- Benefit: The Chinese Embassy in Japan's July 2025 notice "Common scams in Japan, part 4" describes two scripts: first, a recorded call claiming to be a Chinese embassy or consulate says an important document or parcel is uncollected, and after transfer to a "human agent" says the parcel was sent by a public security bureau in China and involves a serious case such as illegal fundraising; second, someone impersonates embassy staff with a false name and staff number and says a parcel the victim sent abroad contained undeclared gold and bank cards and is suspected of smuggling. The scammers threaten that failure to cooperate will lead to revocation of residence status or even a wanted listing, suggest a "transfer" to public security in China, then move into impersonating police, prosecutors and courts, showing fake wanted notices and arrest warrants, demanding "bail money" or a "security deposit", and having money transferred to a "safe account". The embassy warns: state bodies such as police, prosecutors and courts do not transfer calls; anyone claiming to be staff of an embassy or consulate abroad, a telecom operator or a bank who offers to transfer you to the police, prosecutors or courts is a scammer. The consular assistance number published by the embassy is 03-6450-2195
- Evidence: B
- Sources: 中华人民共和国驻日本国大使馆「日本常见诈骗手法之四：冒充使领馆工作人员」（2025-07-21）. <https://jp.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw_0/lstx_138409/202507/t20250721_11673678.htm>
- Notes: Why grade B: this is an official warning, with no victim numbers. Readers who are not Chinese nationals may also find their own embassy or consulate impersonated with the same script. To check, use the number published on your embassy's or consulate's official website, not the number on your caller ID.

### 3. Never invest when someone on social media recommends a "sure profit" or "guaranteed principal" investment, or when an investment ad uses a celebrity's photo
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: SNS 上有人推荐「必赚」「保本」的投资，或者用名人头像的投资广告，一律不投 | 4f2ad4ea -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is holding back when "everyone in the group made money".
- In plain words: In 2025 Japan had over 9,500 social media investment scams, with over 128 billion yen stolen, up nearly half on the year before. Ads often steal celebrities' photos and videos and promise "certain profit" or "guaranteed principal". Someone you met on social media who pulls you into an investment group and has you download an app and transfer money is this kind of scam.
- Benefit: The National Police Agency reports 9,523 recognized social-media investment scams in 2025, up 3,110 or 48.5% on the previous year; losses were 128.80 billion yen, up 41.69 billion yen. From March 2025, ads stealing celebrities' photos and videos tended to increase, and some ads contained phrases such as "certain profit" and "guaranteed principal"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 警察庁「令和７年における特殊詐欺及びＳＮＳ型投資・ロマンス詐欺の認知・検挙状況等について（確定値）」. <https://www.npa.go.jp/bureau/safetylife/sos47/new-topics/260605/01.html>
- Notes: If you are not sure whether an investment is legitimate, call the consumer hotline 188, or check on the Financial Services Agency website whether the firm is registered.

### 4. An online partner you have never met who asks for money or gets you to invest is running a romance scam
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 网上认识、没见过面的恋人开口要钱，或者带你投资，就是恋爱诈骗 | 0d471be3 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is admitting, while emotionally involved, that the other person may be a scammer; ideally go through the chat history with someone you trust.
- In plain words: In 2025 Japan had over 5,600 social media romance scams, with over 54 billion yen stolen, also up nearly half on the year before. A "partner" you have never met and only chat with online who asks to borrow money, suggests investing together, or asks you to help pay fees should be treated as a scam.
- Benefit: The National Police Agency reports 5,645 recognized social-media romance scams in 2025, up 1,821 or 47.6% on the previous year; losses were 54.64 billion yen, up 14.55 billion yen
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 警察庁「令和７年における特殊詐欺及びＳＮＳ型投資・ロマンス詐欺の認知・検挙状況等について（確定値）」. <https://www.npa.go.jp/bureau/safetylife/sos47/new-topics/260605/01.html>
- Notes: Someone chatting with you in Chinese or in your hometown dialect is not more trustworthy for that.

### 5. If you have already transferred money, call the receiving bank and the police at once to have the account frozen; later you may get part of it back
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 已经转了账，马上打给收款的银行和警察，申请冻结账户，之后可能分回一部分钱 | ff68d3f5 -->
- Cost: Free. The sooner the better: first call the bank holding the receiving account, then report to the police or call #9110.
- In plain words: If you were scammed into a bank transfer, contact the police and the receiving bank immediately. If the bank freezes that account, the money left in it may later be returned to victims in proportion. Investment and romance scams count too, as long as the money was transferred. Once the scammer withdraws the money it cannot be recovered, so act fast. Once refunds start, the claim period is at least 30 days, and if you miss it you get nothing.
- Benefit: The Financial Services Agency states that under the Act on Remittance Fraud Relief, money left in the receiving account may be returned to victims of transfer scams. After being scammed, contact the police and the receiving financial institution; social-media investment and romance scams paid by bank transfer are also covered; once the criminal withdraws the money it cannot be recovered. When a financial institution has reasonable grounds to find an account was used for crime, the account holder's rights are extinguished, and damage recovery distributions are paid to victims from the account balance. The claim period runs for at least 30 days from the day after the public notice that payment procedures have begun, and those who do not claim within it receive nothing. If total losses exceed the balance, it is shared in proportion to each person's loss, so full repayment is not guaranteed. The Deposit Insurance Corporation of Japan's website publishes the balances and claim periods of accounts that have been publicly noticed
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 金融庁「振り込め詐欺等の被害にあわれた方へ」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.fsa.go.jp/policy/kyuusai/furikome/index.html> ; 預金保険機構「「振り込め詐欺救済法」に基づく返金制度を装った詐欺行為にご注意ください」. <https://www.dic.go.jp/happyo/page_001772.html>
- Notes: The benefit is rated medium because how much comes back is uncertain and often only part. The Deposit Insurance Corporation has specifically warned of scams posing as this refund system.

### 6. Contracts signed through door-to-door or telephone sales can be cancelled unconditionally within 8 days of receiving the written documents; notify in writing or by email and keep a record
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 上门推销、电话推销签下的合同，拿到书面材料 8 天内可以无条件解约，用书面或邮件通知并留好记录 | 5bb1df4e -->
- Cost: Free. Write a postcard, or send an email or use the seller's web form, and keep a record of sending it.
- In plain words: A contract signed after being approached at your door or in the street, or after a sales call, can be cancelled unilaterally without giving a reason. The deadline is 8 days from the day you received the written contract documents. For pyramid-style recruitment businesses it is 20 days. Online shopping is not covered; whether you can return goods depends on the shop's stated return policy.
- Benefit: Act on Specified Commercial Transactions, Article 9: for a door-to-door sales contract made outside business premises, the buyer may withdraw the offer or cancel in writing or by electronic record within 8 days from receiving the statutory documents. Act on Specified Commercial Transactions, Article 24: telephone sales contracts likewise have 8 days. Act on Specified Commercial Transactions, Article 40: chain sales (pyramid-style recruitment businesses) have 20 days. Act on Specified Commercial Transactions, Article 15-3: online purchases can be returned within 8 days of receiving the goods, but if the shop states a special return clause in its advertising, that clause applies, and the buyer pays return shipping.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 特定商取引に関する法律 第 9、15 条の 3、24、40 条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/351AC0000000057>
- Notes: The deadline counts from the day you receive the statutory documents, not the day you sign. If it is past the deadline or you are unsure whether you can cancel, call 188; see entry 7 of this section (call 188). The benefit is rated medium by judgment: what you recover is the contract amount, which ranges from tens of thousands to several hundred thousand yen. The beneficiary is you.

### 7. If you lose out on a purchase or contract, call 188 to reach your local consumer affairs center
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 买东西、签合同吃了亏，打 188 找当地的消费生活中心 | a2949977 -->
- Cost: Free. One phone call. If you cannot explain things well in Japanese, first find a Japanese speaker to call with you.
- In plain words: Calling 188 connects you to the consumer affairs center near where you live. You can ask about things you were pressured into buying, refused cancellations and online shopping disputes. In principle you can call every day except facility maintenance days and the New Year period. It does not connect you to the Consumer Affairs Agency itself.
- Benefit: Consumer Affairs Agency: the consumer hotline 188 refers you to a consultation counter such as the consumer affairs center of a nearby municipality or prefecture; it does not connect to the Consumer Affairs Agency. In principle it is available every day except facility maintenance days and the New Year period.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: 消費者庁. 消費者ホットライン. <https://www.caa.go.jp/policies/policy/local_cooperation/local_consumer_administration/hotline/>
- Notes: Graded B because this is only a channel, with no figure for how much money calling recovers. The benefit is rated medium by judgment. Door-to-door and telephone sales contracts can be cancelled within 8 days; see entry 6 of this section (unconditional cancellation within 8 days). If you have already been scammed into a transfer, first follow entry 5 of this section (call the receiving bank and the police at once).

### 8. A call saying "you overpaid medical or insurance fees and we'll refund you; just go to an ATM" is always a scam: an ATM can only send money out, not receive refunds
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 电话里说「医疗费、保险费多交了要退给你，去 ATM 操作一下」，一律是诈骗：ATM 上只能转出，不能领退款 | 9e2f72c0 -->
- Cost: Free. Hang up, look up the number of the ward office, tax office or pension office yourself and call to ask. Lower your ATM's daily transfer limit.
- In plain words: The scammer claims to be from the ward office, tax office or pension office, saying you overpaid medical or insurance fees or have unclaimed pension. They press you to go to an ATM right away with your phone and press buttons as they say. Doing so transfers your money to them. The National Police Agency states it is absolutely impossible to get a refund through an ATM.
- Benefit: The National Police Agency states: refund fraud is a method of pretending to process a refund such as a tax refund, getting the victim to operate an ATM, and stealing money through an account-to-account transfer. The scammer claims to be staff of a local government, tax office or pension office, says there is an overpayment of medical or insurance fees or unpaid pension to collect, rushes the victim by saying "refunds have a deadline", and has them go immediately with their phone to a nearby ATM; following the instructions actually transfers money into the scammer's account. The agency states that "money is never refunded at an ATM", and the precautions are: block international calls to your landline, do not answer unknown numbers on your mobile, call the nearest police station to check whether it is real, know that you cannot receive money at an ATM, and set a low ATM usage limit
- Evidence: B
- Sources: 警察庁「還付金詐欺」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.npa.go.jp/bureau/safetylife/sos47/case/refund/>
- Notes: As of 2026-10-03. Graded B because the official source gives the method and countermeasures, with no figure on how much less you lose by following them. Real refunds come as a written notice and are paid into your registered account; you are never asked to operate an ATM. People less fluent in Japanese are more easily rattled by "the deadline is almost up"; if unsure, hang up first. For calls from fake police, see entry 1 of this section (someone claiming to be the police calls). Remind older family members of this entry too; see Section 15, entry 3 ("I'll go home and talk it over with my family"). The benefit is rated medium because typical losses are at most in the hundreds of thousands of yen.
