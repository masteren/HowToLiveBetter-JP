# 17. Permanent residence, naturalization and leaving Japan

<!-- zh-intro: 9ddc6486 -->

This section covers the key points for staying in Japan long-term or leaving for good: how the years for permanent residence are counted, when permanent residence can be revoked after you get it, thinking through your original nationality before naturalizing, and the procedures to complete in order before leaving Japan. The Immigration Services Agency's permanent residence guidelines were just revised on October 1, 2026; information is as of 2026-10-03.

### 1. Even on an ordinary work visa, calculate your highly skilled professional points: if you had 70 points from 3 years ago, you can apply for permanent residence after 3 years, and with 80 points after just 1
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
<!-- zh: 拿普通工作签证的也按高度人才积分算一算：3 年前起就够 70 分的，住满 3 年就能申请永住，80 分只要 1 年 | eba66fea -->
- Cost: Free. Work through the Immigration Services Agency's points table for education, years of work, annual income, age, Japanese and so on. You need documents proving each item.
- In plain words: Permanent residence normally requires 10 years in Japan. By the highly skilled professional points table, people who reach 70 points and keep them for 3 years can apply after 3 years, and with 80 points after just 1. You do not need to hold the "Highly Skilled Professional" visa; having enough points from 3 years before the application date also counts. Spouses of Japanese nationals can apply after 3 years of marriage and 1 year living in Japan.
- Benefit: The Immigration Services Agency's "Guidelines for Permission for Permanent Residence" state that exceptions to the normal 10 years of residence include: spouses of Japanese nationals, permanent residents and special permanent residents whose actual married life has continued for 3 years or more and who have stayed in Japan continuously for 1 year or more; those with 70 points or more under the Highly Skilled Professional ordinance who have stayed continuously for 3 years or more as highly skilled foreign professionals maintaining the required points, or who had 70 points or more calculated as of 3 years before the application date and have stayed in Japan with 70 points or more for 3 years or more since; and for those with 80 points or more under the same calculation, the years in both cases are 1 year or more. Spouses or children of Japanese nationals, permanent residents and special permanent residents are not required to meet "good conduct" and "having assets or skills sufficient for an independent livelihood"

- Notes: As of 2026-10-03. For the points table and the proof required for each item, see the Immigration Services Agency's page on the "points-based system for highly skilled professionals" (高度人材ポイント制). Only the number of years is shortened; the requirement to pay taxes, pension and health insurance on time is unchanged; see Section 4, entry 5 (pay taxes, pension and health insurance on time). For how the normal 10 years is counted, see entry 2 of this section (count your years first). The benefit is rated large because you can get permanent residence several years earlier.

### 2. If you plan to apply for permanent residence, count your years first: normally 10 years or more in Japan, including 5 consecutive years on a work or residence-based status, without any single trip abroad over 6 months
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
<!-- zh: 打算申请永住，先算年数：原则在日本 10 年以上，其中工作或居住资格连续 5 年以上，中途别一次出国超过半年 | a05150ad -->
- Cost: Free. List your statuses of residence, periods of stay and trips abroad over the years, and check them against the guidelines.
- In plain words: Permanent residence normally requires 10 consecutive years or more in Japan, including 5 consecutive years or more on a work visa or residence-based status. The new guidelines spell out how time abroad counts against you. In the 10 years before applying, a single trip abroad of over 6 months, or a total of 2 years and 6 months out of Japan, without good reason, counts against you in principle. Spouses of Japanese nationals or permanent residents and some others have exceptions with fewer years.
- Benefit: The Immigration Services Agency's "Guidelines for Permission for Permanent Residence" (revised October 1, 2026) state: at the time of application, continuous residence in Japan of 10 years or more is in principle a factor considered; within that, continuous residence of 5 years or more on a work or residence-based status, and currently holding that status with the longest period of stay set in Appended Table 2 of the enforcement regulations, are also factors. For applications made by March 31, 2027, a "3-year" period of stay is also treated as the longest period. These periods in principle require living in Japan; in the 10 years before applying, being out of Japan for 6 months or more on a single trip, or 2 years and 6 months or more in total, without good reason, is in principle assessed negatively. Exceptions include: spouses of Japanese nationals, permanent residents and special permanent residents with 5 years or more of actual married life and 3 or more consecutive years in Japan; their biological children and similar with 3 or more consecutive years in Japan; Long-Term Residents with 5 or more consecutive years in Japan; and those maintaining 70 or more highly skilled professional points for 3 years or more
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国在留管理庁「永住許可に関するガイドライン（令和８年１０月１日改定）」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/10_00279.html>
- Notes: The benefit is rated large because permanent residence means no more renewals and no restrictions on work. The guidelines also look at payment of taxes and social insurance; see Section 4, entry 5 (pay taxes, pension and health insurance on time), and at how much pension you will receive in future. If you return to your country of origin for long periods to care for family, keep track of your days abroad.

### 3. After you get permanent residence, keep paying taxes and insurance on time: from April 2027, deliberately not paying can lead to revocation of permanent residence
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
<!-- zh: 拿到永住以后也要按时交税和保险，2027 年 4 月起故意不交可能被取消永住 | df6eb6ef -->
- Cost: No extra money, since you owe these anyway. The hard part is always paying on time; ideally set up automatic debit.
- In plain words: The 2024 amendment to the Immigration Act added several grounds for revoking permanent residence. They include not complying with duties under the Immigration Act, deliberately not paying taxes and social insurance, and being sentenced to imprisonment for specified crimes. "Deliberately not paying" means knowing you should pay and plainly not doing so; genuinely being unable to pay does not count. Even if revoked, most people would be given Long-Term Resident status instead, not made to leave at once.
- Benefit: The Immigration Services Agency's "Guidelines on Revocation of Permanent Resident Status" state that the 2024 amendment to the Immigration Act and related laws added, as grounds for revoking permanent resident status, failure to comply with duties under the Immigration Act, deliberate non-payment of public taxes and charges, and being sentenced to imprisonment for violating specified criminal laws. "Non-payment of public taxes and charges" is not limited to actual arrears; it also covers not paying what should have been paid by knowingly failing to file a required return. "Deliberately" means refusing to pay while aware of the obligation, and applies only where, considering the history of arrears, subsequent living circumstances and the person's words and actions, there is clearly no intention to pay; it does not apply where there are unavoidable circumstances and the person is not at fault. Failure to fulfil duties under the Immigration Act for a legitimate reason such as illness or disaster is judged individually. Even where grounds for revocation apply, unless the person is unsuitable to remain in Japan, the Minister of Justice will on their own authority permit a change to a status other than permanent resident, in most cases expected to be Long-Term Resident; if the conditions are later met again, the person can reapply for permanent residence. The permanent residence guidelines state that this amendment takes effect on April 1, 2027
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国在留管理庁「永住者の在留資格の取消しに関するガイドライン」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/11_00118.html> ; 出入国在留管理庁「永住許可に関するガイドライン（令和８年１０月１日改定）」. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/10_00279.html>
- Notes: The guidelines state that deliberate non-payment after permanent residence was granted but before the amendment takes effect may also become grounds for revocation if discovered after it takes effect. But subsequent payment will be taken into account. Duties under the Immigration Act include carrying your residence card and reporting your address within 14 days; see Section 2, entry 1 (carry your residence card when you go out). Permanent residents leaving Japan also need re-entry permission; see Section 2, entry 4 (tick re-entry when leaving).

### 4. Before naturalizing as a Japanese citizen, think through your original nationality: Japan requires you to give it up, and Chinese nationals who settle abroad and acquire a foreign nationality automatically lose Chinese nationality
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
<!-- zh: 归化日本籍之前，先想清楚原籍国国籍：日本要求放弃原国籍，中国籍的人定居外国后入外籍会自动失去中国国籍 | 4bc98dd2 -->
- Cost: Free. First check your country of origin's nationality law, then consult the Legal Affairs Bureau about naturalization.
- In plain words: Japan's Nationality Act requires, for naturalization, 5 or more consecutive years with an address in Japan, being 18 or older, good conduct and the ability to support yourself. You must also have no nationality, or lose your original nationality on acquiring Japanese nationality. Chinese nationals should know that China does not recognize dual nationality, and those who settle abroad and voluntarily acquire a foreign nationality automatically lose Chinese nationality.
- Benefit: Japan's Nationality Act provides that the Minister of Justice may permit naturalization only for foreigners who meet the following: an address in Japan for 5 or more consecutive years; being 18 or older and having capacity under their national law; good conduct; being able to make a living through their own assets or skills or those of a spouse or other relatives sharing their livelihood; having no nationality, or losing their original nationality by acquiring Japanese nationality; and not having planned or advocated the violent destruction of the Constitution of Japan or the government, among others. Where a foreigner cannot lose their nationality regardless of their own will, and there are special circumstances such as a family relationship with a Japanese national, the "losing original nationality" condition may be waived. The Nationality Law of the People's Republic of China provides: the People's Republic of China does not recognize dual nationality for any Chinese national; any Chinese national who has settled abroad and who has voluntarily joined or acquired foreign nationality shall automatically lose Chinese nationality
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国籍法 第五条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/325AC0000000147> ; 中华人民共和国国籍法 第三条、第九条（外交部领事司网站）. <https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zlbg/flfg/crjxg/200312/t20031201_961842.shtml>
- Notes: The benefit is rated medium because nationality is hard to regain once lost, and affects your status and property in your country of origin. Whether other countries and regions allow dual nationality varies, so check your own country's rules before naturalizing. Permanent residence and naturalization are two different things; permanent residence does not change your nationality.

### 5. To leave Japan for good, do things in order: move-out notice, resident tax, pension lump sum, bank account and phone, and finally return your residence card at the airport
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 彻底离开日本，按顺序办：转出届、住民税、年金一时金、银行账户和手机，最后在机场交回在留卡 | 4c97f829 -->
- Cost: Free. Start one or two weeks before leaving, with trips to the ward office, bank and phone shop, an hour or two each.
- In plain words: The Basic Resident Registration Act says that moving out of Japan requires filing a move-out notice (転出届) at the ward office in advance, stating your destination and planned date. Pay resident tax in full or appoint a tax agent; apply for the pension lump-sum withdrawal payment after moving out; close your bank account and cancel your phone, and do not sell them to anyone. If you leave without re-entry permission, your residence card is handed back at the airport when you depart.
- Benefit: The Basic Resident Registration Act provides that a person moving out must notify the mayor in advance of their name, destination and planned date of moving out. The Immigration Act provides that when a mid- to long-term resident leaves Japan without re-entry permission and receives departure confirmation at the port, the residence card becomes invalid and must be returned immediately. The basis for the other items: for resident tax, see Section 4, entry 1 (resident tax arrives in your second year); for the pension lump sum, see Section 4, entry 3 (the pension lump-sum withdrawal payment) and Section 4, entry 4 (20.42% tax withheld); for bank accounts and phone numbers, see Section 5, entry 6 (do not sell your bank account or phone number)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 住民基本台帳法 第二十四条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/342AC0000000081> ; 出入国管理及び難民認定法 第十九条の十四第三号、第十九条の十五第二項. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/326CO0000000319>
- Notes: The benefit is rated medium because missing one item can mean losing a pension lump sum of hundreds of thousands of yen, or being left with unpaid tax. The pension lump-sum claim only counts if it reaches the Japan Pension Service on or after your move-out date; see Section 4, entry 3 (the pension lump-sum withdrawal payment). If you plan to come back later, do not hand in the card; get re-entry permission instead; see Section 2, entry 4 (tick re-entry when leaving).
