# 9. When money runs out: unemployment benefits, payment exemptions, emergency loans and public assistance

<!-- zh-intro: ed2e9e41 -->

This section covers the safety nets foreigners can use when they lose their job or their income suddenly stops: unemployment benefits from employment insurance, the unemployment exemption from National Pension contributions, emergency small loans from the social welfare council, the housing security benefit, and which statuses of residence give access to public assistance. Amounts and conditions are as of 2026-10-03. After losing a job, your status of residence itself also has a time limit; see Section 2, entry 8 (do not stay idle for more than 3 months after leaving a job or school).

### 1. If you lose your job, first apply for the unemployment exemption from National Pension contributions, so the contributions do not become "unpaid"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 失业了先申请国民年金的失业特例免除，别让保险料变成「未缴」 | b8628711 -->
- Cost: Free. Take a copy of your separation notice (離職票) or employment insurance eligibility certificate to the National Pension counter at the ward office or to a pension office and apply.
- In plain words: If you cannot afford National Pension contributions, applying for an exemption and simply not paying lead to very different results. After losing a job, you can apply for an exemption under the unemployment special rule without regard to last year's income. Approved periods count toward the qualifying period for a pension, and if you become disabled during an exempted period, you may get a disability pension. Simply not paying makes the period "unpaid", with none of this.
- Benefit: The Japan Pension Service states that where unemployment, bankruptcy, closing a business or similar is confirmed, you can apply for exemption or deferral under a special rule regardless of the unemployed person's income in the previous year. Those who were insured under employment insurance attach a copy of the separation notice or of the employment insurance eligibility certificate. Fully exempted periods count toward the qualifying period for the basic old-age pension and partly toward the pension amount; deferred periods count only toward the qualifying period, not the amount. If an accident during an approved exemption or deferral period causes disability or death and certain conditions are met, the basic disability pension or basic survivors' pension can be received. Periods that become unpaid because no application was made count toward neither the qualifying period nor the pension amount
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 日本年金機構「国民年金保険料の免除制度・納付猶予制度」（updated 2026-08-03, accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.nenkin.go.jp/service/kokunen/menjo/20150428.html>
- Notes: The benefit is rated large because the basic disability pension is paid yearly over a long period. Students use the student payment special rule; see Section 4, entry 2 (student payment special rule). An unpaid record counts against a permanent residence application; see Section 4, entry 5 (pay taxes, pension and health insurance on time).

### 2. Public assistance is only open to statuses such as Permanent Resident, Long-Term Resident and spouses of Japanese nationals or permanent residents; work-visa holders and students cannot use it
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 生活保护只对永住、定住、日本人和永住者的配偶等在留资格开放，工作签、留学生用不了 | 6b823889 -->
- Cost: Free. Apply at your local welfare office, explaining your income, assets and family situation.
- In plain words: The Public Assistance Act (生活保護法) itself applies only to Japanese nationals; foreigners are handled by administrative measures "in accordance with" it. Those covered are mainly Permanent Residents, Long-Term Residents, spouses of Japanese nationals, spouses of permanent residents, special permanent residents and recognized refugees. People on work visas such as Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services, students and Dependent visa holders are generally not covered, and have to rely on the options in the other entries.
- Benefit: The MHLW Social Welfare and War Victims' Relief Bureau states that the Public Assistance Act is rooted in Article 25 of the Constitution and covers only Japanese nationals. However, for foreigners legally in Japan with statuses such as Permanent Resident or Long-Term Resident whose activities are not restricted, protection in accordance with the Public Assistance Act is given as an administrative measure on humanitarian grounds (1954 notice of the Director-General of the Social Affairs Bureau, Ministry of Health and Welfare). Specifically: holders of statuses in Appended Table II of the Immigration Act (Permanent Resident, Long-Term Resident, Spouse or Child of Permanent Resident, Spouse or Child of Japanese National); special permanent residents; and recognized refugees under the Immigration Act. Holders of Designated Activities under Appended Table I(5) whose activities in Japan are not restricted may also be covered in some cases
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 厚生労働省社会・援護局「生活保護における外国人の取扱いについて」. <https://www.mhlw.go.jp/content/001684937.pdf> ; 厚生労働省「生活に困窮する外国人に対する生活保護の措置について」（昭和 29 年 5 月 8 日社発第 382 号）. <https://www.mhlw.go.jp/web/t_doc?dataId=00ta1609&dataType=1&pageNo=1>
- Notes: "In accordance with" means it is given as an administrative measure, not as a legal right. The benefit is rated large because public assistance is paid monthly on an ongoing basis. Whether receiving public assistance affects later residence renewals or permanent residence applications depends on the case, and this entry draws no conclusion. Before applying, you can ask immigration's helpline; see Section 2, entry 11 (immigration's Information Center).

### 3. After leaving a job, take your separation notice to Hello Work to claim unemployment benefits; if you quit yourself, you first wait 1 to 3 months
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 离职后拿着离职票去 Hello Work 申请失业金，自己辞职的要先等 1 到 3 个月 | 9381030b -->
- Cost: Free. Take the separation notice from your company, your residence card and so on to the Hello Work (public employment security office, ハローワーク) for your address, register as a job seeker, and then attend the unemployment certification on schedule.
- In plain words: Anyone working 20 hours or more a week and expected to be employed for 31 days or more should be in employment insurance (雇用保険), foreigners included. If you were insured for 12 months in the 2 years before leaving, you can claim unemployment benefits after losing the job. If you left because the company let you go or similar, 6 months in 1 year is enough. You can usually receive them for 90 to 150 days, and people let go for up to 330 days. If you quit yourself, payment is first withheld for 1 to 3 months.
- Benefit: The Employment Insurance Act provides that it does not apply to, among others, people whose scheduled working hours are under 20 a week and people not expected to be employed by the same employer for 31 consecutive days or more. When an insured person becomes unemployed and their insured periods in the 2 years before leaving total 12 months or more, the basic allowance is paid; for specified eligible recipients who left because of bankruptcy, dismissal and the like, and for people who left for specified reasons such as non-renewal at the end of a contract, it is 6 months in the year before leaving. The prescribed number of benefit days is generally 90, 120 or 150 depending on years insured; for specified eligible recipients, depending on age and years insured, it ranges from 90 days up to 330 days for those aged 45 to under 60 with 20 or more years insured. For people dismissed for their own serious fault, or who quit without a legitimate reason, the basic allowance is not paid for a period of 1 to 3 months set by the head of the employment security office, after the 7-day waiting period; this does not apply to those who take public vocational training as instructed
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 雇用保険法（version in force from 2026-05-13）第六条、第十三条、第二十二条、第二十三条、第三十三条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/349AC0000000116>
- Notes: To receive unemployment benefits you must be able to start work immediately and be actively looking. If your status of residence is a work visa, the work you look for must match your status. You must also report to immigration within 14 days of leaving; see Section 2, entry 3 (reporting after leaving or changing jobs). If the company is slow to give you the separation notice, you can ask Hello Work directly.

### 4. If you cannot pay rent after losing your job, apply for the housing security benefit at the self-reliance support counter; rent is covered for up to 9 months
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 失业后交不起房租，去自立咨询支援窗口申请住居确保给付金，房租最多补 9 个月 | ddccd6a7 -->
- Cost: Free. Apply at your local self-reliance support center, register as a job seeker at Hello Work, and attend job counselling and look for work as required.
- In plain words: Within 2 years of leaving a job or closing a business, or if your income fell for reasons not your own and you cannot pay rent, you can apply for the housing security benefit (住居確保給付金). It covers rent up to a cap, in principle for 3 months and up to 9, paid directly to the landlord. The condition is that you actively look for work through Hello Work.
- Benefit: MHLW's explanation states that the housing security benefit is paid under the Act on Self-Reliance Support for Needy Persons. It covers people who have lost their home or may be unable to pay rent, and who are within 2 years of leaving a job or closing a business, or whose income fell due to reasons not their own, such as being stood down. Requirements include applying at Hello Work or similar and actively seeking work. The amount is the rent, up to a cap; the period is in principle 3 months, up to 9 months; in principle the local government pays directly into the landlord's account. There is also a part that covers moving costs for people who need to move somewhere cheaper. Applications go to the local self-reliance support center
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 厚生労働省「住居確保給付金のご案内」. <https://www.mhlw.go.jp/content/12600000/001436774.pdf> ; 厚生労働省「生活困窮者自立支援制度」. <https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/seisakunitsuite/bunya/0000059425.html>
- Notes: The benefit is rated large because several months' rent is often 100,000 to several hundred thousand yen. There are caps on income and savings, and the amounts differ by municipality; ask at the counter. The explanation does not mention any nationality restriction; whether foreigners can apply follows the counter's answer.

### 5. If you have a dispute and cannot afford a lawyer, go to Houterasu first: if your income is below its threshold, you get 3 free consultations per problem, and it can advance your lawyer's fees
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 遇到纠纷请不起律师，先找法テラス：收入在标准以下的，同一个问题免费咨询 3 次，律师费还能先由它垫付 | 799d8d35 -->
- Cost: The consultations are free. You need to show your income and assets are below the threshold. Book through Houterasu's line for foreigners, 0570-078377, or the general line, 0570-078374.
- In plain words: Houterasu (法テラス) is a legal support body set up by the state. If your income and assets are below its thresholds, you can consult a lawyer or judicial scrivener for free 3 times on the same problem. If you need a lawyer for a lawsuit or mediation, and you meet the conditions, it can also advance the lawyer's fees. Foreigners legally resident in Japan can use it; those without a status of residence cannot.
- Benefit: Houterasu states: free legal consultations are limited to 3 per problem; people who meet Houterasu's conditions can use them; foreigners without an address or lawful status of residence in Japan, and corporations and organizations, are not covered. Free legal consultation requires conditions such as "income and the like below a certain amount"; the scheme for advancing lawyers' and judicial scriveners' fees additionally requires conditions such as "it cannot be said there is no prospect of winning". Houterasu's consultation line is 0570-078374, and its multilingual information line for foreigners is 0570-078377
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 法テラス（日本司法支援センター）「無料法律相談に関するよくあるご質問」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.houterasu.or.jp/site/soudan-tatekae/soudanqa.html>
- Notes: As of 2026-10-03. Income thresholds vary by household size and area; see Houterasu's page on how to use free legal consultations. Ask during the consultation how advanced lawyers' fees are repaid. For pay and dismissal, you can also first call a labour helpline in Chinese; see Section 7, entry 7 (labour helplines in Chinese). The benefit is rated medium because it saves the cost of a consultation and lowers the threshold for hiring a lawyer.

### 6. If you are temporarily short of cash, borrow an emergency small loan from your local social welfare council: up to 100,000 yen, interest-free
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 一时周转不开，去所在地的社会福祉协议会借紧急小额资金：10 万日元以内，不要利息 | 8d859cad -->
- Cost: No interest and no guarantor. You need an interview at your municipality's social welfare council (社会福祉協議会) to explain your situation, and you must repay on schedule.
- In plain words: If something urgent happens and you temporarily cannot get by, you can borrow an emergency small loan (緊急小口資金) from the social welfare council. It is up to 100,000 yen, with no interest and no guarantor. You do not repay for 2 months after borrowing, then repay in full within 8 months. It is a loan, not a grant, and must be repaid.
- Benefit: MHLW's "list of lending conditions for livelihood welfare funds" states: the emergency small loan is a small sum lent when it is urgently and temporarily difficult to maintain a livelihood, with a limit of up to 100,000 yen; repayment is deferred for up to 2 months from the loan date, after which it is repaid within 8 months; interest-free; no guarantor required. Livelihood welfare funds are run by each prefecture's social welfare council
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 厚生労働省「生活福祉資金貸付条件等一覧」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.mhlw.go.jp/bunya/seikatsuhogo/kashitsukejoken.html> ; 厚生労働省「生活福祉資金貸付制度」. <https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/seisakunitsuite/bunya/hukushi_kaigo/seikatsuhogo/seikatsu-fukushi-shikin1/index.html>
- Notes: It is for low-income households and similar, and the social welfare council decides whether you can borrow. The benefit is rated medium because 100,000 yen interest-free saves the interest and fees of a consumer loan. Never turn to high-interest online loans or sell your bank account for quick cash; see Section 5, entry 6 (before leaving Japan, do not sell your bank account or phone number).
