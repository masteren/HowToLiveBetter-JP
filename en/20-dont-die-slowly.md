# 20. Don't die slowly: smoking, alcohol, exercise, sleep and diet

<!-- zh-intro: a6701f71 -->

This section collects lifestyle evidence that applies in any country, carrying over the entries from the original book's section "don't die slowly", with only Chinese prices, medicines and services removed. The research figures are unchanged; most come from studies that only track and record, without randomization, and each entry's notes give its limitations. Entries are ordered by cost-effectiveness: those costing almost nothing with large benefits come first. A few entries in the original book that depend on Chinese services and rules were not carried over: smoking cessation clinics and the 12320 hotline, regulations on e-cigarettes, betel nut, burning coal and wood, and buying cooking oil by its SC number.

### 1. Eat chilli peppers four or more times a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 每周吃四次以上辣椒 | 7cbfd10e -->
- Cost: Free, and no extra time.
- In plain words: People who eat chilli peppers four or more times a week are about 23% less likely to die in the same period than those who rarely do, and about a third less likely to die of cardiovascular disease.
- Benefit: An Italian study of 22,800 people, half of whom were followed for at least 8.2 years, with 1,236 deaths. People eating chilli peppers 4 or more times a week had about 23% lower risk of death than those who rarely did (hazard ratio 0.77, 95% CI 0.66–0.90, confidence range). Cardiovascular death was about 34% lower (0.66, 0.50–0.86)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Bonaccio M, Di Castelnuovo A, Costanzo S, et al. (2019). Chili Pepper Consumption and Mortality in Italian Adults. Journal of the American College of Cardiology, 74(25), 3139-3149. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2019.09.068>
- Notes: The dispute is that this was a follow-up study set against a Mediterranean diet, and people who eat chilli may have overall diets closer to the local traditional way of eating. Note also that for people with gastro-oesophageal reflux, haemorrhoids or irritable bowel syndrome, chilli worsens symptoms, so there is no need to force it

### 2. Do not smoke at home or in the car, and do not let guests smoke at home
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 别在家里和车里抽烟，也别让客人在家里抽 | 52c2a76c -->
- Cost: Free, and no extra time. The hard part is speaking up to ask family and guests not to smoke at home.
- In plain words: About 600,000 people worldwide die each year from breathing other people's smoke, nearly 30% of them children. People with long-term exposure to second-hand smoke have about 30% higher risk of high blood pressure, heart disease and stroke. Exposure at home does more harm than exposure elsewhere.
- Benefit: In 2004, 603,000 people worldwide died from second-hand smoke, about 1.0% of global deaths, 28% of them children. Pooling 57 studies: the odds of high blood pressure for people exposed to second-hand smoke were about 1.28 times those of the unexposed (odds ratio). For heart disease 1.39 times, myocardial infarction 1.50 times, stroke 1.36 times. The risk from exposure at home was higher than from exposure outside the home
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Öberg M, Jaakkola MS, Woodward A, Peruga A, Prüss-Ustün A (2011). Worldwide burden of disease from exposure to second-hand smoke: a retrospective analysis of data from 192 countries. Lancet: "603,000 deaths were attributable to second-hand smoke in 2004, which was about 1·0% of worldwide mortality. 47% of deaths from second-hand smoke occurred in women, 28% in children, and 26% in men", "61% of DALYs were in children". <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61388-8>；(2026). The Associations Between Secondhand Smoke Exposure and Various Cardiovascular Diseases: A Meta-Analysis. Nicotine & Tobacco Research: 57 studies, "hypertension (OR: 1.28, 95% CI: 1.15 to 1.40), heart disease (OR: 1.39, 95% CI: 1.28 to 1.50), myocardial infarction (OR: 1.50, 95% CI: 1.17 to 1.84), stroke (OR: 1.36, 95% CI: 1.18 to 1.54)" "Home exposure has a higher risk of CVD than non-home exposure". <https://doi.org/10.1093/ntr/ntaf111>
- Notes: Start with the home, because people spend a long time there, and pooling several studies, second-hand smoke at home does more harm than outside. 61% of the health loss from second-hand smoke falls on children. For quitting yourself, see entry 13 (quit smoking, the earlier the better).

### 3. Do not drink sugary drinks, and switching to sugar-free versions does not solve it
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 不喝含糖饮料，换成无糖的也不算解决 | 664ef328 -->
- Cost: Free, and no extra time. Switching to water or unsweetened tea saves money. The hard part is breaking the habit of sweet drinks, sugar-free ones included.
- In plain words: People who drink two or more bottles of sweet drinks a day are about 20% more likely to die in the same period than those who almost never do. One to two bottles a day is also over 10% higher. Switching to sugar-free versions showed no benefit either: people drinking two or more glasses of artificially sweetened drinks a day were actually about a quarter higher.
- Benefit: Two large US studies following groups of people. These two only record, without randomization, about 118,000 people and 36,000 deaths. People drinking 2 or more servings a day had about 21% higher risk of death than those drinking under 1 serving a month (HR 1.21). 1–2 servings a day was about 14% higher (HR 1.14). The European EPIC follow-up study, recording only without randomization, had 450,000 people in 10 countries. Comparing 2 or more glasses a day with under 1 glass a month: sugar-sweetened soft drinks about 8% higher (HR 1.08, 95% CI 1.01–1.16). Artificially sweetened drinks (sugar-free versions) about 26% higher (HR 1.26, 1.16–1.35). All soft drinks combined about 17% higher (HR 1.17, 1.11–1.22).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Malik VS, et al. (2019). Long-Term Consumption of Sugar-Sweetened and Artificially Sweetened Beverages and Risk of Mortality in US Adults. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.118.037401>；Mullee A, Romaguera D, Pearson-Stuttard J, et al. (2019). Association Between Soft Drink Consumption and Mortality in 10 European Countries. JAMA Internal Medicine, 179(11), 1479-1490. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.2478>
- Notes: The dispute concerns sugar-free drinks. The association for artificially sweetened drinks was actually stronger than for sugary ones, which very likely reflects reverse causation: people already overweight or with diabetes switch to sugar-free versions. So do not conclude that sweeteners are more toxic. But it at least shows that "switching to sugar-free solves it" has no evidence behind it. Water or unsweetened tea is safest. Also, these studies only track and record, without randomized trials, and people who drink a lot of sweet drinks have worse lifestyles to begin with, so the figures may be inflated.

### 4. Do not sit for too long at a stretch; get up and move every so often
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 别连着坐太久，隔一阵起身动一动 | ba62799d -->
- Cost: Free, and getting up a few times takes little time. The hard part is losing track of time once you sit down, so set a reminder.
- In plain words: People who sit the most are 2.6 times as likely to die in the same period as those who sit the least. Sitting for a long time without a break adds its own risk. But 60–75 minutes of moderate activity a day basically cancels out the extra risk of sitting.
- Benefit: A US follow-up study, recording only without randomization, measured sitting with accelerometers. People were split into quarters by total daily sitting time. The quarter sitting most had 2.63 times the risk of death of the quarter sitting least (HR 2.63). Split again into quarters by how long each stretch of sitting lasted: the quarter with the longest stretches had 1.96 times the risk of the quarter with the shortest (HR 1.96). So total sitting and the length of each stretch are each independently related. Another analysis pooled studies of over a million people, recording only without randomization. People sitting over 8 hours a day and doing almost no exercise had 1.59 times the risk of death of the other group (HR 1.59). The other group sat under 4 hours a day and was most active. About 60–75 minutes a day of moderate activity can cancel out the extra risk of sitting. Even sitting over 8 hours a day, the most active group's HR was only 1.04, with no statistical difference.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Diaz KM, et al. (2017). Patterns of Sedentary Behavior and Mortality in U.S. Middle-Aged and Older Adults: A National Cohort Study. Annals of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M17-0212>；Ekelund U, et al. (2016). Does physical activity attenuate, or even eliminate, the detrimental association of sitting time with mortality? A harmonised meta-analysis of data from more than 1 million men and women. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)30370-1>
- Notes: The reliable conclusion: how much you move matters more than how long you sit, and with enough exercise the risk of sitting essentially disappears. Note that these studies only track and record, and many of those who sit most are already ill. So "risk up to 2.63 times" clearly includes "being ill first, then sitting a lot". Also, in the same pooled analysis, the risk of watching TV 3 or more hours a day was present at every level of exercise. The most active group only pushed that threshold to 5 hours.

### 5. Drink little or no alcohol
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 少喝或不喝酒 | ce2298cc -->
- Cost: Free, and it saves money and takes no time. The hard part is the slight awkwardness of turning down drinks socially.
- In plain words: Beyond 100 g of pure alcohol a week, the more you drink, the earlier you die. 100 g of pure alcohol is about 2.5 L of beer. People drinking 100–200 g a week at 40 lose about half a year of life. 200–350 g loses one or two years, and over 350 g four or five years. For overall health, the best amount is none.
- Benefit: Pooling 83 prospective studies, recording only without randomization, of 600,000 current drinkers. The lowest point of death risk was at no more than 100 g of pure alcohol a week. That is about 2.5 L of 5% beer, or 300 mL of 40% spirits. People drinking 100–200 g a week at age 40 had about 6 months' lower life expectancy. 200–350 g a week, 1–2 years lower. Over 350 g, 4–5 years lower. These three figures are reported as years of life in the original. The Global Burden of Disease study GBD 2016, combining all kinds of health loss, found the level of drinking that minimizes risk is 0 drinks a week. Another pooled analysis corrected for biases in study design. People drinking 1.3–24 g a day had about 7% lower risk of death than lifelong non-drinkers (RR 0.93). But this showed no statistical difference. 45–64 g a day was about 19% higher (RR 1.19). 65 g or more a day was about 35% higher (RR 1.35).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Wood AM, et al. (2018). Risk thresholds for alcohol consumption: combined analysis of individual-participant data for 599 912 current drinkers in 83 prospective studies. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)30134-X>；GBD 2016 Alcohol Collaborators (2018). Alcohol use and burden for 195 countries and territories, 1990–2016: a systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2016. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31310-2>；Zhao J, et al. (2023). Association Between Daily Alcohol Intake and Risk of All-Cause Mortality: A Systematic Review and Meta-analyses. JAMA Network Open. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.6185>；Di Castelnuovo A, et al. (2006). Alcohol dosing and total mortality in men and women: an updated meta-analysis of 34 prospective studies. Archives of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archinte.166.22.2437> (opposing side)
- Notes: Disputed. The side arguing "a little is good for you" is a pooled analysis of 34 long-term follow-up studies. It reports that light drinkers had up to 17%–18% lower total mortality. The amount given is up to 4 drinks a day for men and 2 for women. Within this amount, people who drank more had lower mortality. The opposing side argues that the "non-drinker" group includes people who quit because of illness and people already in poor health. One pooled analysis specifically removed these confounders, after which the protective effect vanished. The Global Burden of Disease study also gives 0 as safest. The safe reading: drinking a little does not clearly shorten life, but drinking a lot certainly does. There is no basis for "starting to drink for your health". Daily drinkers must not quit abruptly on their own; see entry 21 (do not stop on your own).

### 6. Eat less processed meat (ham, bacon, sausages, luncheon meat)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 少吃加工肉（火腿、培根、香肠、午餐肉） | 30a26d52 -->
- Cost: Free, possibly saving money, and no extra time. The hard part is eating less of something you like.
- In plain words: People who eat the most ham, bacon and sausages are about 20% more likely to die in the same period than those who eat the least. Each extra serving of processed meat a day also raises the chance by about 20%.
- Benefit: Pooling several follow-up studies (recording only, without randomization): the group eating the most processed meat had about 23% higher risk of death than the group eating the least (RR 1.23). Red meat in total about 29% higher (RR 1.29). Unprocessed red meat about 10% higher (RR 1.10), but this showed no statistical difference. Another pooled analysis calculated by daily amount. Each extra serving of processed meat a day raised the risk of death by about 23% (RR 1.23). Each extra serving of red meat a day by about 10% (RR 1.10).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Larsson SC, Orsini N (2014). Red meat and processed meat consumption and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis. American Journal of Epidemiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwt261>；Schwingshackl L, et al. (2017). Food groups and risk of all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. <https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.117.153148>；Johnston BC, et al. (2019). Unprocessed Red Meat and Processed Meat Consumption: Dietary Guideline Recommendations From the NutriRECS Consortium. Annals of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M19-1621> (the opposing NutriRECS guideline)
- Notes: Disputed. The NutriRECS guideline used a standard evidence grading method (GRADE) and rated these studies as "not very reliable". It gave only a weak recommendation: carry on eating as you are. The two sides dispute whether the evidence is solid enough, not the direction. No study says processed meat is beneficial. Unprocessed red meat has a small effect with no statistical difference. What to cut back is processed meat.

### 7. Add up short bursts of vigorous activity, such as climbing stairs or walking briskly to catch something, to four or five minutes a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 把爬楼梯、快走赶路这类零碎的用力活动攒到每天四五分钟 | 16493baf -->
- Cost: Free, with no need to find extra time for exercise. The hard part is remembering to take the stairs and walk faster rather than taking the easy way.
- In plain words: People who never exercise, if they have three one- or two-minute bursts of vigorous activity a day, are about 40% less likely to die in the same period than those with none. Cardiovascular death is nearly halved. Vigorous activity means climbing stairs, walking briskly to catch something and the like.
- Benefit: A UK Biobank study of 25,200 people who did no deliberate exercise. Followed for 6.9 years on average, 852 died. Some had 3 bursts of vigorous activity a day, 1–2 minutes each. Compared with those who had none, their risk of death and of cancer death fell by 38%–40%. Risk of cardiovascular death fell by 48%–49%. People accumulating 4.4 minutes of such activity a day had 26%–30% lower risk of death and cancer death. Risk of cardiovascular death fell by 32%–34%
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Stamatakis E, Ahmadi MN, Gill JMR, et al. (2022). Association of wearable device-measured vigorous intermittent lifestyle physical activity with mortality. Nature Medicine, 28, 2521-2529. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-022-02100-x>
- Notes: Disputed. This study measured activity with wearable devices, more reliable than asking people to recall it on questionnaires. But it still only tracked and recorded, without randomized trials, and followed people for only 6.9 years. The participants did no exercise at all, so this entry does not apply to people who already exercise regularly

### 8. Eat a small handful of nuts every day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 每天吃一小把坚果 | 58e8960c -->
- Cost: 28 grams a day costs little. Easy to eat on the side, taking no time.
- In plain words: People who often eat nuts are about 20% less likely to die in the same period than those who do not. Even once a week shows a difference, about 10% lower. The difference is clearest at a handful a day.
- Benefit: Two US follow-up studies, 119,000 people and over 3 million person-years. Person-years are the number of people times the years each was followed. Compared with people who ate no nuts: eating them less than once a week, about 7% lower risk of death (hazard ratio 0.93, 95% CI 0.90–0.96, the confidence range). Once a week, about 11% lower (0.89, 0.86–0.93). 2–4 times a week, about 13% lower (0.87, 0.83–0.90). 5–6 times a week, about 15% lower (0.85, 0.79–0.91). 7 or more times a week, about 20% lower (0.80, 0.73–0.86)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Bao Y, Han J, Hu FB, et al. (2013). Association of nut consumption with total and cause-specific mortality. New England Journal of Medicine, 369(21), 2001-2011. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1307352>
- Notes: Buy plain, unsalted nuts. Nuts are high in calories, so do not eat them freely as snacks. Note that these studies only follow a group of people over time, and nut eaters have better lifestyles overall, so discount the figures somewhat

### 9. Drink three to four cups of coffee a day, without sugar or cream topping
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 每天喝三到四杯咖啡，不加糖不加奶盖 | 4f40ff2f -->
- Cost: Making it yourself costs little. A few minutes per cup, taking hardly any time.
- In plain words: People who drink three to four cups of coffee a day are about 17% less likely to die in the same period than those who do not.
- Benefit: A review pooled the results of 201 meta-analyses, whose underlying studies only recorded without randomization. Result: people drinking 3–4 cups of coffee a day had 17% lower risk of death than those who drank none (relative risk 0.83, 95% CI 0.79–0.88, the confidence range)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Poole R, Kennedy OJ, Roderick P, et al. (2017). Coffee consumption and health: umbrella review of meta-analyses of multiple health outcomes. BMJ, 359, j5024. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.j5024>
- Notes: The dispute: the original authors state that the evidence comes almost entirely from follow-up records, and that "robust randomised controlled trials are needed to understand whether the observed associations are causal". That is, people must be randomly split into two groups to compare; follow-up records alone cannot settle cause. Drink it black; sugar and cream toppings cancel out the benefit. Pregnant women, and people with heart rhythm problems, anxiety or insomnia, are not covered by this entry

### 10. Let hot things cool before drinking; do not drink scalding tea, soup or coffee
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 热的东西放一放再喝，不喝滚烫的茶、汤和咖啡 | 9106308a -->
- Cost: Free; wait two or three more minutes each time. The hard part is holding back when you are eager to drink.
- In plain words: Drinking very hot tea makes oesophageal cancer 8 times as likely as drinking warm tea. Even just "hot" is 2 times. Drinking it less than two minutes after pouring carries 5 times the risk of waiting four or more minutes.
- Benefit: In a high-incidence area for oesophageal cancer in northern Iran, 300 patients were compared with 571 people without the disease. The following are all compared with people drinking warm tea. Those drinking "hot" tea had about 2.07 times the odds of oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma (odds ratio, 95% CI 1.28–3.35, confidence range). "Very hot", about 8.16 times (3.93–16.9). Those drinking within 2 minutes of pouring had about 5.41 times the odds of those waiting 4 or more minutes (2.63–11.1)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Islami F, Pourshams A, Nasrollahzadeh D, et al. (2009). Tea drinking habits and oesophageal cancer in a high risk area in northern Iran: population based case-control study. BMJ, 338, b929. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.b929>；Loomis D, Guyton KZ, Grosse Y, et al. (2016). Carcinogenicity of drinking coffee, mate, and very hot beverages. Lancet Oncology, 17(7), 877-878. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(16)30239-X>
- Notes: The International Agency for Research on Cancer classifies hot drinks above 65 °C as Group 2A, meaning probably carcinogenic. The same evaluation found coffee itself not carcinogenic. The high incidence of oesophageal cancer in China's Chaoshan and Taihang Mountains areas is linked to the habit of drinking things scalding hot

### 11. Have one or two servings of milk or yoghurt a day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 每天喝一两份奶或酸奶 | e710c64c -->
- Cost: Inexpensive, and no extra time.
- In plain words: People who have two or more servings of milk or yoghurt a day are about 17% less likely to die in the same period than those who have none.
- Benefit: The PURE follow-up study covered 136,000 people in 21 countries, followed for 9.1 years on average, with 6,796 deaths. People with two or more servings of dairy a day had about 17% lower risk of death than those with none (hazard ratio 0.83, 95% CI 0.72–0.96, confidence range), P for trend=0.0052
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Dehghan M, Mente A, Rangarajan S, et al. (2018). Association of dairy intake with cardiovascular disease and mortality in 21 countries from five continents (PURE): a prospective cohort study. Lancet, 392(10161), 2288-2297. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31812-9>
- Notes: The dispute: PURE included many low- and middle-income countries, where being able to drink milk every day itself indicates a well-off household, a confounder hard to remove completely. People who are lactose intolerant can switch to yoghurt or low-lactose milk instead of forcing themselves to drink milk

### 12. Turn on the range hood when stir-frying or frying, from lighting the stove until you finish
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 炒菜、煎炸时打开抽油烟机，从开火一直开到做完 | 2139f7b0 -->
- Cost: Just a little more on electricity. In Japan the range hood is called 換気扇 or レンジフード. The hard part is remembering to turn it on every time, and not switching it off because it is noisy.
- In plain words: Among Chinese women who do not smoke, those who never or rarely use a range hood when cooking have about twice the risk of lung cancer of those who use it often. Having lived in a home with a poorly ventilated kitchen also raises the risk by about 50%, most clearly in households burning coal.
- Benefit: Pooling 10 case-control studies, all of non-smoking Chinese women. Case-control means comparing lung cancer patients with people without the disease, asking about past habits. People exposed to cooking oil fumes had about 1.74 times the odds of lung cancer (odds ratio, 95% CI 1.57–1.94, confidence range). 4 of these compared people exposed to fumes without using a range hood, about 2.11 times (1.54–2.89). A Taiwanese study compared 1,302 non-smoking women with lung cancer and 1,302 without. Looking only at those who had cooked for ten years or more. Those using a range hood for over two-thirds of those years, compared with under a third, had about half the risk (odds ratio 0.49, 0.32–0.76). A prospective study in Shanghai followed 71,000 non-smoking women from 1996 to 2009, finding 429 cases of lung cancer. Those who reported having lived in homes with poorly ventilated kitchens had about 49% higher risk of lung cancer (HR 1.49, 1.15–1.95). Burning coal with poor ventilation for 20 years or more, about 2.03 times (HR 2.03, 1.35–3.05). Looking only at whether coal had been burned, no difference was seen (HR 1.03, 0.84–1.26). The type of cooking oil also made no difference.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Xue Y, Jiang Y, Jin S, Li Y (2016). Association between cooking oil fume exposure and lung cancer among Chinese nonsmoking women: a meta-analysis. OncoTargets and Therapy, 9, 2987-2992. <https://doi.org/10.2147/OTT.S100949>；Chen TY, Fang YH, Chen HL, et al. (2020). Impact of cooking oil fume exposure and fume extractor use on lung cancer risk in non-smoking Han Chinese women. Scientific Reports, 10, 6774. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-63656-7>；Kim C, Gao YT, Xiang YB, et al. (2015). Home kitchen ventilation, cooking fuels, and lung cancer risk in a prospective cohort of never smoking women in Shanghai, China. International Journal of Cancer, 136(3), 632-638. <https://doi.org/10.1002/ijc.29020>
- Notes: Disputed: the figures supporting range hoods mainly come from case-control studies. Patients recalling the past after diagnosis tend to under-report range hood use, so the figures may be inflated. In the Shanghai prospective study, the raised risk was mainly in households burning coal with poor ventilation. That study cannot give a separate figure for how much difference using a hood makes in households using only gas. So the evidence is graded only B, and the benefit is rated large based on a roughly doubled risk. The participants were Chinese women, and households doing a lot of high-heat stir-frying and frying should follow this most. The beneficiaries of this entry are mainly whoever usually cooks at home.

### 13. Quit smoking, the earlier the better
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 戒烟，越早越好 | f3e443d5 -->
- Cost: Free, and it actually saves money. The money you spend on cigarettes each day stays with you once you quit. The hard part is getting through withdrawal, usually a few weeks to a few months.
- In plain words: Smokers live on average more than ten years less than non-smokers. Quitting before 40 wipes out about 90% of the extra risk of death from continuing to smoke. The earlier you quit, the more you win back.
- Benefit: A US study following a group of people (recording only, without randomization) found: current smokers have a life expectancy more than 10 years shorter than never-smokers. Quitting before 40 removes about 90% of the excess risk of death from continuing to smoke. Quitting at 25–34 gains about 10 years, at 35–44 about 9 years, at 45–54 about 6 years (reported as years of life in the original). A similar Chinese study: in the 2010s, urban male smokers had 1.65 times the risk of death of non-smokers (RR 1.65), and rural males 1.22 times. After 10 years of having quit voluntarily, the risk attributable to smoking nearly disappeared.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Jha P, et al. (2013). 21st-century hazards of smoking and benefits of cessation in the United States. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsa1211128>；Chen Z, et al. (2015). Contrasting male and female trends in tobacco-attributed mortality in China: evidence from successive nationwide prospective cohort studies. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)00340-2>；Oberg M, et al. (2011). Worldwide burden of disease from exposure to second-hand smoke: a retrospective analysis of data from 192 countries. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61388-8> (the two second-hand smoke figures)
- Notes: Breathing others' smoke also kills: about 603,000 people worldwide died from second-hand smoke in 2004, about 1% of all deaths that year. Even if you do not smoke, avoid other people's smoke, and especially do not let children breathe it. For how to quit, see entry 14 (smoking cessation medicines).

### 14. Do not try to quit smoking on willpower alone; get medication first, and your chance of success more than doubles

- Cost: The medicine costs roughly what you save on cigarettes during treatment. For which nicotine replacement products are available at Japanese pharmacies and whether prescription medicine can be prescribed, ask a pharmacist or doctor.
- In plain words: Most people cannot quit by willpower alone. People taking varenicline succeed more than twice as often as those on placebo. Those using nicotine replacement such as patches or gum succeed about 50% more often than those using no medicine. Using patches and gum together raises it by over 20% more.
- Benefit: Pooling 41 randomized trials with 17,395 people, the quit rate in the varenicline group was 2.32 times that of the placebo group (RR 2.32, 95% CI 2.15–2.51, the confidence range; high-certainty evidence). Varenicline also beat bupropion (RR 1.36, about 36% higher) and a single form of nicotine replacement (RR 1.25, about 25% higher). Nicotine replacement versus no medicine: 133 trials with 64,640 people, success about 55% higher (RR 1.55, 95% CI 1.49–1.61). Patches plus a fast-acting form (gum, lozenges) were about 27% higher than a single form (RR 1.27, 95% CI 1.17–1.37, 16 trials with 12,169 people, high-certainty evidence).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Livingstone-Banks J, Fanshawe TR, Thomas KH, et al. (2023). Nicotine receptor partial agonists for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 5, CD006103. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD006103.pub8>；Hartmann-Boyce J, Chepkin SC, Ye W, Bullen C, Lancaster T (2018). Different doses, durations and modes of delivery of nicotine replacement therapy for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 6, CD013308. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013308.pub2>
- Notes: Common reactions to varenicline are nausea, vivid dreams and poor sleep; people with a psychiatric history should tell their doctor. Nicotine replacement courses generally last 8 to 12 weeks; do not stop suddenly on your own, but taper as your doctor says. Medicine only deals with the discomfort of the withdrawal weeks, not with the situations where you "feel like a smoke".

### 15. If you have high blood pressure or high cholesterol, take your medicine regularly as prescribed, and do not stop on your own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 有高血压、高血脂就按医嘱规律吃药，别自行停 | 72438046 -->
- Cost: Most blood pressure medicines and statins are inexpensive. Once a day takes little time. The hard part is keeping it up indefinitely, not stopping when you feel fine.
- In plain words: Each 10 mmHg drop in blood pressure lowers the chance of death by about 13% and stroke by about 27%. A statin lowering LDL cholesterol by 1 unit lowers the chance of death by about 10%. People who take their medicine on time have 30% to 45% lower chance of death than those who often miss doses.
- Benefit: Pooling several randomized trials: each 10 mmHg drop in systolic blood pressure lowered the risk of death by about 13% (RR 0.87). Systolic is the higher of the two blood pressure numbers. Major cardiovascular events about 20% lower (RR 0.80). Stroke about 27% lower (RR 0.73). Heart failure about 28% lower (RR 0.72). Statins lower low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL). Each 1.0 mmol/L drop in LDL lowered the risk of death by about 10% (RR 0.90). The same drop lowered major vascular events by about 22% (RR 0.78). Separately, pooling several follow-up studies, recording only without randomization. People taking ≥80% of prescribed doses had lower risk of death than those often missing doses. For statins about 45% lower (RR 0.55). For blood pressure medicine about 29% lower (RR 0.71).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ettehad D, et al. (2016). Blood pressure lowering for prevention of cardiovascular disease and death: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)01225-8>；Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' (CTT) Collaboration (2010). Efficacy and safety of more intensive lowering of LDL cholesterol: a meta-analysis of data from 170 000 participants in 26 randomised trials. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61350-5>；Chowdhury R, et al. (2013). Adherence to cardiovascular therapy: a meta-analysis of prevalence and clinical consequences. European Heart Journal. <https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/eht295>
- Notes: Only people a doctor judges should take medicine should follow this; healthy people do not need to. The "sticking to medicine" figures come from follow-up records, and people who stick to their medicine are more disciplined in general, which inflates the benefit. The two sets of randomized trial figures above are more reliable. This section has no separate entry on controlling blood sugar: the evidence that pushing blood sugar lower benefits total mortality is not as consistent as for blood pressure and cholesterol.

### 16. Swap some white rice and white flour for whole grains

- Cost: Brown rice, oats and wholemeal noodles cost a bit more than white rice and white flour, with no extra time. The hard part is gradually getting used to the texture.
- In plain words: People eating 90 g more whole grains a day are about 17% less likely to die in the same period. 90 g is about three servings. At around 200 g a day, it is still going down.
- Benefit: Pooling several follow-up studies (recording only, without randomization): each extra 90 g of whole grains a day lowered the risk of death by about 17% (RR 0.83). 90 g is about 3 servings. The benefit continued up to 210–225 g a day. In another pooled analysis, each extra serving a day was about 8% lower (RR 0.92).

- Sources: Aune D, et al. (2016). ### 17. If you can, take baths rather than only showers

<!-- zh: 有条件就泡澡，别只冲淋浴 | 290069ca -->
- Cost: A bit more on water and electricity. Half an hour each time.
- In plain words: People who take a bath almost every day have about 28% lower risk of cardiovascular events than those who bathe fewer than twice a week, and about 46% lower risk of brain haemorrhage.
- Benefit: A Japanese study of 30,000 people followed for 19 years, with 2,097 cardiovascular events. People bathing almost every day had about 28% lower risk of cardiovascular events than those bathing no more than twice a week (hazard ratio 0.72, 95% CI 0.62–0.84, the confidence range). Coronary heart disease about 35% lower (0.65, 0.45–0.94). Stroke about 26% lower (0.74, 0.62–0.87). Brain haemorrhage about 46% lower (0.54, 0.40–0.73)

- Sources: Ukai T, Iso H, Yamagishi K, et al. (2020). Habitual tub bathing and risks of incident coronary heart disease and stroke. Heart, 106(10), 732-737. <https://doi.org/10.1136/heartjnl-2019-315752>
- Notes: The dispute: this result comes from a Japanese population, and whether it applies to people without bathtubs who habitually shower is unknown. Also, the water should not be too hot, and do not soak too long. ### 18. Go out in daylight during the day; do not go all day without seeing the sun
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 白天出门晒晒太阳，别整天不见光 | 43372af2 -->
- Cost: Free. Ten-odd minutes a day; your commute and lunch break are enough.
- In plain words: Women who deliberately avoid the sun have a life expectancy 0.6 to 2.1 years shorter than those who get the most sun. Non-smokers who avoid the sun live about as long as smokers who get the most sun.
- Benefit: A Swedish study of 29,500 women followed for 20 years. Compared with the group getting the most sun, those deliberately avoiding the sun had a life expectancy 0.6 to 2.1 years shorter. The authors wrote that "non-smokers who avoided sun exposure had a life expectancy similar to smokers in the highest sun exposure group"
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Lindqvist PG, Epstein E, Nielsen K, et al. (2016). Avoidance of sun exposure as a risk factor for major causes of death: a competing risk analysis of the Melanoma in Southern Sweden cohort. Journal of Internal Medicine, 280(4), 375-387. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.12496>
- Notes: The dispute: Sweden is at high latitude with little sunshine, so the conclusion cannot be applied directly to sunnier places. And people who avoid the sun may already be frail and stay indoors. Also, do not get sunburned; shade yourself from strong midday sun, because sun exposure raises skin cancer risk

### 19. Keep naps to half an hour and no more than an hour; if you need one or two hours to get through the day, find out why
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 把午睡控制在半小时内，别超过一小时，非得睡一两小时才撑得住就去查原因 | 6d2da52c -->
- Cost: Free. Just set an alarm before sleeping; almost no willpower needed.
- In plain words: People who nap for no more than an hour have no raised risk of death or chronic disease, and are mentally sharper. Those napping over an hour have about 30% higher risk of coronary heart disease and about 20% higher risk of diabetes and obesity. So set an alarm to wake yourself after half an hour. If you need one or two hours every day to get by, that is a signal to get checked.
- Benefit: An umbrella review pooled 16 meta-analyses covering 244 health outcomes. An umbrella review pools existing pooled studies once more. The conclusion: people napping under 60 minutes had no raised total mortality or chronic disease risk, and the clearest improvement in mental performance (SMD 0.69, 95% CI 0.37–1.00, the confidence range). Short naps of 20 to 30 minutes most improved physical performance (SMD 0.99, 0.67–1.31). People napping over 60 minutes had about 30% higher coronary heart disease risk and about 20% higher diabetes and obesity risk. Another study had 1,338 older people aged over 56 wear wrist devices to measure naps, rather than relying on questionnaires. Followed for up to 19 years, 926 died. Each extra hour of napping raised the risk of death by about 13% (HR 1.13, 95% CI 1.04–1.23). Each extra nap a day, about 7% higher (HR 1.07, 1.02–1.13). Napping in the morning was about 30% higher than in the early afternoon (HR 1.30, 1.03–1.64).
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Du P, Li J, Hua Z, et al. (2026). Multiple Health Outcomes of Daytime Napping: A Comprehensive Umbrella Review. Public Health Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.3389/phrs.2026.1609013>；Gao C, Cai R, Zheng X, et al. (2026). Objectively Measured Daytime Napping Patterns and All-Cause Mortality in Older Adults. JAMA Network Open. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.7938>；Dashti HS, et al. (2021). Genetic determinants of daytime napping and effects on cardiometabolic health. Nature Communications. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-20585-3>
- Notes: Disputed. Both studies above only record, without randomization. People who nap for long are already more likely to have sleep apnoea, anaemia, an underactive thyroid, depression or other chronic illness. So how much of "long nappers die more" is caused by napping itself is unclear. Genetic causal inference (Mendelian randomization, 453,000 plus 541,000 people) supports only one thing: more frequent napping slightly raises blood pressure and waist circumference. It found no causal effect of napping on coronary heart disease or diabetes. So do not treat "napping less" as a way to lower mortality. The "find out why" half is the author's advice, grade C. If you are so sleepy in the day you must sleep long, check your night-time sleep first. Snoring and stopping breathing in your sleep need checking for sleep apnoea, then a full blood count and thyroid test. For how short naps refresh you, see Section 24, entry 8 (if you get sleepy in the afternoon, nap for 10 minutes). For how long to sleep at night, see entry 33 of this section (sleep about 7 hours a night).

### 20. After staying up late, catch up on sleep the very next night rather than saving it for the weekend
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 熬夜之后第二天晚上就补觉，别攒到周末 | 15b005ef -->
- Cost: Free. The extra hour or two of sleep is something you want anyway.
- In plain words: People who did not catch up the night after a short night had about 15% higher risk of death than those with regular sleep, and about 42% higher if they lost a lot and did not catch up. Those who caught up showed no raised risk. So after a late night, go to bed earlier the next night.
- Benefit: 85,618 people in the UK Biobank, mean age 61.8, wore wrist devices recording sleep day by day, with a median follow-up of 8 years. The study first calculated each person's own sleep need to define "short nights", meaning 2.5 or more hours less than needed. The first night after a short spell counted as a "rebound night", and sleeping more than needed that night counted as catching up. People were split into five patterns on this basis. Compared with people with regular sleep, those who lost sleep and did not catch up had about 15% higher all-cause mortality (HR 1.15, 95% CI 1.01–1.31, the confidence range). Those who lost over 3.5 hours in total and did not catch up were about 42% higher (HR 1.42, 1.24–1.63). The two groups that caught up showed no significant association, with loss followed by catching up at HR 1.12 (0.98–1.28). Among people who habitually slept short, losing sleep without catching up was about 19% higher (1.19, 1.01–1.40), and severe loss without catching up about 38% higher (1.38, 1.17–1.63). This result was replicated in another group of 4,586 people from a US health survey.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Li X, Zhang M, Li Z, et al. (2026). Acute sleep rebound following sleep restriction is associated with reduced mortality risk. Nature Communications. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-72461-1>
- Notes: This study only records, without randomization. People who cannot catch up may be busier or in poorer health to begin with, which cannot be fully removed. "The two catch-up groups showed no raised risk" only means no difference was detected; their point estimates were still above 1, so catching up does not mean all is well. So pay back lost sleep as soon as possible, and do not take this as licence to stay up. Catching up in this study means sleeping a bit more the first night after a short spell, not saving it all for the weekend. Making "stay up on weekdays, catch up at weekends" a fixed weekly rhythm is called social jet lag, which is itself linked to cardiovascular disease; see entry 33 of this section (sleep about 7 hours a night, on a fixed schedule). The entry on getting up at a fixed time on weekends too is Section 24, entry 13 (get up at a fixed time, weekends included), which does not conflict with this one. This entry asks you to go to bed earlier that night, not to sleep until noon the next day.

### 21. If you drink every day and get shaky and anxious when you stop, do not quit abruptly on your own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 每天都喝酒、一停就手抖心慌的人，别自己硬戒 | bb67941d -->
- Cost: One visit to psychiatry or a specialist clinic for alcohol dependence. Severe cases need a few days in hospital.
- In plain words: People who drink heavily every day and suddenly stop get withdrawal reactions. Mild ones are shaking, sweating, palpitations and insomnia; severe ones are seizures or delirium tremens, a medical emergency that can kill. Norway followed over thirty thousand people, and among those who had had delirium tremens, 8% died each year afterwards. If you want to quit, go to hospital first and say how much you drink each day.
- Benefit: A 2026 clinical review states: people with long-term heavy drinking who suddenly stop or sharply cut down develop withdrawal syndrome, with tremor, autonomic hyperactivity, anxiety, insomnia, perceptual disturbances, seizures and delirium tremens, which in general internal medicine is "common and potentially life-threatening". Norway's national register cohort included 36,287 people diagnosed with alcohol dependence, alcohol withdrawal state or delirium tremens from 2009 to 2015: annual mortality was 8.0% in the delirium tremens group, 5.0% in the withdrawal state group and 3.6% in the alcohol dependence group. The standardized mortality ratio for the delirium tremens group was 9.8 (95% CI 8.9–10.7, the confidence range), meaning about 9.8 times the risk of death of the general population of the same age.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Caputo F, Lungaro L, Costanzini A, De Giorgio R, Addolorato G (2026). Alcohol withdrawal syndrome in hospitalized patients: a practical review. European Journal of Internal Medicine, 107103. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2026.107103>；Bramness JG, Heiberg IH, Høye A, Rossow I (2023). Mortality and alcohol-related morbidity in patients with delirium tremens, alcohol withdrawal state or alcohol dependence in Norway: A register-based prospective cohort study. Addiction, 118(12), 2352–2359. <https://doi.org/10.1111/add.16297>
- Notes: How to judge for yourself: if you drink almost every day, get shaky, sweaty, anxious and sleepless after a day without, or need a drink in the morning to feel right, any one of these means do not stop on your own. Hospitals routinely use benzodiazepines to get you through these days, and give thiamine (vitamin B1). Graded B because the Norwegian cohort describes "what happened later to people who had delirium tremens", not "what happens if you quit abruptly on your own". The link between the two is an inference. You should still quit, just do it in hospital. For how much a week counts as a lot, see entry 5 (drink little or no alcohol).

### 22. Walk 7,000–8,000 steps a day, or accumulate 150–300 minutes of brisk walking a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 每天走到 7000–8000 步，或者每周累计快走 150–300 分钟 | 21d0f914 -->
- Cost: Free. By steps, 60–90 minutes of walking a day, which can be split into commuting and shopping. By exercise time, 20–45 minutes a day. The hard part is keeping it up long-term.
- In plain words: People walking about 7,800 steps a day are about 45% less likely to die in the same period than those walking only 3,500. The same holds by time: 150–300 minutes of brisk walking a week is about 30% lower than no exercise. Beyond a certain amount it stops falling; for people over 60, 6,000–8,000 steps is enough. Meeting either measure is enough.
- Benefit: Pooling 15 studies following groups of people, recording only without randomization. These 15 had 47,471 people and 3,013 deaths. People were split into four bands by daily steps, with the lowest band's median at 3,553 steps a day. The other three bands' medians were 5,801, 7,842 and 10,901 steps a day. These three bands had about 40%, 45% and 53% lower risk of death than the lowest band (HR 0.60, 0.55, 0.47). For people over 60, the benefit levelled off beyond 6,000–8,000 steps. For people under 60, beyond 8,000–10,000 steps. Another analysis pooled several studies: benefit started from about 3,867 steps a day. After that, each extra 1,000 steps a day lowered the risk of death by about 15%. By exercise time: pooling several follow-up studies (recording only, without randomization), people doing 1–2 times the guideline minimum had about 31% lower risk of death than those doing none (HR 0.69). That amount is 7.5–15 MET·h a week, roughly 150–300 minutes of brisk walking a week. People below the minimum were also about 20% lower (HR 0.80). People doing 3–5 times the minimum were about 39% lower (HR 0.61), the peak reduction. Adding more did not lower it further, but did no harm either (HR 0.69 at 10 times or more). Another pooled study measuring with accelerometers: the quarter of people with the most moderate-to-vigorous activity had about 48% lower risk of death than the quarter with the least (HR 0.52). An accelerometer is a device worn on the body to measure activity.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Paluch AE, et al. (2022). Daily steps and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis of 15 international cohorts. Lancet Public Health. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(21)00302-9>；Banach M, et al. (2023). The association between daily step count and all-cause and cardiovascular mortality: a meta-analysis. European Journal of Preventive Cardiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwad229>；Arem H, et al. (2015). Leisure time physical activity and mortality: a detailed pooled analysis of the dose-response relationship. JAMA Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2015.0533>；Ekelund U, et al. (2019). Dose-response associations between accelerometry measured physical activity and sedentary time and all cause mortality: systematic review and harmonised meta-analysis. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l4570>
- Notes: Steps and exercise time are the same thing counted differently; meeting either is enough. Going from 4,000 to 7,000 steps is the best value; there is no need to push for 10,000. Note these studies only track and record: among those who walk and move least are people already ill and unable to walk. They move less because they are failing, not die early because they move less. So the calculated gaps are often inflated. The studies measuring activity with devices had short follow-up and older participants, and the real benefit is not as large as "risk down to 0.52 times". But the trend that more movement is better is clear.

### 24. Eat fewer ultra-processed foods (crisps, instant noodles, pastries, ready meals)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 少吃超加工食品（薯片、方便面、糕点、速食） | e6645d81 -->
- Cost: Free; you just buy different things. You need to cook for yourself or spend time choosing minimally processed ingredients, taking some time every day. The hard part is resisting "convenience is fine" over the long run.
- In plain words: People eating the most ultra-processed food are about 20% more likely to die in the same period than those eating the least, and 50% more likely to die of cardiovascular disease. But this evidence is rated as not very certain, so discount the figures.

- Evidence: A

### 25. Keep your BMI at 20–25, and lose weight if you are overweight
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 把 BMI 控制在 20–25，超重就减 | 9ac82dc3 -->
- Cost: Free. Diet and exercise take time every day. This is the entry in the section that takes the most willpower: long-term control of eating and getting moving, and keeping weight off is harder than losing it.
- In plain words: The chance of death is lowest at a BMI of 20–25. At 27.5–30 it is about 20% higher, at 30–35 about 45% higher, and at 35–40 nearly double. East Asians are more sensitive to excess weight: each 5 points of BMI raises the risk by about 40%.
- Benefit: Pooling individual data from 239 prospective studies (recording only, without randomization). This analysis kept only three kinds of people: never-smokers, those without chronic disease at enrolment, and those who survived 5 years after enrolment. Result: the risk of death was lowest at BMI 20–25. BMI 25–27.5 about 7% higher (HR 1.07). 27.5–30 about 20% higher (HR 1.20). 30–35 about 45% higher (HR 1.45). 35–40 about 94% higher (HR 1.94). 40–60 was 2.76 times (HR 2.76). For people with BMI over 25, each extra 5 kg/m² raised the risk in East Asian populations by about 39% (HR 1.39).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Global BMI Mortality Collaboration (2016). Body-mass index and all-cause mortality: individual-participant-data meta-analysis of 239 prospective studies in four continents. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)30175-1>；Flegal KM, et al. (2013). Association of all-cause mortality with overweight and obesity using standard body mass index categories: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2012.113905> (opposing side)
- Notes: Disputed. One side argues "being a bit heavier means living longer". Their pooled analysis found overweight people had about 6% lower risk of death, and mildly obese people about 5% lower. The disagreement is mainly over whether to exclude three kinds of data: smokers, people already ill, and the first few years after enrolment. They need excluding because seriously ill people lose weight first. The analysis pooling 239 studies excluded these three, and the risk of overweight reappeared. Note also that no randomized trial has shown weight loss itself lowers total mortality. The benefits above come from comparing people of different weights side by side, and do not mean losing weight lowers risk by that much. People wanting to lose weight need not focus on meal timing; neither breakfast nor 16:8 intermittent fasting gives extra benefit; see Section 22, entry 25 (do not count on breakfast or 16:8 to control your weight)

### 26. The longer you work night shifts, the higher your cardiovascular risk; if you can move to another role, do it early
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 上夜班干得越久心血管风险越高，能转岗就趁早转 | f60b5be3 -->
- Cost: Changing role or job may mean less pay, and losing the night-shift allowance. Job hunting takes time, and making the decision is hard. If you factor this in when choosing a job, the cost is zero.
- In plain words: Night-shift workers have about 13% more cardiovascular disease than those who do not work nights, and about 27% more cardiovascular deaths. And it grows the longer you do it: each extra 5 years of night shifts adds about 7% more cardiovascular disease. So this account is counted in years, and if you can move to another role, do it early. As for night shifts causing cancer, the evidence is much softer than widely claimed.
- Benefit: Pooling 23 follow-up studies, recording only without randomization. Night-shift workers had about 13% higher risk of cardiovascular events (RR 1.13, 95% CI 1.10–1.16, the confidence range). Cardiovascular death about 27% higher (RR 1.27, 1.18–1.36). By years: each extra 5 years of night shifts raised cardiovascular disease incidence by about 7% (RR 1.07, 1.04–1.09) and cardiovascular death by about 5% (RR 1.05, 1.03–1.06). By condition: coronary heart disease incidence about 22% higher (1.22, 1.16–1.28), coronary heart disease death about 22% higher (1.22, 1.10–1.36), ischaemic heart disease death about 39% higher (1.39, 1.06–1.84), stroke death about 49% higher (1.49, 1.04–2.12). Stroke incidence was not significant (1.06, 0.95–1.18).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Xi J, Ma W, Tao Y, et al. (2025). Association between night shift work and cardiovascular disease: a systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis. Frontiers in Public Health. <https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1668848>；Esposito G, Bravi F, Santucci C, et al. (2025). Night shift work and breast cancer risk in healthcare workers: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Occupational Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1093/occmed/kqaf040>；Shen QM, Li ZY, Tan YT, et al. (2026). Night shift work and risk of total and site-specific cancer: results from a prospective cohort study among Chinese men. Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health. <https://doi.org/10.5271/sjweh.4290>；Czeisler CA, Johnson MP, Duffy JF, et al. (1990). Exposure to bright light and darkness to treat physiologic maladaptation to night work. New England Journal of Medicine, 322(18), 1253-1259. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199005033221801>
- Notes: These are all studies that only record, without randomization. Night-shift workers already differ from others in smoking, weight and income, which cannot be fully adjusted for. On night shifts causing cancer, the evidence is much softer than widely claimed. In an analysis pooling 12 studies with 12,132 breast cancer cases, people who had worked nights had only about 5% higher risk. The confidence range crossed 1 (RR 1.05, 0.96–1.14). The 20-years-or-more band was about 25% higher (1.25, 1.01–1.55), but became non-significant after the authors' influence analysis. After correcting for possible publication bias it was close to 1, and in the authors' words the association is "far from established". Another study following Chinese men for 16.1 years with 8,202 cancer cases found night shifts unrelated to total cancer and most common cancers. Only pancreatic cancer was about 59% higher in the band with 11 to 20 cumulative years (HR 1.59, 1.09–2.31). So this entry is accounted on cardiovascular risk, not cancer. Not everyone should leave night work; you have to do this account by years yourself. If you are already doing it, change what you can first. For quitting smoking, see entry 13 of this section (quit smoking, the earlier the better); for blood pressure and cholesterol, entry 15 of this section (take your medicine regularly as prescribed); for how to catch up on sleep after night shifts, entry 20 of this section (catch up on sleep the very next night). There are two more measures to try: concentrating eating in the daytime, and shifting the body clock with bright light plus strict daytime darkness. Both only have endpoints in metabolic measures and sleep, and no trial has tested whether they lower cardiovascular risk. For how to do them, doses and original figures, see the long-form article [body clocks and night shifts](../docs/生物钟和夜班.md) (in Chinese).

### 27. Replace the table salt at home with a low-sodium (potassium) salt substitute
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 把家里的食盐换成低钠盐（钾盐） | 6489b8d5 -->
- Cost: A little dearer than ordinary salt. Switch when you next buy it, with no extra time. The taste is almost unchanged.
- In plain words: People who have had a stroke, or are over 60 with high blood pressure, who switch to low-sodium salt at home are about 12% less likely to die within five years. Stroke is about 14% lower.
- Benefit: A trial randomizing people into two groups, done in rural China, with 20,995 people, all stroke survivors or high blood pressure patients over 60, followed for 4.74 years. Result: the low-sodium salt group had about 12% lower risk of death than the ordinary salt group (RR 0.88). Stroke about 14% lower (RR 0.86). Major cardiovascular events about 13% lower (RR 0.87). Events of high blood potassium showed no statistical difference between groups.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Neal B, et al. (2021). Effect of Salt Substitution on Cardiovascular Events and Death. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2105675>；O'Donnell M, et al. (2014). Urinary sodium and potassium excretion, mortality, and cardiovascular events. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1311889> (the opposing PURE study)
- Notes: Disputed. The PURE study only tracked and recorded long-term, without randomization. It reported: people excreting under 3 g of sodium a day had about 27% higher risk of death plus cardiovascular events. People excreting 7 g or more a day, about 15% higher. By its results, both too little and too much are bad, with the middle best. But in the rural Chinese randomized trial cited in this entry, low-sodium salt only replaces part of the sodium and does not push it that low. Note also that the trial participants were high-risk older people. Healthy young people get much less benefit from switching. People with kidney problems, or taking potassium-sparing medicines, should ask a doctor before switching.

### 28. Drink tea three or more times a week

- Cost: Inexpensive. A few minutes to make a cup, taking hardly any time.
- In plain words: Habitual tea drinkers are about 15% less likely to die in the same period. Counting from age 50, they live 1.26 years longer on average, with 1.41 more years free of cardiovascular disease.
- Benefit: A Chinese study of 100,900 people (China-PAR), half followed for at least 7.3 years. Habitual tea drinkers had about 15% lower risk of death (hazard ratio 0.85, 95% CI 0.79–0.90, the confidence range). Starting from age 50: habitual tea drinkers had 1.41 more years free of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and 1.26 years longer life expectancy
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Wang X, Liu F, Li J, et al. (2020). Tea consumption and the risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease and all-cause mortality: The China-PAR project. European Journal of Preventive Cardiology, 27(18), 1956-1963. <https://doi.org/10.1177/2047487319894685>；Mycotoxins in tea: Cui P, et al. (2020). ### 29. Cook with vegetable oil instead of lard and butter; there is no need to rotate oils for health, and do not count on flaxseed oil to protect your heart
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 做饭用植物油代替猪油和黄油，不必为了健康换着买油，也别指望亚麻籽油护心 | 5f78f79e -->
- Cost: No extra money. Vegetable oil is already most households' main oil. The hard part is breaking the taste habit of stir-frying with lard or mixing it into rice.
- In plain words: Eating less saturated fat such as lard and butter and switching to vegetable oil cut heart disease and stroke events by about 17%, but no change in overall risk of death was seen. Replacing it with polyunsaturated fat (the main component of soybean and corn oil) or with carbohydrate showed no difference in effect. Data on replacing it with monounsaturated fat (the main component of rapeseed and olive oil) were too few to say. A third pooled 86 randomized trials with 162,796 people: eating more plant-based omega-3 (the main component of flaxseed and perilla oil) gave total mortality RR 1.01 (0.84–1.20) and coronary events RR 1.00 (0.82–1.22), essentially no effect.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Hooper L, Martin N, Jimoh OF, et al. (2020). Reduction in saturated fat for cardiovascular disease. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (5), CD011737. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011737.pub3>；Hooper L, Al-Khudairy L, Abdelhamid AS, et al. (2018). Omega-6 fats for the primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (11), CD011094. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011094.pub4>；Abdelhamid AS, Brown TJ, Brainard JS, et al. (2020). Omega-3 fatty acids for the primary and secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (3), CD003177. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003177.pub5>
- Notes: Disputed: some argue that soybean and corn oil contain too much omega-6, which promotes inflammation, so you should eat less and switch oils. The randomized trials in the omega-6 review above found no harm, and the authors rated the evidence as low quality. The benefit is rated medium on the 17% fewer cardiovascular events; total mortality did not change. Whatever the oil, keep the amount in check.

### 30. Do 30–60 minutes of strength training a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 每周做 30–60 分钟力量训练 | 791ec425 -->
- Cost: Bodyweight squats and push-ups are free. Train 1–2 times a week, 20–30 minutes each time. The hard part is fitting it into your weekly schedule.
- In plain words: People who do 30–60 minutes of strength training a week are 10% to 20% less likely to die in the same period than those who do none. Training more is no better. Strength and aerobic exercise together work best.
- Benefit: Pooling several follow-up studies (recording only, without randomization): people doing strength training had 10%–17% lower risk of death than those who did none. More is not better; the largest reduction, about 10%–20%, was at about 30–60 minutes a week, and more training brought no extra benefit. People doing both strength and aerobic exercise had lower risk of death than those doing neither.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Momma H, et al. (2022). Muscle-strengthening activities are associated with lower risk and mortality in major non-communicable diseases: a systematic review and meta-analysis of cohort studies. British Journal of Sports Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2021-105061>
- Notes: These studies only track and record, and the amount of exercise was self-reported. The evidence for "overdoing it makes things worse" is very weak, so there is no need to limit training for that reason. Strength training also helps older people prevent falls and keep muscle.

### 31. Set a quit day and stop completely on that day, rather than cutting down gradually first
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 定一个戒烟日，到那天一次停掉，别先慢慢减 | 76a49378 -->
- Cost: Free. Just pick a day on the calendar and tell family and colleagues in advance.
- In plain words: Many people plan to smoke a few fewer each day and gradually get to zero. But at six months, 22% of those who set a day and stopped completely had not smoked again, versus only 15.5% of those cutting down gradually. So set a day and stop completely on that day.
- Benefit: 697 UK smokers were randomized into two groups. One stopped completely on quit day; the other cut down by 75% over the two weeks before quit day. Both groups had behavioural support from nurses and used nicotine replacement before and after quit day. At 4 weeks, 49.0% (95% CI 43.8–54.2, the confidence range) of the abrupt group had not relapsed, versus 39.2% (34.0–44.4) of the gradual group, relative risk 0.80 (0.66–0.93). At 6 months, 22.0% (18.0–26.6) of the abrupt group versus 15.5% (12.0–19.7) of the gradual group, relative risk 0.71 (0.46–0.91). People who preferred cutting down gradually to begin with also had lower success at 4 weeks than those preferring to stop abruptly (38.3% vs 52.2%).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Lindson-Hawley N, Banting M, West R, Michie S, Shinkins B, Aveyard P (2016). Gradual Versus Abrupt Smoking Cessation: A Randomized, Controlled Noninferiority Trial. Annals of Internal Medicine, 164(9), 585–592. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M14-2805>；Theodoulou A, Chepkin SC, Ye W, et al. (2023). Different doses, durations and modes of delivery of nicotine replacement therapy for smoking cessation. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 6, CD013308 (the part on starting nicotine replacement in advance). <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013308.pub2>
- Notes: Disputed. Cochrane previously had a review specifically comparing the two approaches, concluding they were similar (RR 0.94, 95% CI 0.79–1.13, 10 trials with 3,760 people). But that review was withdrawn in 2019 and is no longer updated. So the best current evidence is the randomized trial above, and its direction supports stopping abruptly. Also, in that trial both groups started nicotine replacement before quit day, which itself has evidence. Starting in advance raises success by about 25% compared with starting on quit day (RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.08–1.44, 9 trials with 4,395 people, moderate-certainty evidence). So a sensible plan is to set the day first, start patches two weeks before, and stop completely on the day.

### 32. Brush your teeth properly, clean between them once a day, and replace missing teeth promptly

### 33. Sleep about 7 hours a night, on a fixed schedule
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 每晚睡 7 小时左右，作息固定 | b32a2d4d -->
- Cost: Free. You need to set aside time to sleep; for most people that means trading phone time for sleep. The hard part is fixing your bedtime and wake-up time.
- In plain words: People who habitually sleep under 7 hours are about 10% more likely to die in the same period. The more irregular the schedule, the higher the risk. People with the most regular sleep are 20% to nearly 50% lower than those with the least regular. So whether your schedule is regular says more about risk of death than how long you sleep.
- Benefit: Pooling 16 follow-up studies, recording only without randomization, 1.38 million people and 113,000 deaths. Short sleepers had about 12% higher risk of death (RR 1.12). Long sleepers about 30% higher (RR 1.30). Another pooled analysis by sleep duration: the 7-hour band had the lowest risk. Below 7 hours, each hour less was about 6% higher (RR 1.06). Above 7 hours, each hour more was about 13% higher (RR 1.13). The UK Biobank has wrist-device records for about 61,000 people, recording only without randomization. People were split into five bands by sleep regularity. The four more regular bands had 20%–48% lower risk of death than the least regular band. Regularity predicted death better than duration. Another UK Biobank study measured sleep timing with wrist devices in 51,562 people, of whom 3,853 developed new cardiovascular disease. Social jet lag means how much the midpoint of sleep differs between workdays and weekends. People with social jet lag of 2 hours or more had about 30% higher cardiovascular disease risk (HR 1.30, 95% CI 1.11–1.54). This association was also present in people with normal sleep duration.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Cappuccio FP, et al. (2010). Sleep duration and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective studies. Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/33.5.585>；Yin J, et al. (2017). Relationship of Sleep Duration With All-Cause Mortality and Cardiovascular Events: A Systematic Review and Dose-Response Meta-Analysis of Prospective Cohort Studies. JAHA. <https://doi.org/10.1161/JAHA.117.005947>；Windred DP, et al. (2024). Sleep regularity is a stronger predictor of mortality risk than sleep duration: A prospective cohort study. Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsad253>；Kumar N, Krishnamurthy S (2026). Social jet lag is associated with incident cardiovascular disease independent of sleep duration and cardiac genetic risk. Journal of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.70133>
- Notes: What to change is too little sleep and an irregular schedule; there is no need to deliberately cut sleep. The higher risk in long sleepers most likely reflects reverse causation: depression, chronic illness and sleep apnoea make people sleep more. Also, regularity is currently supported by two follow-up studies, both recording only without randomization, which on their own would be grade B evidence.

### 34. Eat 5 portions (about 400 g) of fruit and vegetables a day

- Cost: A bit more on groceries every day. Some time each day to wash and chop. The hard part is buying them daily and eating enough every day.
- In plain words: Eating 200 g more vegetables and fruit a day makes you about 10% less likely to die in the same period. People eating 5 portions a day are about 13% lower than those eating only 2. Eating more than that does not lower it further.
- Benefit: Pooling several follow-up studies (recording only, without randomization): each extra 200 g of fruit and vegetables a day lowered the risk of death by about 10% (RR 0.90). The benefit continued up to 800 g a day. Two large US follow-up studies plus a pooled analysis of 26 studies: people eating 5 portions a day had about 13% lower risk of death than those eating only 2 (HR 0.87). The best combination was 2 portions of fruit plus 3 of vegetables; more did not lower it further.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Aune D, et al. (2017). Fruit and vegetable intake and the risk of cardiovascular disease, total cancer and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of prospective studies. International Journal of Epidemiology. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyw319>；Wang DD, et al. (2021). - Notes: 5 portions is enough; there is no need to reach 10, and the two studies agree on this. ### 35. No need to give up eggs, but do not eat three or four a day

- Cost: Free, and no extra time.
- In plain words: Eating an extra half egg a day makes you about 7% more likely to die in the same period. Replacing that half with egg white, fish, poultry or nuts lowers the risk of each kind of death. No need to give up eggs; just do not eat three or four a day.
- Benefit: A US follow-up study of 521,000 people, with 129,000 deaths. Each extra half whole egg a day raised the risk of death by about 7% (hazard ratio 1.07, 95% CI 1.06–1.08, the confidence range). Replacing this half egg with an equivalent amount of egg white, poultry, fish, dairy or nuts and legumes lowered the risk of death, and also lowered cardiovascular, cancer and respiratory death
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Zhuang P, Wu F, Mao L, et al. (2021). Egg and cholesterol consumption and mortality from cardiovascular and different causes in the United States: A population-based cohort study. PLoS Medicine, 18(2), e1003508. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003508>
- Notes: Highly disputed: other studies following large groups over long periods, and several pooled analyses, found no link between eggs and mortality. ### 36. To drink less, first count how much you drink in a week, then talk to a doctor for a few minutes
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 想少喝酒，先把一周喝了多少数出来，再找医生聊几分钟 | d2c64323 -->
- Cost: Free. Just note the number of drinks each day. Mentioning it at a clinic visit costs nothing extra either.
- In plain words: A doctor spending a few minutes looking at how much you drink, explaining the risks and helping you set a goal leads to about 20 grams less pure alcohol a week a year later, roughly half a litre of beer. Talking longer brings no more benefit. For people who already cannot stop, two medicines have been shown to work abroad: for about every 12 people treated, 1 more does not go back to drinking heavily.
- Benefit: Pooling 34 randomized trials with 15,197 people, those receiving a brief intervention drank 20 grams less pure alcohol a week a year later than those receiving minimal or no intervention (95% CI 12 to 28 grams less, the confidence range; moderate-quality evidence). Participants' baseline drinking in these studies averaged 244 grams a week. A brief intervention means advice or lifestyle counselling of no more than 5 sessions totalling under 60 minutes; talking longer brought no extra benefit. On medicines: pooling 122 randomized trials with 22,803 people, for every 12 people treated with acamprosate (95% CI 8 to 26), 1 more did not return to any drinking; with oral naltrexone 50 mg a day, for every 12 treated (8 to 26), 1 more did not return to heavy drinking.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Kaner EF, Beyer FR, Muirhead C, et al. (2018). Effectiveness of brief alcohol interventions in primary care populations. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 2, CD004148. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004148.pub4>；Jonas DE, Amick HR, Feltner C, et al. (2014). Pharmacotherapy for adults with alcohol use disorders in outpatient settings: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA, 311(18), 1889–1900. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2014.3628>
- Notes: The benefit is rated small because both pieces of evidence measure how much people drink, not mortality. By this book's rules, surrogate outcomes only are rated small. The medicine trials were mostly done abroad, and most participants met diagnostic criteria for alcohol dependence. All trials also gave psychosocial interventions, so the figures are "extra benefit on top of that". Whether these two medicines are approved for this use in Japan and can be prescribed depends on your doctor and the package insert; do not buy them online yourself. For people already having withdrawal reactions, see entry 21 of this section (do not quit abruptly on your own).

### 37. Replace some red meat with fish and poultry

- Cost: Free, and no extra time. It is switching meats, not buying more. The hard part is adjusting your taste preferences a little.
- In plain words: Eating two more servings of processed or red meat a week makes you about 3% more likely to die in the same period. Replacing them with the same number of servings of fish or poultry shows no rise. The effect in this entry is small for this section, something to switch casually without fuss.
- Benefit: Pooling 6 US follow-up studies with nearly 30,000 people. Two extra servings of processed meat a week raised the risk of death by about 3% (hazard ratio 1.03, 95% CI 1.02–1.05, confidence range). Two extra servings of unprocessed red meat a week, also about 3% higher (1.03, 1.01–1.05). The same for poultry was 0.99 (0.97–1.02); for fish, 0.99 (0.97–1.01). Neither showed a statistical difference
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Zhong VW, Van Horn L, Greenland P, et al. (2020). Associations of Processed Meat, Unprocessed Red Meat, Poultry, or Fish Intake With Incident Cardiovascular Disease and All-Cause Mortality. JAMA Internal Medicine, 180(4), 503-512. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.6969>
- Notes: The difference from two servings a week is very small, so do not expect switching meats to add years to your life. When spending on meat anyway, you can choose fish and poultry first
