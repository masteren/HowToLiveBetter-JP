# 4. Tax and pension: what you pay and how to get it back

<!-- zh-intro: 89815541 -->

This section covers the taxes and pension payments that foreigners working or studying in Japan meet most often: when resident tax arrives, how much pension you can get back when you leave Japan, whether supporting family abroad reduces your tax, and how unpaid amounts affect your residence status and permanent residence. Amounts and conditions are as of 2026-10-03. They are often adjusted around April each year, so check again before you act.

### 1. Resident tax arrives in your second year; pay it off before leaving your job or Japan, and appoint a tax agent if you cannot
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
<!-- zh: 住民税第二年才来，离职或离开日本前交清，交不完就指定纳税管理人 | d91ab5b8 -->
- Cost: No extra money, since you owe resident tax anyway. Before leaving Japan, make one trip to the ward office, or find someone living in Japan to act as your tax agent (納税管理人) and fill in one form.
- In plain words: Resident tax (住民税) is based on the previous year's income, and whoever lives in Japan on January 1 must pay it, even if they leave on January 2 or later. So nothing is deducted in your first year, and deductions start in the second. The Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications states that unpaid resident tax may cause a renewal of your residence to be refused. If you cannot pay it all before leaving Japan, appoint someone living in Japan to handle it for you.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications states that people, including foreigners, who have an address in Japan on January 1 and a certain level of income must pay resident tax to their municipality, even if they leave Japan on January 2 or later. The amount is based on salary and other income from January 1 to December 31 of the previous year. If resident tax owed is not paid, applications such as renewal of the period of stay may not be approved. If you cannot pay in full before leaving Japan, you must, before leaving, choose a tax agent from among people living in Japan to handle your tax procedures, and notify your municipality. If your employer deducts resident tax, on request the remaining amount can be deducted in one go from your last salary or retirement allowance when you leave the job. If you leave between January and May, it must be deducted in one go whether or not you ask
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 総務省「外国人の方の個人住民税について」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.soumu.go.jp/main_sosiki/jichi_zeisei/czaisei/czaisei_seido/individual-inhabitant-tax.html>
- Notes: The benefit is rated large because the consequence is that a residence renewal may not be approved. If the tax notice cannot reach you after you move or return to your country of origin, that does not mean you do not have to pay. For how unpaid amounts affect a permanent residence application, see entry 5 (pay taxes, pension and health insurance on time).

### 2. Students in Japan must join the National Pension at 20; if you cannot pay, apply for the student payment special rule, or a late application can cost you the disability pension
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 在日本上学的满 20 岁就要交国民年金，交不起就申请学生缴费特例，晚了出事拿不到残疾年金 | 8612e617 -->
- Cost: Free. Submit the application at the National Pension counter of your ward office or at a pension office; some schools handle it for you. You need to apply once each fiscal year.
- In plain words: Everyone living in Japan, of any nationality, must pay into the National Pension (国民年金) from age 20, international students included. Students with low income can apply to defer payment. If you apply late and become disabled from an accident or illness that happened before you applied, you may not get the basic disability pension. Deferred amounts can be paid later within 10 years.
- Benefit: The Japan Pension Service states that everyone living in Japan becomes insured under the National Pension from age 20 and is obliged to pay contributions. Students can apply for the student payment special rule (学生納付特例) to defer contributions while in school. The condition is that the student's own income in the previous year is below a threshold such as 1.28 million yen plus 380,000 yen times the number of dependents; family income is not counted. Students include those at universities, graduate schools, junior colleges, specialized training colleges, and miscellaneous schools with a course length of 1 year or more. Approved periods count toward the 10-year qualifying period for the basic old-age pension, but not toward the pension amount. Approved periods can be paid later within 10 years. If you apply late, you may not receive the basic disability pension for a disability caused by an accident or illness that occurred before the application date
- Evidence: B
- Sources: 日本年金機構「国民年金保険料の学生納付特例制度」（updated 2026-08-03, accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.nenkin.go.jp/service/kokunen/menjo/20150514.html>
- Notes: Why grade B: the conditions are stated officially, but there is no figure for how much money applying protects. The benefit is rated large because the basic disability pension is paid yearly over a long period. Whether a Japanese language school is covered depends on whether it is an approved miscellaneous school with a course of 1 year or more; the Japan Pension Service has a list of eligible schools. Your pension payment record is also checked when you apply for permanent residence; see entry 5 (pay taxes, pension and health insurance on time).

### 3. If you leave Japan for good, apply for the pension lump-sum withdrawal payment within 2 years, but first be clear that this pension period is then cancelled
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 离开日本不再回来，2 年内申请年金的脱退一时金，先想清楚这段年金就作废了 | 954ec14f -->
- Cost: Free. Fill in a claim form, attach a copy of your passport, proof of your bank account and your pension number, and mail it to the Japan Pension Service. If you file your move-out notice before leaving, you do not need to submit a separate certificate of removal from the residence record.
- In plain words: Foreigners who paid into the Japanese pension for 6 months or more and leave Japan for good can apply within 2 years to get part of it back. From the National Pension you can get back up to a little over 500,000 yen, capped at 60 months. But once you receive it, all your previous pension periods in Japan are cancelled. People who paid for 10 years or more cannot claim it, because they can receive an old-age pension later.
- Benefit: The Japan Pension Service states that a person who is not a Japanese national, is no longer covered by Japan's public pension, paid contributions for 6 months or more, does not meet the 10-year qualifying period for the old-age pension, and does not live in Japan, can claim the lump-sum withdrawal payment (脱退一時金) within 2 years from the day they no longer have an address in Japan. Since April 2021 the maximum number of months counted rose from 36 to 60. For those whose last National Pension contribution month falls between April 2026 and March 2027, 6 to 11 months of contributions return 53,760 yen, and 60 months or more return 537,600 yen. For the Employees' Pension, the amount is the average salary over the insured period times a payment rate. Anyone who receives the lump-sum withdrawal payment loses, in every case, all periods of Japanese pension coverage before the claim. For countries that have a pension agreement with Japan allowing periods to be combined, the periods can be added to those in the other country, but not after receiving the lump sum. As of March 2026, the 20 countries with such agreements include Germany, the United States, France, Canada, Australia, Brazil, India and the Philippines; China and South Korea are not among them. If you leave without filing a move-out notice, you are still covered by the National Pension while your re-entry permit is valid, and cannot claim
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 日本年金機構「脱退一時金の制度」（updated 2026-04-01, accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.nenkin.go.jp/service/jukyu/seido/sonota-kyufu/dattai-ichiji/20150406.html> ; 日本年金機構「日本から出国される外国人のみなさまへ」脱退一時金請求書（English version, with notes in Japanese）. <https://www.nenkin.go.jp/international/english/japanese-system/benefit/payment.files/A.pdf>
- Notes: If you were in Japan for more than 5 years, the money is capped at 60 months, but the whole period is cancelled. If you may come back to Japan later, or may end up staying 10 years, do not rush to claim it. If you come and go several times, for example finishing technical intern training, going home and returning as a Specified Skilled Worker, pay attention to claiming for each stay separately. The Japan Pension Service states that you may need to claim each time a stay ends and you return to your country of origin, to receive payments for each period separately. The claim form must reach the Japan Pension Service on or after the move-out date on your residence record; if it arrives early, it does not count.

### 4. If 20.42% tax was withheld from your Employees' Pension lump sum, appoint a tax agent at the tax office before returning home, and apply for a refund afterwards
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 领厚生年金的脱退一时金被扣了 20.42% 的税，回原籍国前在税务署定好纳税管理人，事后申请退回 | 51166b1f -->
- Cost: Free. Before returning to your country of origin, submit a tax agent notification at the tax office for your last address, naming someone living in Japan as your tax agent. Afterwards, mail them the original payment decision notice that the Japan Pension Service sends you, and they file the return.
- In plain words: When the Employees' Pension (厚生年金) lump-sum withdrawal payment is paid, 20.42% tax is taken off first. If a separate return is filed, this tax may be refunded. The procedure has to be done in Japan, so before you go home, appoint someone living in Japan at the tax office to handle it for you. The National Pension lump sum has no tax withheld.
- Benefit: The Japan Pension Service states that when a non-resident receives the Employees' Pension lump-sum withdrawal payment, 20.42% tax is withheld at source. By filing a "return for elective taxation of retirement income" (退職所得の選択課税の申告書) with the tax office, the withheld tax may be refunded. The National Pension lump sum has no tax withheld. The return goes to the tax office for your last address in Japan. To file and receive the refund, submit a "notification of tax agent for income tax and consumption tax" to that tax office before returning to your country of origin. The tax agent only needs an address in Japan; there are no other requirements. If you left without submitting it, submit it together with the return. When the Japan Pension Service sends the money, it also sends a payment decision notice, and the original must be sent to the tax agent
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 日本年金機構「日本から出国される外国人のみなさまへ」脱退一時金請求書 【脱退一時金にかかる所得税】. <https://www.nenkin.go.jp/international/english/japanese-system/benefit/payment.files/A.pdf>
- Notes: How much comes back depends on your situation; the Japan Pension Service only says it "may" be refunded. The benefit is rated large based on typical Employees' Pension lump-sum amounts. For a lump sum of several hundred thousand yen, the 20.42% withheld is tens of thousands to over 100,000 yen. The forms for the return and the notification can be downloaded from the National Tax Agency website.

### 5. Pay taxes, pension and health insurance on time: past arrears count against a permanent residence application, even if paid later
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
<!-- zh: 按时交税、年金和健康保险：申请永住时，以前拖欠过就会扣分，补交了也一样 | 8f7833bd -->
- Cost: No extra money, since you owe these anyway. The hard part is paying every instalment on time; ideally set up automatic bank debit.
- In plain words: Immigration's permanent residence guidelines, revised in October 2026, spell out what counts against you. Late payment of taxes such as income tax and resident tax, or of health insurance and pension, counts against you. It still counts even if everything is paid up by the time you apply, and having been subject to collection in the past also counts against you in principle. If you plan to apply for permanent residence, pay every instalment on time from now on.
- Benefit: The Immigration Services Agency's "Guidelines for Permission for Permanent Residence" (revised 2026-10-01) state that failure to properly pay "public taxes" (公租), meaning taxes such as income tax, resident tax, corporate tax and fixed asset tax, or "public charges" (公課), meaning non-tax burdens such as public medical insurance and public pension, is assessed negatively. Even if nothing is unpaid at the time of application, a past delinquency disposition or past failure to pay properly is in principle also assessed negatively. In principle the review covers the household sharing the same livelihood. The guidelines also state that the Immigration Act amendment of 2024, in force from April 1, 2027, writes "complying with the obligations under this Act and paying public taxes and charges" into the statutory conditions for permanent residence
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国在留管理庁「永住許可に関するガイドライン（令和８年１０月１日改定）」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/10_00279.html>
- Notes: Family members are included: if your spouse's National Pension or National Health Insurance is unpaid, that also affects your application. The guidelines also have a requirement about how much pension you are expected to receive in future; the details are many and will go into the section on permanent residence and naturalization. Unpaid resident tax can also lead to a refused renewal of your period of stay; see entry 1 (resident tax arrives in your second year).

### 6. To claim parents abroad as dependents, those aged 30 to 69 must receive at least 380,000 yen from you, backed by proof of relationship and remittance records
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 扶养国外的父母抵税，30 到 69 岁的要寄够 38 万日元，还要备齐亲属证明和汇款凭证 | 7f207109 -->
- Cost: No extra money. Prepare proof of the family relationship and remittance records, with Japanese translations for documents in other languages, and submit them to your employer at year-end adjustment or to the tax office when you file a return.
- In plain words: Since 2023, relatives living abroad aged 30 to 69 count as dependents in only three cases: they are studying abroad, they are disabled, or they received at least 380,000 yen from you that year for living or education costs. People aged 16 to 29 and 70 or over are not subject to this limit. When claiming, you must submit proof of the relationship and of remittances, with Japanese translations for documents in other languages.
- Benefit: The National Tax Agency states that the dependent deduction (扶養控除) is generally 380,000 yen per person, and 480,000 yen for elderly dependents aged 70 or over who do not live with you. From tax year 2023, non-resident relatives count as eligible dependents only if, as of December 31 of the year, they are 16 or over and under 30; 70 or over; or 30 or over and under 70 and either live outside Japan for study, are disabled, or received 380,000 yen or more from the taxpayer that year for living or education costs. Salaried workers applying the dependent deduction for relatives abroad must submit or show their employer documents on the relationship, plus remittance documents or documents showing remittances of 380,000 yen. Documents in other languages must come with Japanese translations
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国税庁「No.1180 扶養控除」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.nta.go.jp/taxes/shiraberu/taxanswer/shotoku/1180.htm> ; 国税庁「国外居住親族に係る扶養控除等の適用について」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.nta.go.jp/taxes/tetsuzuki/shinsei/annai/gensen/kokugai/index.htm>
- Notes: The deduction comes off taxable income; it is not a direct refund of that amount. The tax saved depends on your tax rate. Estimated at common rates, it is tens of thousands of yen per person per year, so the benefit is rated medium. If you claim dependents who do not qualify, you will be asked to pay the tax later.

### 7. If an employee's side income is 200,000 yen or less a year, no income tax return is needed, but you still have to declare it for resident tax at the ward office
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 上班族的副业所得一年不超过 20 万日元，所得税可以不报，但住民税还是要去区役所申报 | e4e52ce6 -->
- Cost: Free. Around March 15 each year, submit a resident tax declaration to your municipality.
- In plain words: Employees paid by one company whose year-end adjustment has been done do not need to file an income tax return if their non-salary income totals 200,000 yen or less a year. Resident tax has no such exemption, so side income of 200,000 yen or less must still be declared at the ward office. If you file an income tax return, you do not need a separate resident tax declaration.
- Benefit: The National Tax Agency states that people paid salary by one employer, with all salary taxed at source, must file a final return (確定申告) if their other income, apart from salary and retirement income, totals more than 200,000 yen. Nagoya City states that municipal and prefectural resident tax has no exemption range like income tax, and in principle all income must be declared; so salaried workers with non-salary income of 200,000 yen or less must also declare it for municipal and prefectural resident tax; those who filed an income tax return need not declare separately
- Evidence: B
- Sources: 国税庁「No.1900 給与所得者で確定申告が必要な人」. <https://www.nta.go.jp/taxes/shiraberu/taxanswer/shotoku/1900.htm>；名古屋市「給与所得者で副収入がある場合、市民税・県民税の申告は?」（updated 2026-01-13）. <https://www.city.nagoya.jp/kurashi/zeikin/1035069/1012012/1012022/1033974/1012026.html>
- Notes: As of 2026-10-03. Graded B because the resident tax rule is taken from Nagoya City's page; other municipalities follow their own pages. Income (所得) here means receipts minus expenses, not receipts. Whether your status of residence allows a side job at all comes first; see Section 2, entry 10 (permission for activities outside your status), which matters more than the tax. The benefit is rated small because it avoids failing to declare resident tax.
