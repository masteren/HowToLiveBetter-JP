# 1. Don't die early

<!-- zh-intro: 1e8d50ce -->

This section covers the most common accidental and injury deaths in Japan, and the things that prevent them at almost no cost. The medical evidence is carried over from the original book's section "don't die early", with data replaced by official Japanese statistics. Statistics use the latest year available as of 2026-10-03. Entries are ordered by cost-effectiveness: those costing almost nothing with large benefits come first. Apart from a few entries specific to Japan, the rest are carried over from the original book, covering blood pressure and blood sugar, driving, child safety, fall prevention for older people, vaccines, cancer screening, warning signs that need checking, condoms and suicidal thoughts. For vaccines and screening, Japan's routine vaccination targets and the ages recommended by the National Cancer Center have been added. The original book's entries on poisonous mushrooms, household emergency supplies, free HIV testing, paraquat, falls from height, selling kidneys and radioactive sources were based on Chinese statistics, rules or cases and were not carried over.

In Japan, far more older people die from accidents at home than on the road: among people 65 and over, more die from falls, choking and drowning than from traffic accidents, and about 80% of drownings happen in the bathtub. So several entries below are mainly for older people in your household.

### 1. Wear a seatbelt, in the front and the back
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 系安全带，前排后排都系 | 8ebde495 -->
- Cost: Free. 2 extra seconds each time you get in.
- In plain words: Wearing a seatbelt in the front roughly halves the chance of being killed in a crash. In Japan, rear-seat passengers without a seatbelt have about 14 times the fatality rate of those wearing one on expressways, and about 2.8 times on ordinary roads. Over half the rear-seat passengers killed in vehicles in Japan in 2025 were not wearing one.
- Benefit: The US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) estimates that seatbelts reduce the risk of fatal injury for front-seat occupants by 45% in passenger cars and 60% in light trucks. Of people killed in passenger vehicles in the US in 2022 whose restraint use was known, 50% were unrestrained. The World Health Organization's (WHO) position is that seatbelts reduce the risk of death among vehicle occupants by up to 50%. Japan's National Police Agency reports that the fatality rate (deaths as a share of casualties) for unbelted rear-seat passengers is about 13.9 times that of belted ones on expressways and about 2.8 times on ordinary roads. In 2025, 869 people died in vehicles in Japan, of whom 309, or 35.6%, were not wearing seatbelts. By seat, 52.4% of rear-seat deaths were unbelted. In 2025 surveys, rear-seat belt use was only 45.8% on ordinary roads and 79.9% on expressways. Driver and front passenger were both over 96%
- Evidence: A
- Sources: NHTSA (2024). Occupant Protection in Passenger Vehicles: 2022 Data (DOT HS 813 573). <https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813573> ; WHO (2025). Road traffic injuries fact sheet. <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/road-traffic-injuries> ; 警察庁「全ての座席でシートベルトを着用しましょう」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.npa.go.jp/bureau/traffic/anzen/seatbelt.html> ; 警察庁「シートベルト関連統計」（令和７年中）. <https://www.npa.go.jp/bureau/traffic/anzen/8-1.pdf>
- Notes: The 45% and 60% are NHTSA estimates calculated from US crash databases, not randomized trials. The National Police Agency's 13.9 times and 2.8 times are comparisons of accident statistics, and people who do and do not wear belts may differ in other ways. Japanese law requires seatbelts in all seats; an unbelted rear-seat passenger on an expressway costs the driver 1 penalty point. An unbelted rear passenger is thrown forward in a crash and can injure people in front too.

### 2. If you have thoughts of ending your life, call the Yorisoi Hotline, which has Chinese, and do not stockpile large amounts of sleeping pills at home
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 有轻生的念头时打能说中文的よりそい热线，家里不囤大量安眠药 | e964364b -->
- Cost: Free; one free phone call. Also lock medicines away and do not keep large amounts of sleeping pills to hand.
- In plain words: Removing lethal means from reach has visible effects. After controls on painkillers, related suicides fell 43%. Barriers at places where people often jump cut them by 86%. When you feel you cannot go on, call 0120-279-338 and press 2 after the Japanese prompt to reach the foreign-language line, which includes Chinese.
- Benefit: A review systematically going over research from the past 10 years: evidence on restricting access to lethal means has kept getting stronger. After painkillers were controlled, painkiller-related suicides fell 43%. After barriers were added at places where people often jump, they fell 86% (79% to 91%; the brackets are this figure's confidence range). School-based cognitive programmes lowered suicide attempts by about 55% (OR 0.45, 95% CI 0.24 to 0.85). Drug treatment and psychotherapy for depression are an important part of suicide prevention. The consultation services page of Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare lists the Yorisoi Hotline (よりそいホットライン), 0120-279-338, free and answered 24 hours, with a foreign-language option in the voice menu. From IP phones starting with 050, call 050-3655-0279. The operator's page states that pressing 2 transfers you to the foreign-language line, daily from 10 a.m. to 10 p.m., in 10 languages including Chinese, with days for each language scheduled monthly
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Zalsman G, et al. (2016). Suicide prevention strategies revisited: 10-year systematic review. Lancet Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(16)30030-X> ; 厚生労働省「まもろうよ こころ 電話相談」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.mhlw.go.jp/mamorouyokokoro/soudan/tel/> ; 一般社団法人社会的包摂サポートセンター「よりそいホットライン Helpline for foreign languages」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.since2011.net/yorisoi/n2/>
- Notes: Why grade B: most of the evidence on restricting means compares figures before and after policies, and there is no evidence that hotlines themselves lower mortality. Chinese is not answered every day, so before calling you can check that month's schedule on the operator's page. The hotline also takes messages on Facebook Messenger. For family in mainland China, the original book, 《高性价比人生指南》, covers the 12356 hotline there.

### 3. Install residential fire alarms on bedroom ceilings and above stairs, press the test button, and replace them after 10 years
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 寝室和楼梯上方装住宅用火灾报警器，按一下测试按钮，满 10 年就换 | ff7ed9d7 -->
- Cost: Sold at electronics stores and home centres, usually a few thousand yen each. Once installed, press the test button every so often, and replace the whole unit after 10 years. If you rent, look up at the bedroom ceiling to see if there is one, and if so, press it to test.
- In plain words: According to Japan's Fire and Disaster Management Agency, homes with fire alarms have half the deaths and losses in fires. Residential fires are only 30% of fires but 70% of fire deaths. Smoke-type alarms are required in bedrooms and above the stairs on bedroom floors. Alarms last about 10 years, after which the whole unit should be replaced.
- Benefit: The Fire and Disaster Management Agency analysed damage from residential fires: compared with homes without residential fire alarms, homes with them had half the deaths and losses and about 60% less area burned. In 2024, residential fires were about 30% of all fires but about 70% of deaths. Fire regulations require smoke-type alarms in bedrooms and above the stairs on floors with bedrooms, and some municipalities also require them in kitchens and elsewhere. There is no penalty. Alarms last about 10 years; press the test button regularly, and replace them after 10 years. A US study looking back to compare households with and without fires: having one working smoke alarm at home meant about 60% lower risk of fire death (OR 0.39, 95% CI 0.18 to 0.83). In fatal fires in occupied homes in the US from 2018 to 2020, 24% of homes had no smoke alarm, and in 41% the people were asleep when the fire started
- Evidence: B
- Sources: 消防庁予防課「住宅用火災警報器を設置しましょう！」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.fdma.go.jp/relocation/html/life/juukei.html> ; 消防庁予防課「住宅用火災警報器Q＆A」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.fdma.go.jp/relocation/html/life/yobou_contents/qa/> ; Marshall SW, et al. (1998). Fatal residential fires: who dies and who survives? JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.279.20.1633> ; USFA (2022). Fatal Fires in Residential Buildings (2018-2020), Topical Fire Report Series 22(2). <https://www.usfa.fema.gov/downloads/pdf/statistics/v22i2.pdf>
- Notes: Why grade B: both the agency's figures and the US study compare fires that have already happened, not randomized trials. The agency warns of people claiming to be "from the fire station" selling alarms door to door, or frightening people into buying expensive ones by saying "it's illegal not to have one". Fire stations do not sell door to door; if you are scammed, contact the fire station or a consumer affairs center.

### 4. Wear a helmet when cycling, with the strap fastened
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 骑自行车戴头盔并扣好扣带 | 3c608e4a -->
- Cost: A few thousand yen for one; choose one with the SG mark. 5 seconds to put it on each time.
- In plain words: About half of cyclists killed in accidents in Japan suffered fatal head injuries. Motorcyclists wearing helmets are about 40% less likely to die in a crash. Since April 2023, Japan has asked all cyclists to make an effort to wear helmets. Fasten the strap tightly.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooled several studies: helmets reduced the risk of death for motorcyclists by 42% (OR 0.58, 95% CI 0.50 to 0.68) and head injury by 69% (OR 0.31, 95% CI 0.25 to 0.38). Japan's National Police Agency reports that about 50% of people killed in traffic accidents while cycling had fatal head injuries. Over the five years 2021 to 2025, among those killed or seriously injured mainly in the head, the proportion not wearing helmets was about 1.7 times that of those who were. The amended Road Traffic Act, from April 1, 2023, made helmet wearing an "obligation to make efforts" for all cyclists, meaning they should try to do it, but with no penalty
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Liu BC, et al. (2008). Helmets for preventing injury in motorcycle riders. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004333.pub3> ; 警察庁「頭部の保護が重要です ～自転車用ヘルメットと頭部保護帽～」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.npa.go.jp/bureau/traffic/anzen/toubuhogo.html>
- Notes: The Cochrane data come from motorcycles. There are no studies of the same grade for bicycles; this is extrapolated, since the head is injured the same way in a crash. The National Police Agency's 1.7 times is a statistical comparison, and helmet wearers may ride more carefully to begin with. The agency recommends choosing helmets with safety marks such as SG.

### 5. Do not speed or drink-drive
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 开车不超速、不酒驾 | 36d7c236 -->
- Cost: Free. Driving a bit slower adds a few minutes per trip. The hard part is drinking less at dinners.
- In plain words: Each 1% increase in average speed raises the chance of a fatal crash by about 4%. There is no "a little is fine" safe line for alcohol. Risk starts rising even when blood alcohol concentration is still very low.
- Benefit: The World Health Organization (WHO) says: each 1% increase in average speed raises the risk of a fatal crash by 4%. The risk of drink-driving starts rising from very low blood alcohol concentrations
- Evidence: B
- Sources: WHO (2025). Road traffic injuries fact sheet. <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/road-traffic-injuries>
- Notes: The 1% and 4% for speed and death were estimated from crash models, not randomized trials. For drink-driving, WHO gives only the direction, no figure. The question "how much drinking raises the risk by how much" has not been checked against original literature in this section.

### 6. If you have small children, fit restrictors to windows and balconies; insect screens are no protection
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 家里有小孩就给窗户和阳台装限位器，纱窗不算防护 | cf48d8b8 -->
- Cost: Window restrictors or child safety locks are inexpensive. Ten minutes to fit.
- In plain words: Restrictors are cheap. New York City installed free window guards and gave home visits in high-risk households, and reported child falls in one borough halved in three years. New York then passed a law requiring landlords to install guards in homes with children. Insect screens pop out with a push and are no protection.
- Benefit: From 1972 New York City ran a programme called "Children Can't Fly", giving out free window guards and home education in high-risk neighbourhoods. Reported falls in the Bronx fell 50% from 1973 to 1975. New York City then amended its health code to require landlords to install window guards in apartments where children aged 10 and under live
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Spiegel CN, Lindaman FC (1977). Children can't fly: a program to prevent childhood morbidity and mortality from window falls. American Journal of Public Health: "Significant reduction in falls resulted, particularly in the Bronx, where reported falls declined 50 percent from 1973 to 1975", and the 1976 amendment of the New York City Health Code "to require that landlords provide window guards in apartments where children ten years old and younger reside". <https://doi.org/10.2105/AJPH.67.12.1143>；(2021). Unintentional Window Falls in Children and Adolescents. Academic Pediatrics: 38,840 emergency visits from January 2007 to August 2017 in the National Electronic Injury Surveillance System, "The majority of falls occurred in children under the age of 6 and were related to falls from a second story or below". <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.acap.2020.07.008>
- Notes: Do not put beds, sofas, boxes or anything children can climb on next to windows and balcony edges. Insect screens only keep out mosquitoes and fall out with a push; do not treat them as guards. It is not only high-rise dwellers who need them: in the US data, most falls were from the second storey or lower.

### 7. Get tested for hepatitis B markers, and get vaccinated if you have no antibodies
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 查乙肝两对半，没有抗体就打疫苗 | c224b749 -->
- Cost: You pay part of the cost of both the test and the vaccine. Adult hepatitis B vaccination is 3 doses, completed within half a year.
- In plain words: One blood test tells you whether you have antibodies. In the Qidong trial followed for 30 years, people vaccinated against hepatitis B at birth were 84% less likely to develop primary liver cancer later. The proportion carrying the hepatitis B virus was 72% lower. Adults found to have no antibodies get three doses over half a year.
- Benefit: The Qidong trial randomized by community rather than individual and followed people for 30 years. Neonatal hepatitis B vaccination reduced primary liver cancer by 84%. The confidence range is 95% CI 23% to 97%. The hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) positivity rate fell 72% (95% CI 68% to 75%); this rate is the proportion of people found to be carrying the virus.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Qu C, et al. (2014). Efficacy of neonatal HBV vaccination on liver cancer and other liver diseases over 30-year follow-up of the Qidong hepatitis B intervention study. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001774> ; Cui F, et al. (2017). Prevention of Chronic Hepatitis B after 3 Decades of Escalating Vaccination Policy, China. Emerging Infectious Diseases. <https://doi.org/10.3201/eid2305.161477>
- Notes: This applies to adults born before 1992 who were never vaccinated or whose antibodies have faded. The 84% reduction in liver cancer comes from people vaccinated at birth. In the same trial, for those given catch-up vaccination as adolescents, protection against becoming carriers was only 21%. Adults get vaccinated mainly to avoid future infection, so the 84% cannot be applied directly. Vaccination does nothing for people already infected, who need regular check-ups at a liver clinic. How to tell if you are already infected: the hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) item on your test report is positive.

### 8. If you are pierced by a nail or splinter, or a wound gets soil in it, get it treated that day and ask whether you need a tetanus shot
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 被铁钉、木刺扎到或者伤口沾了泥土，当天去处理，顺便问破伤风要不要打 | d4844288 -->
- Cost: One doctor's visit to treat the wound. If the doctor says you need one, a vaccine or immunoglobulin shot on top.
- In plain words: Once tetanus develops, about 1 in 10 people die. Deep wounds and wounds with soil in them are the highest risk. For a dirty wound, if it has been 5 years since your last shot, you need 1 booster. Get it treated that day; do not wait until the wound swells.
- Benefit: About 1 in 10 tetanus cases in the US is fatal. Deep wounds and wounds contaminated with soil carry higher risk. The CDC's wound management guidance for doctors states: for people who completed the 3-dose primary series, a clean minor wound needs a booster only if it has been 10 years since the last dose. Wounds contaminated with soil, faeces or saliva, and puncture, avulsion, crush, burn and frostbite wounds, need a booster if it has been 5 years since the last dose
- Evidence: B
- Sources: US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Tetanus: "Tetanus bacteria can get into someone's body through broken skin, usually through injuries." "Tetanus can lead to death (1 in 10 cases in the United States are fatal)." "People who didn't complete the primary series or who aren't up to date with their 10-year tetanus booster shots are also at increased risk." "Vaccination also helps prevent tetanus in people with wounds, depending on their tetanus vaccination history." <https://www.cdc.gov/tetanus/about/index.html>；CDC. Pink Book Chapter 21: Tetanus (wound management table): "Yes, if ≥5 years since the last tetanus toxoid-containing vaccine dose". <https://www.cdc.gov/pinkbook/hcp/table-of-contents/chapter-21-tetanus.html>
- Notes: At the hospital, let the doctor decide; do not decide yourself, and do not skip asking to save hassle. Not every wound needs a shot: the doctor looks at the nature of the wound and when you were last vaccinated, then decides. "Shots in childhood last a lifetime" is wrong. For a dirty wound, a booster is needed if it has been 5 years since the last one, so before going, think about what year you last had a tetanus shot.

### 9. Women over 30 should have cervical cancer screening, preferably an HPV test
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 30 岁以上女性做宫颈癌筛查，优先 HPV 检测 | 5f70f0e5 -->
- Cost: Municipal cervical cancer screening is publicly subsidized. If the result is negative, once every 5 years is enough. Taking the sample takes a few minutes.
- In plain words: In the trial in rural India, women who had just one HPV test in their lives were about half as likely to die of cervical cancer. They were also about 50% less likely to be diagnosed with advanced cervical cancer. Municipal screening is subsidised, and a negative result covers 5 years.
- Benefit: The rural India trial randomized by community, not by individual. Participants were women aged 30 to 59. Screening with a single HPV test lowered the risk of dying of cervical cancer by about 48%. The original figure is HR 0.52, with confidence range 95% CI 0.33 to 0.83. The risk of advanced cervical cancer was about 53% lower (HR 0.47, 95% CI 0.32 to 0.69)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Sankaranarayanan R, et al. (2009). HPV screening for cervical cancer in rural India. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0808516>；国立がん研究センター がん情報サービス. 子宮頸がん検診について. <https://ganjoho.jp/public/pre_scr/screening/cervix_uteri.html>
- Notes: People who have had the HPV vaccine still need screening, because the vaccine does not cover all virus types. What is rare about this study is that it is a randomized screening trial with deaths as the outcome, and only one round of screening.

### 10. People with cardiovascular disease and older people should get a flu vaccine every year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 有心血管病的人和老年人每年打流感疫苗 | 436b3fd7 -->
- Cost: Once every autumn, paying part of the cost. In Japan, people 65 and over get it as a routine vaccination subsidized by the municipality.
- In plain words: In the trial of flu vaccination after a heart attack, the proportion dying within a year fell from 4.9% to 2.9%. People with cardiovascular disease and older people should get one every autumn.
- Benefit: A trial after myocardial infarction randomized 2,571 people. Neither doctors nor patients knew which shot was given. Total mortality over 12 months was 2.9% for those given the flu vaccine. 4.9% for those given placebo. The original figure is HR 0.59, with confidence range 95% CI 0.39 to 0.89. Placebo is a control shot with no active ingredient. HR for cardiovascular death was 0.59. Another study pooled several studies. Major cardiovascular events were 3.6% in those vaccinated against flu and 5.4% in controls. The original figure is RR 0.66, with confidence range 95% CI 0.53 to 0.83. Cardiovascular death RR 0.74 (95% CI 0.42 to 1.30; this difference is still within the margin of error and not statistically significant)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Fröbert O, et al. (2021). Influenza Vaccination After Myocardial Infarction: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Multicenter Trial. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.121.057042> ; Behrouzi B, et al. (2022). Association of Influenza Vaccination With Cardiovascular Risk: A Meta-analysis. JAMA Network Open. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2022.8873>；Demicheli V, et al. (2018). Vaccines for preventing influenza in the elderly. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004876.pub4> (the Cochrane review mentioned in the notes)；厚生労働省. インフルエンザワクチン（定期接種の対象者：65歳以上の方）. <https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/seisakunitsuite/bunya/kenkou_iryou/kenkou/kekkaku-kansenshou/yobou-sesshu/vaccine/influenza/index.html>
- Notes: Disputed. Grade A applies only to people with cardiovascular disease. For older people who are otherwise healthy, the Cochrane review gives only one conclusion: flu cases in a season fell from 6% to 2.4%. It rated the certainty of this evidence as "low", and the evidence on mortality as "very low". The review is Demicheli V and colleagues, 2018.

### 11. Heavy smokers should have a low-dose chest CT once a year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 重度吸烟者每年做一次低剂量胸部 CT | bb6095a9 -->
- Cost: You pay part, and it takes 10 minutes. Also count the follow-up tests after a false alarm, and the anxiety in the meantime.
- In plain words: Eligible heavy smokers who have a low-dose CT once a year are 20% less likely to die of lung cancer than those who only have chest X-rays. The overall chance of death is 6.7% lower. The cost is follow-up tests after a suspicious result, and the anxiety in the meantime.
- Benefit: The US NLST randomized trial enrolled 53,454 people, all aged 55 to 74, with a smoking history of 30 or more pack-years and having quit no more than 15 years earlier. A pack-year is a unit of smoking: one pack a day for one year is one pack-year. The results were as follows. Compared with chest X-rays only, low-dose CT reduced lung cancer deaths by a relative 20.0%. Confidence range 95% CI 6.8 to 26.7. Total mortality fell by a relative 6.7%, confidence range 95% CI 1.2 to 13.6
- Evidence: A
- Sources: National Lung Screening Trial Research Team (2011). Reduced Lung-Cancer Mortality with Low-Dose Computed Tomographic Screening. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1102873>
- Notes: This entry only applies to high-risk smokers. In the same trial, 24.2% of those having low-dose CT had a suspicious finding, and 96.4% of those were false alarms (false positives). For non-smokers, this test means paying to buy anxiety.

### 12. As soon as a suicidal thought appears, tell one person near you, and hand over these few dozen minutes
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 自杀念头一冒出来先告诉身边的一个人，把这几十分钟交出去 | 748ad7b8 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is speaking up in the moment, and admitting you cannot be alone for these few hours.
- In plain words: Nearly half of people who attempted suicide said it was no more than 10 minutes from the thought appearing to acting on it, so what needs getting through is often just these few dozen minutes. Three-quarters had contact with people close to them during that time, but did not speak up. Of people hospitalized for self-harm, about 2% die by suicide after 1 year and about 7% after 9 or more years; the vast majority do not later die by suicide.
- Benefit: 82 people who attempted suicide were interviewed within 3 days of admission: 47.6% (39 people) said no more than 10 minutes passed between first thinking of suicide this time and actually acting. Those for whom the process took longer had clearly higher suicidal intent scores. Impulsivity scale scores were unrelated to the length of the process. 76.8% (63 people) said they had some contact with a partner, family member or friend during this time. The authors concluded: the window for intervention is usually short, professionals have limited room to step in at this stage, so recognizing the signs and how to intervene needs to be spread among ordinary people. A systematic review of 90 follow-up studies: among people seen for self-harm, the median proportion repeating non-fatal self-harm after 1 year was 16%. 2% died by suicide after 1 year. For follow-up of 9 or more years, about 7% died by suicide
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Deisenhammer EA, Ing CM, Strauss R, Kemmler G, Hinterhuber H, Weiss EM (2009). The duration of the suicidal process: how much time is left for intervention between consideration and accomplishment of a suicide attempt? Journal of Clinical Psychiatry, 70(1), 19-24. <https://doi.org/10.4088/JCP.07m03904>；Owens D, Horrocks J, House A (2002). Fatal and non-fatal repetition of self-harm: systematic review. British Journal of Psychiatry, 181, 193-199. <https://doi.org/10.1192/bjp.181.3.193>
- Notes: Why grade B: the Deisenhammer study had only 82 people from one hospital, relying on recall afterwards. The Owens review gives the median proportion from 90 studies with varying methods, not a single pooled figure. The same review also clearly states that people who self-harm still have hundreds of times the suicide risk of the general population. So "the vast majority do not later die by suicide" describes this group as a whole. It cannot be taken as a guarantee for any individual, and treatment should still be sought. For moving lethal means out of reach and helplines, see entry 2 of this section (Yorisoi Hotline). For what to do first when you feel low, see Section 24, entry 5 (move, get sunlight, sleep on schedule).
