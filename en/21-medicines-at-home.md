# 21. Don't let medicines at home harm you

<!-- zh-intro: c2b2a7a2 -->

Fever reducers, painkillers, cold remedies and anti-diarrhoea medicines can mostly be bought without a prescription at drugstores in Japan. Being able to buy them without a prescription does not mean you can take them any way you like. This section carries over the universally applicable entries from the original book's section "don't let medicines at home harm you", choosing only the things where a single action avoids a serious outcome. Entries in the original book based on China's drug regulator's rules were not carried over.

All entries are measured by mortality, including health outcomes such as liver failure, stomach bleeding and kidney failure in newborns. Most benefits are "one fewer serious incident", and research cannot give a figure for "how much less it happens if you do this", so the size of the benefit is judged by the severity of the consequence; each entry's notes say what the judgment is based on. If unsure, ask the pharmacist or registered seller (登録販売者) at the drugstore.

### 1. Before taking two cold remedies or painkillers together, check the ingredient lists: only one of them may contain acetaminophen
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 同时吃两种感冒药或止痛药之前，先看成分表，对乙酰氨基酚只能占一种 | c740dcd2 -->
- Cost: Free. A minute or two to read the ingredient list on each box.
- In plain words: Many cold remedies contain acetaminophen, so taking two boxes together means a double dose, and too much damages the liver. In the US, nearly 30% of patients with acute liver failure caused by it died before they could get a liver transplant. Also do not drink alcohol while taking it.
- Benefit: 22 US hospitals registered 662 consecutive cases of acute liver failure over 6 years. 275 of them (42%) were caused by acetaminophen, with a median dose swallowed of 24 grams. Of these 275, 131 (48%) took too much unintentionally, not on purpose. Of those who took too much unintentionally, 38% were taking two or more medicines containing this ingredient at the same time. Of the 275, 27% died before a liver transplant, and 8% received one. US regulations require these over-the-counter medicines to state three liver-damaging situations as the first warning: exceeding the maximum daily dose, taking them with other medicines containing acetaminophen, and drinking 3 or more alcoholic drinks a day while using them.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Larson AM, Polson J, Fontana RJ, et al.; Acute Liver Failure Study Group (2005). Acetaminophen-induced acute liver failure: results of a United States multicenter, prospective study. Hepatology, 42(6):1364-1372. <https://doi.org/10.1002/hep.20948>；21 CFR 201.326(a)(1)(iii)(A) Liver warning. <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-201/subpart-G/section-201.326>
- Notes: Acetaminophen is also called paracetamol, and on Japanese ingredient lists it is written 「アセトアミノフェン」. If unsure, take all the boxes to the drugstore pharmacist. Graded B because the US figures are a case registry with no control group, so how many incidents reading the ingredient list prevents cannot be calculated. The benefit is rated large by consequence: nearly 30% of acute liver failure cases died outright.

### 2. People over 60, those who have had stomach bleeding, and those taking anticoagulants or steroids should ask a doctor before taking painkillers such as ibuprofen
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 60 岁以上、胃出过血、在吃抗凝药或激素的人，吃布洛芬这类止痛药之前先问医生 | f7e1ea10 -->
- Cost: Free. Ask a doctor or pharmacist once.
- In plain words: Painkillers such as ibuprofen, naproxen and aspirin can damage the stomach. In high-dose trials, people taking ibuprofen had about 4 times the risk of stomach bleeding and perforation of those not taking it. The risk is higher for older people, people who have had stomach bleeding, people also taking anticoagulants or steroids, and people who drink every day.
- Benefit: These medicines are called non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs); common ones are ibuprofen, naproxen and diclofenac. A meta-analysis combined 280 randomized trials of these drugs against placebo, with 124,513 people. Compared with placebo, the risk of upper gastrointestinal perforation, obstruction or bleeding was: ibuprofen RR 3.97 (95% CI 2.22–7.10, about 4 times), naproxen 4.22 (2.71–6.56), diclofenac 1.89 (1.16–3.09). All these drugs roughly doubled the risk of heart failure. US regulations require these over-the-counter medicines to carry a stomach bleeding warning. The warning lists six groups at higher risk: age 60 or over, a history of stomach ulcers or bleeding, taking anticoagulants or steroids, taking other drugs of this type at the same time, drinking 3 or more alcoholic drinks a day while using them, and taking more or for longer than the label says.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Coxib and traditional NSAID Trialists' (CNT) Collaboration; Bhala N, Emberson J, Merhi A, et al. (2013). Vascular and upper gastrointestinal effects of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs: meta-analyses of individual participant data from randomised trials. Lancet, 382(9894):769-779. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(13)60900-9>；21 CFR 201.326(a)(2)(iii)(A) Stomach bleeding warning. <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-201/subpart-G/section-201.326>
- Notes: The paper's conclusions concern high-dose use. It gives no separate figure for the risk of taking them occasionally for a day or two at the label dose. Many combination cold remedies also contain these ingredients, so taking them with a painkiller doubles up; check the ingredient list first. If your doctor has you on long-term low-dose aspirin to prevent clots, do not stop it yourself, and do not add another painkiller on your own.

### 3. After 20 weeks of pregnancy, do not take painkillers such as ibuprofen on your own
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 怀孕 20 周以后，别自己吃布洛芬这类止痛药 | f3d9af06 -->
- Cost: Free.
- In plain words: After 20 weeks of pregnancy, most of the amniotic fluid is made by the baby's kidneys. Taking drugs such as ibuprofen at this stage can harm the baby's kidneys and reduce the amniotic fluid. Among such reports received in the US were cases of newborn deaths. Low-dose aspirin prescribed by a doctor is not included.
- Benefit: The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) issued a safety warning in October 2020. Using NSAIDs from about 20 weeks of pregnancy may cause kidney problems in the unborn baby, leading to low amniotic fluid (oligohydramnios). The warning covers both prescription and over-the-counter drugs, including aspirin, ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac and celecoxib. The FDA reviewed adverse event reports up to July 2017 and found 35 cases of oligohydramnios or newborn kidney dysfunction, all with serious outcomes. Of these, 5 newborns died, all with newborn kidney failure. In the literature, most oligohydramnios resolved within 72 hours to 6 days of stopping the drug. The FDA recommends that pregnant women not use these drugs from 20 weeks unless a doctor specifically advises it. Low-dose aspirin of 81 mg prescribed by a doctor for certain pregnancy problems is not subject to this restriction. When pain or fever relief is needed, the FDA mentions that other drugs such as acetaminophen can be used; ask a pharmacist or doctor first.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: U.S. Food and Drug Administration (2020-10-15). FDA recommends avoiding use of NSAIDs in pregnancy at 20 weeks or later because they can result in low amniotic fluid. Drug Safety Communication. <https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-recommends-avoiding-use-nsaids-pregnancy-20-weeks-or-later-because-they-can-result-low-amniotic>
- Notes: The FDA warns that many over-the-counter medicines for colds, flu and sleep also contain these ingredients, so check the ingredient list. US over-the-counter labels used to warn only about the last 3 months of pregnancy; this moved the line forward to about 20 weeks. Graded B because the figures come from adverse event reports, and how many people took the drugs without problems is unknown. The benefit is rated medium by judgment: the outcome is serious, but it happens rarely.

### 4. Do not ask the doctor for antibiotics for a common cold
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 普通感冒别找医生要抗生素 | 5fd5341a -->
- Cost: Free, and it saves the cost of the medicine.
- In plain words: Taking antibiotics for a cold does not make you better any faster than not taking them. Adults who take them have more than twice the risk of side effects. For a runny nose with pus of less than 10 days, antibiotics also have no definite benefit.
- Benefit: A Cochrane systematic review combined 6 randomized trials with 1,147 people. Comparing antibiotics with placebo for colds, there was no clear difference in the proportion not cured or with persistent symptoms (RR 0.83, 95% CI 0.60–1.14, not significant). The risk of adverse effects was 1.8 times that of the placebo group (95% CI 1.01–3.21). For adults 2.62 times (1.32–5.18); for children there was no increase (0.91, 0.51–1.63). For acute purulent rhinitis, a runny nose with pus for less than 10 days, antibiotics also had no clear benefit (RR 0.73, 0.47–1.13). But adverse effects increased (RR 1.46, 1.10–1.94, about 46% higher). The authors concluded that routine antibiotic use is not recommended in either case.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Kenealy T, Arroll B (2025). Antibiotics for the common cold and acute purulent rhinitis. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 11:CD000247. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000247.pub4>
- Notes: This version was republished in 2025, and the abstract states the literature search ran to 2013. Antibiotics need a doctor's prescription, and do not take antibiotics left over at home on your own either. The benefit is rated medium by judgment: it prevents adverse effects, not death.

### 5. For diarrhoea, first drink oral rehydration solution, and do not give anti-diarrhoea medicine to children under 5
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 拉肚子先喝口服补液盐补水，5 岁以下的孩子不给止泻药 | 4216726f -->
- Cost: A box of oral rehydration salts costs little; keep one at home.
- In plain words: With diarrhoea, the most important thing is replacing fluids, not stopping the diarrhoea. Mix oral rehydration salts with water as the label says and drink it; do not substitute juice drinks, which are too sweet and make dehydration worse. Do not give children under 5 anti-diarrhoea medicines such as loperamide; in serious cases they cause bowel obstruction, which can be fatal.
- Benefit: The World Health Organization's diarrhoea treatment manual states that "anti-diarrhoeal" and anti-vomiting drugs have no practical benefit for children with acute or persistent diarrhoea. They do not prevent dehydration or improve nutrition. Some have dangerous, sometimes fatal side effects, and must never be given to children under 5. Drugs that slow bowel movement, such as loperamide, can cause severe paralytic ileus, which can be fatal, and may also prolong infection. The manual also states that drinks with too much sugar draw water from the body into the bowel, causing hypernatraemic dehydration; examples are soft drinks and commercial fruit juice drinks. The WHO manual recommends the reduced-osmolarity formula, with a total osmolarity of 245 mOsm/L. Compared with the old formula, it reduced unscheduled intravenous fluids by 33%. A Cochrane review combining 8 trials found similar results (OR 0.59, 95% CI 0.45–0.79, about 40% fewer).
- Evidence: A
- Sources: World Health Organization (2005). The treatment of diarrhoea: a manual for physicians and other senior health workers, 4th revision. <https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/43209>；Hahn S, Kim S, Garner P (2002). Reduced osmolarity oral rehydration solution for treating dehydration caused by acute diarrhoea in children. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (1):CD002847. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002847>
- Notes: When buying, check the osmolarity on the label and choose a reduced-osmolarity formula. The manual states that blood in a child's stool is most likely bacterial dysentery, which needs a doctor and antibiotics, not just rehydration at home. The benefit is rated medium by judgment: the figures compare two rehydration solutions, not drinking versus not drinking.

### 6. Do not take painkillers more than 15 days a month, or combination painkillers more than 10, or your headaches may be caused by the medicine
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 止痛药一个月别吃超过 15 天，复方止痛药别超过 10 天，不然头痛可能就是药吃出来的 | 4c63560e -->
- Cost: Free. You need to count the days you take medicine, and resist taking it casually.
- In plain words: If you get frequent headaches and take painkillers every day, the pain may get worse the more you take. The international headache classification calls this medication-overuse headache. Taking ordinary painkillers 15 or more days a month, or combination painkillers 10 or more days, for more than 3 months in a row is enough to qualify. Most people improve after stopping the overused medicine.
- Benefit: The International Headache Society's International Classification of Headache Disorders, 3rd edition, gives the diagnostic criteria. A person with a pre-existing headache disorder has headache on 15 or more days a month, together with regular overuse of headache medicine for more than 3 months. For non-opioid painkillers such as acetaminophen, aspirin and ibuprofen, the overuse threshold is 15 or more days a month. For combination painkillers it is 10 or more days a month. Combination painkillers means two or more types of pain-relieving drugs combined, or with an added ingredient such as caffeine, for example acetaminophen plus codeine. If you rotate several non-opioid painkillers, the days are added together. The classification notes that epidemiological evidence from several countries shows more than half of people with headache on 15 or more days a month have this type of headache. Most patients improve after stopping the overused medicine, and headache prevention treatment also works better.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Headache Classification Committee of the International Headache Society (2018). The International Classification of Headache Disorders, 3rd edition. Cephalalgia, 38(1):1-211. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0333102417738202>；ICHD-3 online version 8.2 Medication-overuse headache (including 8.2.3, 8.2.5). <https://ichd-3.org/8-headache-attributed-to-a-substance-or-its-withdrawal/8-2-medication-overuse-headache-moh/>
- Notes: Painkillers with caffeine (カフェイン) in the ingredient list generally count as combination products, with the 10-day limit. If the number of headache days in a month keeps rising, see a neurologist rather than just adding more medicine. Graded B because the classification gives diagnostic thresholds, not controlled trials. The benefit is rated medium by judgment: what it gains is fewer days with headache, not involving death.

### 7. If you spend over 12,000 yen a year on over-the-counter medicine, keep the receipts and deduct it from income under the self-medication tax system
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 一年买非处方药超过 12,000 日元的，留好收据，用「セルフメディケーション税制」从所得里扣 | bfe15ee8 -->
- Cost: Free. Keep your drugstore receipts and enter them when you file your final tax return the next year. The condition is that you had a company health check, specific health checkup or municipal screening that year.
- In plain words: For eligible over-the-counter medicines bought in a year by you and the family members you share a livelihood with, the amount above 12,000 yen can be deducted from income, up to 88,000 yen. The condition is that you had a check-up or similar that year. You can use either this or the ordinary medical expense deduction, not both.
- Benefit: The National Tax Agency states: residents who have made "certain efforts" to maintain health and prevent illness and who paid for specified over-the-counter medicines for themselves or a spouse or relatives sharing their livelihood can receive an income deduction (a special case of the medical expense deduction, セルフメディケーション税制). The deduction is the total actually paid for specified over-the-counter medicines (excluding amounts reimbursed by insurance and the like) minus 12,000 yen, up to 88,000 yen. "Certain efforts" include health checkups by insurers (comprehensive medical checkups, various screenings and the like) and health checkups by municipalities as health promotion programs. This special case cannot be used together with the ordinary medical expense deduction, and once chosen, the choice cannot be changed by a request for correction or an amended return
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 国税庁「No.1129 特定一般用医薬品等購入費を支払ったとき（医療費控除の特例）【セルフメディケーション税制】」（令和 8 年 4 月 1 日現在法令等）. <https://www.nta.go.jp/taxes/shiraberu/taxanswer/shotoku/1129.htm>
- Notes: As of 2026-10-04. Not all over-the-counter medicines count; for which are eligible, see the National Tax Agency's page "No.1132". If you pay a lot for medical care, the ordinary medical expense deduction is better value; see Section 6, entry 4 (medical expense deduction). It is deducted from income, so the tax saved depends on your rate and is typically a few thousand yen, so the benefit is rated small.

### 8. Do not drink alcohol while taking antibacterials such as cephalosporins or metronidazole and for 7 days after stopping, and avoid medicines containing alcohol
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 用头孢、甲硝唑这类抗菌药期间和停药后 7 天内不喝酒，含酒精的药也不碰 | d966f26d -->
- Cost: Free. Go without alcohol for a week or so, and before taking other medicines check whether the ingredients include ethanol (on Japanese ingredient lists, エタノール or アルコール).
- In plain words: Some cephalosporins and drugs such as metronidazole react with alcohol, causing flushing, a racing heart, chest tightness and vomiting, and in serious cases a drop in blood pressure. Not only drinks: medicines containing alcohol, such as cough syrups, can cause it too. Avoid them while taking the drug and for 7 days after stopping.
- Benefit: This is called a disulfiram-like reaction. A 2023 safe-medication explainer from the Fujian Provincial Medical Products Administration (China) says that drinking before or after taking some cephalosporins may cause this reaction, which "can seriously harm health and even endanger life". Symptoms include facial flushing, sweating and palpitations, and even breathing difficulty, falling blood pressure and anaphylactic shock. Drugs the explainer lists, following China's Pharmacopoeia Clinical Drug Use Guide, include cefazolin, ceftriaxone, cefoperazone, metronidazole, tinidazole and furazolidone. The explainer states "no alcohol, and no medicines or foods containing alcohol, within 7 days after taking the drug", with examples such as liqueur chocolates and Huoxiang Zhengqi Shui (藿香正気水, a Chinese herbal liquid). The reason is that the enzyme the drug inhibits often takes 4 to 5 days to recover. A hospital in Guangzhou compiled 82 cases over 2 years. 57 (69.51%) were caused by cephalosporins, and 10 (12.20%) by nitroimidazoles (such as metronidazole and tinidazole). The alcohol came from alcoholic drinks in 40 cases (48.78%), and from medicines containing ethanol in 31 (37.80%). Oral ones included Huoxiang Zhengqi Shui, cough syrups and motherwort liquid extract. Of the 82, 68 were mild, 9 moderate and 5 severe, all resolved within 1 to 3 hours, with no deaths. The US metronidazole label requires no alcohol, and no products containing propylene glycol, during treatment and for at least 3 days after stopping.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: 福建省药品监督管理局（福建省药品科普与监管数据中心）(2023). 2023年“全国安全用药月”原创科普小知识——为什么吃头孢不能喝酒. <https://yjj.scjgj.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/kpzl/qgaqyyy/202312/t20231215_6334595.htm>；房财富, 涂星, 刘韬, 黄红兵, 邱孟, 梁蔚婷 (2015). 我院双硫仑样反应82例分析. 中国药房, 26(29):4089-4091. <https://doi.org/10.6039/j.issn.1001-0408.2015.29.19>；Pfizer (2026). FLAGYL 375 (metronidazole) capsules, prescribing information: Contraindications, Interaction with Alcohol. DailyMed. <https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a2883ca1-5a9a-4259-9d80-46ab67274384>
- Notes: Not every cephalosporin does this, but ordinary people cannot tell them apart, so following this entry for all of them is simplest. When a doctor prescribes a cephalosporin or metronidazole, mention whether you have drunk alcohol in the last few days. The explainer suggests not drinking for 3 days before starting either, while admitting there is no authoritative guidance on this interval. Check the ingredients of medicines brought from your country of origin too; Huoxiang Zhengqi Shui, for example, contains alcohol, so avoid it while on antibacterials. If you start flushing and your heart races, stop anything containing alcohol at once. The explainer says mild cases resolve on their own, and severe cases need hospital. Graded B because the evidence is case series and drug labels, with no control. The benefit is rated small: all 82 cases resolved within hours with no deaths, and only 5 were severe.
