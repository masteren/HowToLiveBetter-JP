# 23. After a major loss or shock

<!-- zh-intro: 4f55a8cf -->

The days and months after a loved one's death, a serious diagnosis, the loss of a spouse or the death of a parent are when the body and judgment are most likely to fail. This section carries over the universally applicable entries from the original book's section "after a major blow", removing helplines, medical insurance and community services specific to China. For helplines in Japan where you can ask for help in Chinese, see Section 1, entry 2 (Yorisoi Hotline). For funeral procedures and the money you can claim, see Section 16, entry 3 (burial allowance or funeral expenses).

### 1. In the first days after a loved one dies, do not stay alone; if you have heart disease, high blood pressure or diabetes, keep taking your medicine, and call 119 for chest pressure or pain
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 亲人刚走的那几天别一个人待着，有心脏病、高血压、糖尿病的照常吃药，胸口压着疼就叫 119 | a58e6525 -->
- Cost: Free. Do not stay alone during these days, and keep taking your medicine.
- In plain words: On the first day after a loved one dies, the chance of a heart attack is 21.1 times normal, and it falls day by day after that. People aged 60 to 89 have roughly double the rate of heart attack or stroke in the 30 days after their partner dies, falling back after a month. People who already have cardiovascular disease should not stop their medicine during these days. For chest pressure or pain, shortness of breath, or weakness on one side, call 119 at once.
- Benefit: 1,985 patients with acute myocardial infarction, each compared with themselves across different time windows before onset. 270 of them (13.6%) had lost someone important in the 6 months before onset, and 19 within 1 day before the heart attack. In the 24 hours after an important person's death, the rate of acute myocardial infarction rose 21.1-fold (95% CI 13.1–34.1, meaning the true multiple is roughly between 13 and 34). It fell day by day after that. In actual numbers: among people with a 5% ten-year heart attack risk, 1 extra case per 1,394 newly bereaved. Among people with a 20% risk, 1 extra case per 320. Another UK study compared 30,447 bereaved people aged 60 to 89 with 83,588 matched controls. Within 30 days of the partner's death, heart attack or stroke occurred in 50 bereaved people (0.16%). And in 67 controls (0.08%). IRR 2.20 (1.52–3.15), meaning about 2.2 times the controls' chance. For heart attack 2.14 (1.20–3.81), about 2.1 times. For stroke 2.40 (1.22–4.71), about 2.4 times. The effect weakened after 30 days. Two more conditions were examined within 90 days. One was non-MI acute coronary syndrome (an acute problem with the heart's blood supply that falls short of a heart attack), IRR 2.20 (1.12–4.29). The other was pulmonary embolism (a clot blocking blood vessels in the lungs), IRR 2.37 (1.18–4.75)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Mostofsky E, et al. (2012). Risk of acute myocardial infarction after the death of a significant person in one's life: the Determinants of Myocardial Infarction Onset Study. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.111.061770>；Carey IM, et al. (2014). Increased risk of acute cardiovascular events after partner bereavement: a matched cohort study. JAMA Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2013.14558>
- Notes: Hand your pill box to someone else to manage; do not rely on your own memory. Avoid long drives during these days. Do not get through it with alcohol. For how to recognize a heart attack or stroke and call an ambulance, see Section 3, entry 2 (the mouth suddenly droops) and Section 3, entry 3 (chest pressure or pain). Both studies look back at population data and cannot tell how many people would have had the event anyway. But both point in the same direction over the same period. If there is no one to hand your pill box to, you can call a helpline where Chinese is spoken; see Section 1, entry 2 (Yorisoi Hotline).

### 2. In the week you are told of a serious diagnosis, do not go alone to get the results, and put off decisions other than treatment
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 被告知重病诊断的那一周，别一个人去拿报告，治疗以外的决定往后放 | 1a0f3435 -->
- Cost: Free. Have someone go with you to get the results. Put off big decisions other than treatment for a few days.
- In plain words: In the first week after a cancer diagnosis, the chance of suicide is 12.6 times that of people without a diagnosis, and of cardiovascular death 5.6 times. By the end of the first year, the suicide risk falls to 3.1 times, more marked for harder-to-treat cancers. So bring someone the day you get the results, and do not be alone at home. Things like quitting your job or selling your home can wait until the next appointment.
- Benefit: Swedish national registers followed over 6.07 million people (6,073,240), from 1991 to 2006. Compared with people without a cancer diagnosis, the relative risk of suicide in the first week after diagnosis was 12.6 (95% CI 8.6–17.8), about 12.6 times. The true multiple is roughly between 8.6 and 17.8. The rate in this week was 2.50 per 1,000 person-years, meaning 2.5 cases if 1,000 people were followed for a year. In the first year it fell to 3.1 (2.7–3.5, 0.60 per 1,000 person-years), about 3.1 times. Cardiovascular death in the first week was 5.6 (5.2–5.9, 116.80 per 1,000 person-years), about 5.6 times. In the first 4 weeks 3.3 (3.1–3.4, 65.81 per 1,000 person-years). The raised risk fell back quickly within the first year after diagnosis. The harder the cancer is to treat and the lower the chance of survival, the more marked the rise. Comparing the same patients with themselves across time windows gave the same conclusion
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Fang F, et al. (2012). Suicide and cardiovascular death after a cancer diagnosis. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1110307>
- Notes: These are data on cancer patients. For other serious diagnoses there is no original evidence of equal weight. Very few people are actually affected: the first-week suicide rate is 2.50 per 1,000 person-years, 2.5 cases if 1,000 people were followed for a year. It is written here not to frighten but to show that someone should be with you "in the first days after you find out". For caps on medical costs, see Section 6, entry 1 (high-cost medical expense benefit).

### 3. If a loved one died by suicide, accident or homicide, do not try to tough it out; actively seek professional help
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 亲人死于自杀、意外或凶案的，别指望硬扛，主动去找专业帮助 | 7add4e72 -->
- Cost: Calling a helpline is free. To see a doctor, go to psychiatry or psychosomatic medicine, paying your health insurance share. The hard part is reaching out yourself.
- In plain words: People whose spouse died by suicide are about 80% (men) and 70% (women) more likely than the general population to be diagnosed with a mental disorder within five years. Compared with people whose spouse died of other causes, they are also 70% to 100% more likely. People bereaved this way more often feel shunned and ashamed. That is exactly why you should reach out, rather than waiting to get better on your own.
- Benefit: Danish national registers followed 6.7 million adults from 1980 to 2014, over 136 million person-years in total (the years each person was followed, added up). For people widowed by a spouse's suicide, the risk of a new mental disorder diagnosis within 5 years compared with the general population was IRR 1.8 for men (95% CI 1.6–2.0), about 80% higher. For women 1.7 (1.6–1.8), about 70% higher. Compared with people widowed by other causes of death, IRR was 1.7 for men (1.5–1.9) and 2.0 for women (1.9–2.2), 70% to 100% higher. In this group, suicidal behaviour, mortality and the need for municipal assistance were also higher. Risks of physical conditions such as liver cirrhosis and sleep disorders were also raised. A further review summarized 57 studies one by one, without pooling the numbers. Suicide bereavement was associated with the following four things. First, a raised risk of suicide in partners. Second, a raised risk of psychiatric admission for parents after a child's suicide. Third, a raised risk of suicide for mothers after an adult child's suicide. Fourth, a raised risk of depression for children after a parent's suicide. Compared with people bereaved by other violent deaths, people bereaved by suicide more often reported feeling shunned and ashamed
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Erlangsen A, et al. (2017). Association Between Spousal Suicide and Mental, Physical, and Social Health Outcomes: A Longitudinal and Nationwide Register-Based Study. JAMA Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2017.0226>；Pitman A, et al. (2014). Effects of suicide bereavement on mental health and suicide risk. The Lancet Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(14)70224-X>；Kristensen P, et al. (2012). Bereavement and mental health after sudden and violent losses: a review. Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1521/psyc.2012.75.1.76> (the review in the notes)
- Notes: Sudden and violent deaths means accidents, homicides and disasters. After such deaths, rates of post-traumatic stress disorder, depression and grief getting stuck are all higher than after death from illness. Recovery is also slower. If you have thoughts of ending your life, call a helpline where Chinese is spoken; see Section 1, entry 2 (Yorisoi Hotline).

### 4. In the first six months after losing a spouse, put one specific person in charge of keeping an eye on meals, sleep, chronic-illness medicine and check-ups
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 丧偶后的头半年，把三餐、睡觉、慢性病的药和复查交给一个固定的人盯着 | bab0c541 -->
- Cost: Free. Find a specific person, whether a child, sibling or friend, to visit or call regularly. The hard part is asking someone to take this on.
- In plain words: In the first six months after losing a spouse, the chance of dying in that period is about 40% higher than for people who have not lost a spouse. After six months it falls to about 15% higher. Men are about 23% higher; for women the figure is so small it may be just chance. This gap is not something you get through by staying positive. What helps is putting medicine, meals and check-ups in the hands of one specific person, rather than relying on your own memory.
- Benefit: 15 studies that registered people first and then followed them, 2,263,888 people combined. In the first 6 months of widowhood, mortality RR was 1.41 (95% CI 1.26–1.57), meaning the chance of dying in that period was about 40% higher than for people not widowed. After 6 months it was 1.14 (1.10–1.18), falling to about 15% higher. Men RR 1.23 (1.18–1.28), about 23% higher. Women 1.04 (1.00–1.08, not statistically significant, meaning this small difference may be just chance). No clear difference was seen between under 65 and 65 or over. Another analysis combined 123 papers, 1,377 risk estimates and over 500 million people. After adjusting for differences in conditions, HR was 1.23 (1.19–1.28), about 23% higher overall. Men were also higher than women. Men 1.27 (1.19–1.35), 27% higher. Women 1.15 (1.08–1.22), 15% higher
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Moon JR, et al. (2011). Widowhood and mortality: a meta-analysis. PLoS ONE. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0023465>；Shor E, et al. (2012). Widowhood and mortality: a meta-analysis and meta-regression. Demography. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s13524-012-0096-x>
- Notes: These studies look back at population data, and other factors mixed in cannot be removed. Couples' lifestyles and illnesses are similar to begin with. But the pattern of "much higher in the first six months than later" is the same in two independent pooled analyses, so it is worth treating as a window for looking after someone. Men are at higher risk and also less likely to ask for help. If you cannot find such a person, you can call a helpline where Chinese is spoken; see Section 1, entry 2 (Yorisoi Hotline).

### 5. After a parent dies, tell the child the truth, let them take part in the farewell, and do not send them elsewhere "for a change of scene"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 家长去世后，如实告诉孩子、让他参加告别、别送去别处「换个环境」 | 40ce4a8f -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is explaining things clearly to the child while you are suffering yourself.
- In plain words: Children who lose a father or mother before 18 are about 50% more likely to die by early adulthood than children who have not. Where the parent died by accident or suicide it is about 80% higher, and by illness about 30% higher. What the family can do is tell the child the truth, let them take part in the farewell, not interrupt their schooling, and not send them to relatives for a change of scene.
- Benefit: Everyone born in Denmark from 1968 to 2008 and in Sweden from 1973 to 2006 was included. Plus an 89.3% sample of people born in Finland from 1987 to 2007. Over 7.3 million people in total. 189,094 of them lost a parent before 18. These children's all-cause mortality was 50% higher than that of people not bereaved (mortality rate ratio 1.50, 95% CI 1.43–1.58, about 1.5 times). Where the parent died of unnatural causes such as accident or suicide it was 1.84 (1.71–2.00), about 80% higher. Where the parent died of natural causes such as illness it was 1.33 (1.24–1.41), about 30% higher. It was highest when the child's cause of death was of the same type as the parent's
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Li J, et al. (2014). Mortality after parental death in childhood: a nationwide cohort study from three Nordic countries. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001679>
- Notes: When telling the child, do not make up "they've gone on a long trip", and put the custody and care arrangements in writing. Grade B is not because the figure is weak. This is a follow-up of the whole population of three countries, and the figure itself is good enough for grade A. It is B because there is no direct trial evidence between this figure and specific practices such as "how to tell the child, and whether they should attend the farewell". The authors also note that data on parent-child relationships and the child's lifestyle after bereavement are lacking. The rise includes both inherited constitution and the long-term effects of bereavement. If the child is not a Japanese national, ask immigration how their residence will be arranged; see Section 2, entry 11 (immigration's Information Center).

### 6. If after six months your grief is still stuck and you cannot get on with life, book an appointment in psychiatry or clinical psychology
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 哀伤过了半年还在原地、日子过不下去，去精神科或临床心理科挂号 | 46fcb5ae -->
- Cost: The consultation fee at your health insurance share. This kind of treatment is given as a course; in the trial it averaged 16 sessions over 19 weeks. The hard part is taking the step to book it yourself.
- In plain words: About one in ten bereaved adults gets stuck. After more than six months they still long for the person so much they cannot eat, work or look after the children, feel numb, and feel life has no meaning. This is not a lack of strength; it has diagnostic criteria and a specific treatment. The response rate for targeted treatment is 51%, versus 28% for ordinary psychotherapy, and it also works faster.
- Benefit: 14 studies combined, covering adults whose loved one did not die violently and who were not themselves in psychiatric care. The prevalence of prolonged grief disorder was 9.8% (95% CI 6.8–14.0), about 10 in every 100 bereaved adults. The older the person, the higher the proportion. Another study tested how accurate these diagnostic criteria are, interviewing 291 bereaved people three times, at 0–6, 6–12 and 12–24 months after the loss. The core feature of the diagnosis is intense longing for the deceased. Plus at least five of the following nine. The first five are emotional numbness, being stunned, feeling life has no meaning, distrust of others, and bitterness over the loss. The last four are difficulty accepting the loss, confusion about one's role in life, avoiding the reality, and inability to move on with life. These must occur every day, or be severe enough to stop you functioning. They must also still be present at least 6 months after the death and be affecting daily life. There was also a trial randomizing 95 people into two groups, with 16 sessions over an average of 19 weeks. The response rate for treatment targeted at complicated grief was 51%. For ordinary interpersonal psychotherapy 28% (P=0.02, a difference unlikely to be chance). The former also worked faster. The number needed to treat was 4.3, meaning one extra person improved for every 4.3 treated
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Lundorff M, et al. (2017). Prevalence of prolonged grief disorder in adult bereavement: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Affective Disorders. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2017.01.030>；Prigerson HG, et al. (2009). Prolonged grief disorder: Psychometric validation of criteria proposed for DSM-V and ICD-11. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000121>；Shear MK, et al. (2005). Treatment of complicated grief: a randomized controlled trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.293.21.2601>
- Notes: Do not hold yourself to a number of months. The real test is "it affects eating, sleeping, working or caring for children"; if that fits, book an appointment. In Japan, the departments are called psychiatry (精神科) or psychosomatic medicine (心療内科). This diagnosis is called complicated grief in some sources and prolonged grief disorder in others. Different criteria also require different durations, some 6 months and some 12. The 9.8% figure comes from 14 studies with quite different methods, and the authors also caution that it may not transfer to other populations.

### 7. Do not start by paying for grief counselling; first check whether your grief is really stuck (the symptoms in entry 6)
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 别一上来就花钱做哀伤咨询，先看自己的哀伤是不是真的卡住了（第 6 条那几条症状） | 74caceaa -->
- Cost: Free, and it saves you a counselling fee.
- In plain words: Arranging counselling for everyone who is bereaved does very little good, and the effect disappears after a while, because most people would recover on their own. But for people who clearly cannot adjust, treatment works as well as treatment for other psychological problems. So first check against entry 6 (book psychiatry when grief gets stuck), and only spend the money if it fits.
- Benefit: 61 controlled studies combined. Psychological interventions given to all bereaved people had a very small benefit right after treatment, and no benefit could be seen at later follow-up. Interventions that only enrolled bereaved people who were "already clearly not adjusting" were as effective as psychotherapy for other psychological problems. Studies without prior screening had poor results, because the group without intervention also recovered on its own over time. A further 14 randomized trials combined: treatment for people who already had problems was clearly better than control both after treatment and at follow-up, with the effect growing over time. Preventive interventions given to everyone who did not yet have problems did not work. Another study followed 205 people from before losing their spouse to 6 and 18 months after. It identified five trajectories. Common grief, chronic grief, chronic depression, improvement during bereavement, and resilience (coping well). Of these, the resilient pattern was the most common, and common grief was relatively rare
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Currier JM, et al. (2008). The effectiveness of psychotherapeutic interventions for bereaved persons: a comprehensive quantitative review. Psychological Bulletin. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.134.5.648>；Wittouck C, et al. (2011). The prevention and treatment of complicated grief: a meta-analysis. Clinical Psychology Review. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2010.09.005>；Bonanno GA, et al. (2002). Resilience to loss and chronic grief: a prospective study from preloss to 18-months postloss. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.83.5.1150>；Stroebe M, et al. (2007). Health outcomes of bereavement. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(07)61816-9> (the review in the notes)
- Notes: The point is "not everyone needs it", not "nobody should have it". The high-risk bereaved in entry 3 (a loved one died by suicide, accident or homicide) and those already stuck in entry 6 (book psychiatry when grief gets stuck) are exactly the people the evidence says should have it. A Lancet review reaches the same conclusion: grief is not an illness, and most people do not need professional intervention. Effort should go to those at high risk. And to those who already have complicated grief, post-bereavement depression or stress disorders.

### 8. In the first three months after a loss, postpone every big irreversible decision
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 变故后的头三个月，凡是不可逆的大决定一律往后推 | 2a66cd01 -->
- Cost: Free; you only postpone decisions, possibly missing some opportunities. The hard part is that at the time it feels like the opportunity will not wait.
- In plain words: During this time your judgment of all kinds of "opportunities" is at its worst. People claiming they can contact the deceased, offering to win your money back, urging you to quit and start over somewhere else, or pulling you into a partnership will all come to you. Anything costing more than a month's income, or very costly to undo, gets postponed three months. First talk it over with someone who has no stake in it, and do not decide on the same day.
- Benefit: It avoids making irreversible decisions about property during the period right after a blow, when judgment is at its worst. Such decisions include selling a home, quitting a job, investing a survivor's payment or compensation all at once, remarrying in haste, and guaranteeing someone else's debt. The amounts involved are usually from hundreds of thousands of yen up to the value of a home, so the benefit is rated medium
- Evidence: C
- Sources: Author's experience; no direct literature.
- Notes: If you have no one around without a stake in it, call a helpline and talk it through; see Section 1, entry 2 (Yorisoi Hotline), or write it down and reread it three days later. "Three months" is a line the author drew; no literature supports that specific length, so it is grade C. Some money you should actively claim, and it is not among the things to postpone: burial allowance, survivors' pension and the like; see Section 16, entry 3 (burial allowance or funeral expenses). For the pitfalls of guarantees, see Section 8, entry 1 (do not readily act as a friend's rental guarantor).
