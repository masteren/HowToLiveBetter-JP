# 13. School-age children: enrolment, falling behind, and high school fees

<!-- zh-intro: 44ceca5d -->

This section covers the key things about foreign children going to school in Japan: whether they can attend public schools for free, what to do if their Japanese is not good enough, and which statuses of residence qualify for high school tuition support. MEXT information is as of 2026-10-03. The entries on children's emergencies, myopia, mental health and discipline are carried over from the original book's section "school-age children", with the research figures unchanged. Bullying and corporal punishment have been replaced with Japan's Act for the Promotion of Measures to Prevent Bullying (いじめ防止対策推進法), the Child Abuse Prevention Act and the Civil Code.

### 1. To send a foreign child to a public primary or junior high school, apply to the municipal board of education; tuition and textbooks are free, as for Japanese children
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 外国籍孩子想上公立中小学，向市区町村教育委员会申请，学费和教科书和日本孩子一样免费 | a1a44619 -->
- Cost: Free. Complete the enrolment procedure at your municipality's board of education. If the family is struggling financially, ask about school attendance assistance at the same time.
- In plain words: Japan does not require foreign parents to send their children to school. But if a child wants to attend a public primary or junior high school, the school accepts them free of charge as it does Japanese children, and textbooks are free too. Families in hardship can also apply for school attendance assistance (就学援助) for school supplies, lunch fees, school trips and so on, foreign children included. The board of education sends an enrolment notice based on resident registration.
- Benefit: MEXT states: in Japan, parents of foreign children have no obligation to send them to school; but those wishing to attend public compulsory education schools are accepted free of charge, as Japanese children are, based on international human rights covenants and the like. MEXT's guidelines require boards of education to introduce schooling for foreign children, together with the school attendance assistance system, through publicity and information sessions, and to send enrolment notices with the enrolment procedure based on information in the Basic Resident Register, so that school-age foreign children do not miss the chance to attend school. The obligation to send children to school under Article 17 of the School Education Act applies to Japanese nationals and not to parents of foreign children. MEXT's report materials state that equal educational opportunities with Japanese children, including free textbooks and school attendance assistance, are guaranteed for foreign children
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 文部科学省「就学事務Ｑ＆Ａ 13. 外国人の子等の就学に関する手続について」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/shotou/shugaku/detail/1422256.htm> ; 文部科学省「外国人の子供の就学促進及び就学状況の把握等に関する指針」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/shotou/clarinet/004/1415154_00003.htm> ; 文部科学省「外国人の子どもの公立義務教育諸学校への受入について」（外国人児童生徒教育の充実方策について 参考資料）. <https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/shingi/chousa/shotou/042/houkoku/08070301/009/005.htm>
- Notes: The benefit is rated large compared with international or private school fees of several hundred thousand to over a million yen a year. If your child attends a foreign or international school, check eligibility for entering a Japanese high school well in advance.

### 2. From fiscal 2026, high school tuition support has no income limit, up to 457,200 yen a year at private schools, but for foreign students it depends on status of residence
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 高中学费支援 2026 年度起取消收入限制，私立最高每年 457,200 日元，但外国籍学生要看在留资格 | b2db167b -->
- Cost: Free. After enrolling, submit the eligibility application as the school instructs, attaching a copy of your residence record or residence card.
- In plain words: From fiscal 2026, high school tuition support does not depend on income, covering up to 457,200 yen a year at full-time private schools, with nothing to repay. Foreign students must have certain statuses: Special Permanent Resident, Permanent Resident, Spouse or Child of a Japanese National or Permanent Resident, and Long-Term Residents and Dependents who meet the conditions. Others have a separate income-tested support route.
- Benefit: MEXT's leaflet "for people without Japanese nationality" states: from fiscal 2026 the scope of high school tuition support is expanded, with nothing to repay. Examples of annual payment caps: 115,200 yen at national full-time high schools, 118,800 yen at public full-time high schools, 457,200 yen at private full-time high schools, 337,200 yen at private correspondence high schools; there is no income cap. The status requirement for foreign students is attending a high school or similar (excluding foreign schools), having an address in Japan, and holding one of the following: Special Permanent Resident; Permanent Resident; Spouse or Child of Japanese National; Spouse or Child of Permanent Resident; Long-Term Resident recognized as intending to settle permanently; Dependent who completed Japanese primary and junior high school and is recognized as intending to work and settle in Japan after high school. Dependents must also submit a copy of their Japanese primary and junior high school diplomas or proof of graduation. MEXT's procedure flowchart for foreign new students shows that those outside these groups follow a separate income-tested support route
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 文部科学省「高等学校等就学支援金・新制度【日本国籍以外の方用】リーフレット」（2026-06）. <https://www.mext.go.jp/content/20260616-mxt_shuukyo03-100002595_3.pdf> ; 文部科学省「令和8年度における申請・認定手続き（イメージ）【日本国籍以外の新入生用】」. <https://www.mext.go.jp/content/20260616-mxt_shuukyo03-100002595_10.pdf>
- Notes: The benefit is rated large at up to over a million yen over three years of private high school. For amounts and conditions on the income-tested route, follow MEXT and your prefecture. Before the payment decision, some schools collect tuition first and refund it later.

### 3. If your child says they have sudden severe pain that keeps getting worse, go to hospital at once; do not let them endure it until school is over
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 孩子说突然剧痛、而且越来越重，立刻去医院，不要让他忍到放学 | 9c69916e -->
- Cost: Free. Take half a day off and bring your child to the emergency department.
- In plain words: Some conditions are counted in hours. Sudden severe pain on one side of a boy's scrotum is testicular torsion: the blood vessel attached to the testicle twists and cuts off the blood supply. Treated within 6 hours, 97.2% of testicles are saved. Delayed to 19 to 24 hours, only 42.5%. This pain cannot wait until the end of school or the end of an exam.
- Benefit: A systematic collection of 30 case series with 2,116 testicular torsion patients, analysed together. 1,283 were counted in 6-hour bands. From the onset of pain, the testicle salvage rate was 97.2% at 0 to 6 hours. 79.3% at 7 to 12 hours. 61.3% at 13 to 18 hours. 42.5% at 19 to 24 hours. 24.4% at 25 to 48 hours. 7.4% beyond 48 hours. In larger bands: 90.4% within 12 hours, 54.0% at 13 to 24 hours, 18.1% beyond 24 hours. The authors concluded that the window for salvage can be longer than the usually cited 6 to 8 hours. So even if the pain has lasted a long time, still get it treated; do not skip it because "it's too late anyway"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Mellick LB, et al. (2019). A Systematic Review of Testicle Survival Time After a Torsion Event. Pediatric Emergency Care. <https://doi.org/10.1097/PEC.0000000000001287>
- Notes: Tell boys in advance: if you suddenly get severe pain down there, tell us at once; there's nothing to be embarrassed about. Testicular torsion mostly affects adolescent boys, and many endure it because it is hard to talk about. Other conditions counted in hours include acute appendicitis (which perforates if left too long), diabetic ketoacidosis and meningitis. This section has not checked, condition by condition, "how much worse each hour of delay makes it". Their common signal: sudden onset, steadily worsening, unlike any previous episode. If unsure whether to call an ambulance, call #8000; see Section 3, entry 39 (call #8000 for children).

### 4. Treat myopia as lasting damage: the stronger the prescription, the more retinal detachment, macular degeneration and glaucoma later
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 把近视当成会留下的损伤：度数越深，将来视网膜脱离、黄斑病变、青光眼越多 | 32ff5ab8 -->
- Cost: Free. Nothing to do in itself; what to do is in entry 7 of this section (2 hours outdoors a day).
- In plain words: Myopia (short-sightedness) is not just "wearing glasses". Compared with people without myopia, the chance of myopic macular degeneration is over ten times higher for mild myopia and hundreds of times higher for −6 dioptres or more. For retinal detachment, about 3 times higher for mild and about 13 times for −6 or more. So the earlier a child becomes short-sighted and the faster it progresses, the greater the risk they carry later.
- Benefit: A systematic review and meta-analysis combined studies up to June 2019, in three bands: mild (−0.5 to −3.00 D), moderate (−3.00 to −6.00 D) and high (≤−6.00 D). Compared with people without myopia, the odds ratios for myopic macular degeneration were 13.57 (95% CI 6.18 to 29.79), 72.74 (33.18 to 159.48) and 845.08 (230.05 to 3104.34) respectively. For retinal detachment 3.15 (1.92 to 5.17), 8.74 (7.28 to 10.50) and 12.62 (6.65 to 23.94). For posterior subcapsular cataract 1.56 (1.32 to 1.84), 2.55 (1.98 to 3.28) and 4.55 (2.66 to 7.75). For open-angle glaucoma, 1.59 (1.33 to 1.91) for mild, and 2.92 (1.89 to 4.52) for moderate and high combined. Odds ratios for visual impairment over age 60 were 1.71 (1.07 to 2.74), 5.54 (3.12 to 9.85) and 87.63 (34.50 to 222.58) for the three bands
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Haarman AEG, Enthoven CA, Tideman JWL, Tedja MS, Verhoeven VJM, Klaver CCW (2020). The complications of myopia: a review and meta-analysis. Investigative Ophthalmology & Visual Science, 61(4), 49. <https://doi.org/10.1167/iovs.61.4.49>
- Notes: All the studies combined are observational, and the odds ratios are unusually large. For high myopia and macular degeneration, the confidence range runs from 230 to 3104, showing large differences between the underlying studies. These numbers only show orders of magnitude and should not be used as precise values. Most of these complications only appear in middle and old age. Myopia itself is not a disability, and most people see normally with correction. But myopia does not end with a pair of glasses, and delaying it is worth the effort. If a school check-up finds poor eyesight, take your child to an eye doctor once, and get glasses and follow-ups as the eye doctor advises.

### 5. If your child is bullied, report it to the school that day and ask the school to investigate under the Act for the Promotion of Measures to Prevent Bullying; for assault or extortion, the school must work with the police
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 孩子被欺凌，当天就报到学校，要求学校按いじめ防止対策推進法调查；打人、抢钱的，学校要和警察联手 | 5c4f5863 -->
- Cost: Free. Go to the homeroom teacher and to the teacher in charge of bullying at the school. The hard part is resisting advice like "don't make a big deal of it". If your child wants to talk to someone themselves, the 24-hour Children's SOS Dial (子供SOSダイヤル) is free.
- In plain words: Children bullied by peers are about 2.2 times as likely to have suicidal thoughts as those not bullied, and about 2.6 times as likely to have attempted suicide. Japanese law requires a school that receives a report to find out promptly whether bullying is happening. Once confirmed, it must stop it and prevent recurrence, and work with the police where it should be handled as a crime. So report it the same day and keep a written record.
- Benefit: 34 studies with 284,375 children and adolescents combined. Being bullied by peers was associated with suicidal thoughts, OR 2.23 (95% CI 2.10–2.37), about 2.2 times the chance of those not bullied. Another 9 studies with 70,102 people: being bullied was associated with suicide attempts, OR 2.55 (1.95–3.34), about 2.6 times. Results were the same across sex, age and study quality. The association between cyberbullying and suicidal thoughts was stronger than for offline bullying. Article 2 of the Act for the Promotion of Measures to Prevent Bullying defines bullying as acts (including online) by other students with whom the student has a certain relationship, such as at the same school, that have a psychological or physical effect and cause the student physical or mental suffering. Article 23 of that Act provides: on receiving a report, the school must promptly confirm whether bullying is occurring; once confirmed, it must stop it, prevent recurrence and keep supporting the bullied student; where it should be handled as a crime, it must work with the local police station; where the student's life, body or property may suffer serious harm, it must notify the police station immediately. Article 28 of that Act provides: suspicion of serious harm to life, body, mind or property from bullying, or of being forced into long absence from school, is a "serious situation", and the school or its operator must set up a body to investigate and explain the facts to the bullied student and parents. MEXT's 24-hour Children's SOS Dial, 0120-0-78310, can be called by children and parents 24 hours a day
- Evidence: A
- Sources: van Geel M, et al. (2014). Relationship between peer victimization, cyberbullying, and suicide in children and adolescents: a meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2013.4143>；いじめ防止対策推進法 第 2 条、第 23 条、第 28 条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/425AC1000000071>；文部科学省「24 じかんに相談できる窓口」. <https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/shotou/seitoshidou/06112216.html>；こども家庭庁「児童相談所虐待対応ダイヤル「189」について」（other consultation services column）. <https://www.cfa.go.jp/policies/jidougyakutai/gyakutai-taiou-dial>
- Notes: This pooled analysis only tracks and records, without randomized comparison. So it cannot prove bullying alone causes suicide, but the direction and size of the risk are clear. The legal threshold is "the bullied child suffers", regardless of whether the other side was "just playing". If your child stays away from school for a long time, you can ask the school to investigate it as a "serious situation" under Article 28 of the Act. Note: this Act covers schools under Article 1 of the School Education Act, meaning ordinary primary, junior high and high schools. International and ethnic schools are mostly "miscellaneous schools" outside this Act, so ask the school directly about its own rules. If your child has unexplained injuries, suddenly refuses to go to school, or keeps losing things, ask them first.

### 6. If your child says they like people of the same sex, do not scold them, throw them out or send them for "conversion": the family's attitude is linked to whether they attempt suicide
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 孩子说自己喜欢同性，别骂、别赶出家门、别送去「矫正」：家里的态度和他会不会自杀有关 | 823f48ad -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is that parents first have to process their own feelings and not say harsh things in the moment.
- In plain words: Among secondary school students in China, those attracted to the same sex or to both sexes were about 3 to 5.5 times as likely as others to have attempted suicide in the past year. US research found that children strongly rejected by their families in adolescence went on to attempt suicide far more often. Those sent by their parents for "conversion" attempted suicide the most.
- Benefit: A Chinese survey covered 506 secondary schools in 7 provinces, with 150,822 students from the first year of junior high to the last year of high school. Those who attempted suicide in the past year were 2.2% of boys attracted only to the opposite sex, 6.9% of those attracted only to the same sex, and 12.2% of those attracted to both. For girls, 3.1%, 8.9% and 10.9%. That works out to about 3 to 5.5 times classmates attracted only to the opposite sex. The researchers adjusted for age, academic pressure, family finances and being bullied. After that, the ORs for these two groups of boys were 3.13 (95% CI 2.28–4.28) and 3.83 (2.85–5.14), about two to three times higher. A meta-analysis of 35 studies found sexual minority adolescents had an OR of 3.50 (2.98–4.12) for attempted suicide. A US study interviewed 224 gay and bisexual young adults aged 21 to 25, asking them to recall their family's reaction during adolescence. Compared with peers whose families showed almost no rejection, those with high family rejection had an OR of 8.4 for attempted suicide. In another study, where parents themselves tried to change their child's sexual orientation, the child's OR for attempted suicide was 3.08 (1.39–6.83). Where parents also sent the child to a therapist or religious figure, it was 5.07 (2.38–10.79) (China / multiple countries / US)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Huang Y, Li P, Guo L, et al. (2018). Sexual minority status and suicidal behaviour among Chinese adolescents: a nationally representative cross-sectional study. BMJ Open, 8(8), e020969. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2017-020969>；di Giacomo E, Krausz M, Colmegna F, Aspesi F, Clerici M. (2018). Estimating the Risk of Attempted Suicide Among Sexual Minority Youths: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics, 172(12), 1145–1152. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2018.2731>；Ryan C, Huebner D, Diaz RM, Sanchez J. (2009). Family rejection as a predictor of negative health outcomes in white and Latino lesbian, gay, and bisexual young adults. Pediatrics, 123(1), 346–352. <https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2007-3524>；Ryan C, Toomey RB, Diaz RM, Russell ST. (2020). Parent-Initiated Sexual Orientation Change Efforts With LGBT Adolescents: Implications for Young Adult Mental Health and Adjustment. Journal of Homosexuality, 67(2), 159–173. <https://doi.org/10.1080/00918369.2018.1538407>
- Notes: Graded B because these are all one-time surveys, with the degree of family rejection recalled in adulthood, so they only show association, not cause. Parents do not need to accept it immediately; just do nothing that hurts the child first. If your child is low or says they do not want to live, call a helpline first; see Section 1, entry 2 (Yorisoi Hotline), or take them to psychiatry following entry 8 of this section (depression screening at 12 to 18). For why to avoid "conversion", see Section 22, entry 2 (do not use sexual orientation conversion). The beneficiaries are the child and you.

### 7. Have your child spend 2 hours outdoors every day; this is the way to prevent myopia currently supported by randomized trials
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 让孩子每天在户外待够 2 小时，这是目前有随机试验支持的防近视办法 | b22e38c1 -->
- Cost: Free. But time has to be found every day, mostly squeezed from homework, cram school and screen time. The hard part is doing it every day.
- In plain words: When first-year primary school children got an extra 40 minutes of outdoor class a day, after three years new myopia fell from about 40% to about 30% of children. It only keeps some children from becoming short-sighted; it does not guarantee it. Being outdoors on cloudy days counts, and standing outside during breaks counts too.
- Benefit: First-year students at 12 primary schools in Guangzhou, randomized by school (intervention schools 952 children, control schools 951, mean age 6.6). The intervention added one 40-minute outdoor activity class each school day and encouraged parents to take children outdoors more after school. The proportion with new myopia over 3 years was 30.4% in the intervention group, 259 of 853 eligible; 39.5% in the control group, 287 of 726. The difference was -9.1 percentage points (95% CI -14.1 to -4.1, P<0.001), meaning the true difference is roughly between 4 and 14 percentage points fewer. Progression over 3 years (change in spherical equivalent) was -1.42 D vs -1.59 D, a difference of 0.17 D (P=0.04). The intervention group progressed slightly less. D is the unit of prescription strength. Growth in axial length (the front-to-back length of the eye) showed no difference between groups (P=0.07)
- Evidence: A
- Sources: He M, et al. (2015). Effect of Time Spent Outdoors at School on the Development of Myopia Among Children in China: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.10803>
- Notes: The trial ran only 3 years, with children starting at age 6. "Being outdoors" is not the same as "PE class"; the key is time outdoors. In Japan's summer, avoid the hottest hours; for preventing heatstroke, see Section 1, entry 24 (use the air conditioner at home in summer).

### 8. Have children aged 12 to 18 screened once for depression, and do not treat the school's psychological questionnaire as a diagnosis
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 12 到 18 岁的孩子做一次抑郁筛查，别拿学校的心理问卷当诊断 | 9174bdeb -->
- Cost: One outpatient visit and a screening questionnaire. If your child wants to talk to someone themselves, the 24-hour Children's SOS Dial is free.
- In plain words: US prevention guidelines recommend screening 12- to 18-year-olds for major depression; the evidence for 11 and under is insufficient. Suicide is the second leading cause of death among 10- to 19-year-olds. If your child says they are struggling, or their eating, sleep, interests or grades suddenly change, book psychiatry or child psychiatry directly.
- Benefit: The US Preventive Services Task Force issued a recommendation statement in 2022. It recommends screening adolescents aged 12 to 18 for major depressive disorder (grade B recommendation). Grade B means moderate certainty of a moderate net benefit, worth doing. For screening children 11 and under for major depression, the evidence is insufficient (I statement, meaning not enough evidence to make a recommendation). For specifically screening children and adolescents for suicide risk, evidence on both benefits and harms is insufficient (I statement). The document notes that suicide is the second leading cause of death among people aged 10 to 19. It also notes that major depression in children and adolescents is closely linked to many later problems. These include recurrent depression in adulthood, other mental disorders, and raised risk of suicidal thoughts, suicide attempts and death by suicide. MEXT's 24-hour Children's SOS Dial, 0120-0-78310, can be called by children and parents 24 hours a day
- Evidence: B
- Sources: US Preventive Services Task Force (2022). Screening for Depression and Suicide Risk in Children and Adolescents: US Preventive Services Task Force Recommendation Statement. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.16946>；文部科学省「24 じかんに相談できる窓口」. <https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/shotou/seitoshidou/06112216.html>
- Notes: Graded B because the source gives a rating of recommendation strength, not an effect that can be turned into a number. It is also a US recommendation, and Japan has no corresponding official screening recommendation. A positive screening result only means another assessment is needed, not a diagnosis. If your child says they want to die, adults should first call a helpline where Chinese is spoken; see Section 1, entry 2 (Yorisoi Hotline).

### 9. Have sealants applied once the permanent molars come in
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 恒磨牙长出来以后做窝沟封闭 | 0c87e76b -->
- Cost: Done at the dentist; in Japanese it is called シーラント. No anaesthetic and no drilling, a few minutes per visit.
- In plain words: A fissure sealant seals the grooves on the chewing surface of the back teeth with resin, so food cannot get stuck in them. Two years later, the chance of decay is about one-eighth of unsealed teeth. Without sealant 16% of tooth surfaces decay; with it only about 5%. It still works after four years.
- Benefit: 38 trials with 7,924 children aged 5 to 16, systematically collected by Cochrane and analysed together. Resin-based sealants compared with no sealant. For first permanent molars of children aged 5 to 10, decay at 24 months had OR 0.12 (95% CI 0.08–0.19), about one-eighth the chance of unsealed teeth. This comes from 7 trials, 1,548 randomized and 1,322 examined at the end, moderate-quality evidence. If 16% of surfaces in the control group decayed within 24 months, sealing gives 5.2% (3.13%–7.37%). If the control group is 40%, 6.25% (3.84%–9.63%). If the control group is 70%, 19% (12.3%–27.2%). At 48 to 54 months OR 0.21 (0.16–0.28), still about one-fifth of unsealed teeth. The authors concluded that resin sealants reduce decay by 11% to 51% at 24 months compared with no sealant. Of 4 trials recording adverse effects, none occurred. Evidence on which other sealant materials such as glass ionomer are better is insufficient
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Ahovuo-Saloranta A, et al. (2017). Pit and fissure sealants for preventing dental decay in permanent teeth. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001830.pub5>
- Notes: The first permanent molar (the big back tooth that will not be replaced) usually appears around age 6, and the second around 12. Sealing should be done when the tooth has just come in and is still healthy; once decayed, it can only be filled.

### 10. If your child cannot keep up in Japanese, they can start a grade lower; those past school age who did not finish junior high can enter a public junior high or a night junior high
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
<!-- zh: 孩子日语跟不上，可以先读低一个年级；超过学龄没读完初中的，可以进公立中学或夜间中学 | a5a80e08 -->
- Cost: Free. Discuss the entry grade with the school and board of education, and ask whether Japanese language support is available.
- In plain words: Children who have just come to Japan and whose Japanese is not yet good enough can temporarily or formally enter a lower grade. If compulsory education in the country of origin is shorter than Japan's and they have not completed 9 years, those still of school age can join the grade matching their age. Those past school age who have not completed compulsory education can enter a public junior high school, or a night junior high school (夜間中学) where one exists.
- Benefit: MEXT's guidelines state: when accepting foreign children who are not suited to entering the grade for their age right away, because of insufficient Japanese communication ability or differences in the order of learning content between Japan and abroad, entry to a lower grade may be permitted temporarily or formally according to their Japanese ability and learning situation; those who have not completed 9 years because compulsory education abroad is shorter than in Japan may, while of school age, be placed in the grade for their age if they wish; schools should respond flexibly when parents ask for supplementary teaching, repeating a year or postponing graduation. Those who passed school age without completing compulsory education for various reasons may enter a public junior high school at the municipal board of education's discretion; municipalities with night junior high schools should introduce the option of entering one. The guidelines also require boards of education to promote foreign children's progression to high school, for example by setting special quotas for foreign students in public high school entrance selection
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 文部科学省「外国人の子供の就学促進及び就学状況の把握等に関する指針」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/shotou/clarinet/004/1415154_00003.htm>
- Notes: The benefit is rated medium because the child can keep up with the curriculum and avoid detours, with no research figure. Whether public high schools have special quotas for foreign students differs by prefecture, so ask during junior high.

- Cost: Free. The hard part is stopping your hands and mouth the moment your temper flares, and switching to a way of disciplining without hitting or scolding; see entry 12 of this section (parenting classes that teach specific techniques).

### 12. If your child is hard to manage and you keep losing your temper and hitting or scolding, take a parenting course that teaches specific techniques

- Cost: The evidence-based courses meet weekly or fortnightly, one to two hours each time, for four to twenty-four weeks. You practise at home. The hard part is finishing the course and practising every day.
- In plain words: In randomized trials, courses that teach parents specific techniques reduced children's hitting and rule-breaking, and improved parents' mood. Parents also hit and scolded their children less, most clearly right after the course; it was still there a year or two later, but much smaller. This kind of course has also been tried in Shanghai and worked.
- Benefit: Cochrane combined 13 trials, 10 of them randomized, with 1,078 parents. Children were 3 to 12, all with behaviour problems. Children of parents who took the course had fewer behaviour problems. Rated by parents SMD −0.53 (95% CI −0.72 to −0.34, confidence range), and by independent observers −0.44 (−0.77 to −0.11). SMD and d are the same yardstick; 0.5 is medium. Parents' mental health improved, −0.36 (−0.52 to −0.20). Negative or harsh parenting decreased, rated by parents −0.77 (−0.96 to −0.59), by others −0.42 (−0.67 to −0.16). The authors say these are short-term effects. Another meta-analysis screened 346 randomized trials, 60 of which measured behaviours like hitting and scolding children, across 22 countries. Hitting and scolding fell right after the course, d = −0.46 (−0.59 to −0.33). After 1 to 6 months, −0.24 (−0.37 to −0.11). After 7 to 24 months, −0.18 (−0.34 to −0.02), shrinking over time. These data were mostly reported by parents themselves. In Shanghai, 81 parents were randomized, some to Triple P group sessions. After the course, children's adjustment problems and parents' practices and mood were all better than for parents waiting for the course, and this held 6 months later
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Furlong M, McGilloway S, Bywater T, Hutchings J, Smith SM, Donnelly M. (2012). - Notes: Graded A because there are figures pooled by Cochrane and from several randomized trials. The courses in the trials have several things in common. The leader teaches specific techniques, such as playing with the child, praise, rewards and discipline. Parents practise and rehearse in class and have homework. Courses that only hand out materials or only hold discussions were excluded, so the evidence does not cover a single lecture. When choosing a course, check that it runs over several weeks and has you practise specific techniques. Common course names include Triple P, Incredible Years and Parent-Child Interaction Therapy (PCIT). This book does not recommend specific organizations or paid products. The reduction in hitting and scolding shrinks after a year or two, so keep practising after the course. The beneficiaries are the child and you.

### 13. If your child loves video games, first check whether sleep, homework and going out are being crowded out, rather than only watching how long they play

- Cost: Free. Once a week, notice when your child goes to bed, whether homework is handed in, and whether they get out and about.
- In plain words: Those who truly qualify as having "gaming disorder" are about two or three in a hundred. Questionnaires with low thresholds, or surveys only of adolescents, inflate the proportion. Looking at real records from game companies, how much people play has almost nothing to do with how happy they feel.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis combined 53 studies from 17 countries with 226,247 people. The prevalence of gaming disorder was 3.05% (95% CI 2.38–3.91, the confidence range). Counting only studies with stricter sampling, it was 1.96%. Results varied widely between studies, and 77% of the variation came from which questionnaire was used. Samples of adolescents, low cut-off scores and small samples all pushed the proportion up. Another study obtained data from 7 game companies, linking 38,935 players' real playing time over six weeks with their self-reported well-being. It found almost no evidence that playing time affected well-being
- Evidence: B

- Notes: Graded B because prevalence varies too much with the questionnaire, and the well-being study only ran six weeks. The most common cost of gaming is crowding out sleep and outdoor time; for outdoors, see entry 7 of this section (2 hours outdoors a day). If grades, sleep and family relationships all collapse together and they cannot stop even when asked, take your child to psychiatry or child psychiatry. The beneficiary is the child.
