# 22. Not worth it: things that look valuable but aren't

<!-- zh-intro: bfdde1c9 -->

This section lists things that cost money or effort but do not deliver the promised effect. Entries are ordered by cost-effectiveness. Apart from entry 20 (Foods with Function Claims), which is specific to Japan, the rest carry over the universally applicable entries from the original book's "not worth it" section, with only prices in Chinese yuan removed. The entry on health check-up packages has been replaced with Japan's specific health checkups and municipal cancer screening. The original book's two entries on health supplement warning labels and paying for fortune-telling were based on Chinese rules and cases and were not carried over.

### 1. Do not cut out calcium to prevent kidney stones
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 不要为了防肾结石把钙戒掉 | 5eba5206 -->
- Cost: Free. It actually saves effort, as you no longer have to watch out for what contains calcium.
- In plain words: People who have had calcium oxalate stones are often told to eat less calcium, but trials found the opposite. Men with recurring stones who ate normal calcium while eating less animal protein and salt had only half the recurrence of those on a low-calcium diet over five years. UK guidelines also say not to restrict calcium.
- Benefit: A 5-year randomized controlled trial recruited 120 men with recurring calcium oxalate stones and high urinary calcium (called idiopathic hypercalciuria). They were randomized into two groups. One ate normal calcium, 30 mmol a day. This group also ate less animal protein (52 g a day) and less salt (50 mmol of sodium chloride a day). The other ate a traditional low-calcium diet, 10 mmol a day. Over 5 years, recurrence was 12/60 versus 23/60. Unadjusted relative risk 0.49 (95% CI 0.24–0.98, the confidence range), P=0.04; the normal-calcium group had only half the risk of recurrence. During follow-up, urinary calcium fell in both groups by about 170 mg a day. But oxalate excreted in urine differed. It rose by an average of 5.4 mg a day in the low-calcium group and fell by an average of 7.2 mg a day in the normal-calcium group. UK NICE guideline NG118, recommendation 1.8.1, says not to restrict daily calcium intake. Adults should keep to 700 to 1,200 mg a day. Children and young people 350 to 1,000 mg a day depending on age. Cutting out calcium looks worthwhile because of the intuition "stones are made of calcium, so eat less calcium"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Borghi L, Schianchi T, Meschi T, et al. (2002). Comparison of two diets for the prevention of recurrent stones in idiopathic hypercalciuria. New England Journal of Medicine, 346(2), 77-84. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa010369>；National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (2019). Renal and ureteric stones: assessment and management. NICE guideline NG118, recommendation 1.8.1. <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng118/chapter/Recommendations>
- Notes: Disputed: this trial was only in men, all with recurring calcium oxalate stones and high urinary calcium. So it cannot be applied directly to all types of stones. Also, the trial group changed three things at once: normal calcium, less animal protein and less salt. So the whole benefit cannot be credited to "not cutting calcium"; low salt and less animal protein were also at work. What is rejected is "actively cutting out calcium-containing foods", not "you should take more calcium". This is about calcium in everyday meals; calcium supplements are another matter, and people who have had stones should ask their doctor whether to take them. Ask your doctor how much water to drink

### 2. Do not pay for "sexual orientation conversion" or "gay therapy", and do not send family members to it
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 不要花钱做「性取向矫正」「同性恋治疗」，也不要把家里人送去 | b794cd63 -->
- Cost: Free. It saves the money charged by conversion organizations, psychological clinics and "gay cure" camps. The hard part is when family members insist on sending someone.
- In plain words: An expert panel of the American Psychological Association, after reviewing the research, said such conversion cannot change sexual orientation and may cause harm. In a US survey, people who had been through conversion were nearly 90% more likely to have had suicidal thoughts. Children whose parents sent them for conversion had the highest rate of later suicide attempts.
- Benefit: The American Psychological Association's 2009 task force report said efforts to change sexual orientation are "unlikely to be successful and involve some risk of harm". On sending minors to coercive or residential conversion programs, the report advises both parents and children not to consider it. A nationally representative US survey interviewed 1,518 sexual minority adults; about 7% had undergone conversion, 80% of them from religious figures. After adjusting for age, adverse childhood experiences and other factors, those who had undergone conversion had OR 1.92 (95% CI 1.01–3.64, about 90% higher) for lifetime suicidal thoughts. OR 1.75 (1.01–3.06) for planning suicide. OR 1.88 (1.01–3.50) for suicide attempts with no or minor injury. Another study interviewed 245 young people aged 21 to 25. Of those not subjected to conversion in adolescence, 22.0% had attempted suicide. Where parents themselves tried to change their child's sexual orientation, 48.1%. Where parents also sent the child to a therapist or religious figure, 62.8%. The World Health Organization's disease classification ICD-10 states: "sexual orientation by itself is not to be regarded as a disorder"
- Evidence: B
- Sources: American Psychological Association Task Force on Appropriate Therapeutic Responses to Sexual Orientation (2009). Report of the Task Force on Appropriate Therapeutic Responses to Sexual Orientation. <https://www.apa.org/pi/lgbt/resources/therapeutic-response.pdf>；Blosnich JR, Henderson ER, Coulter RWS, Goldbach JT, Meyer IH. (2020). Sexual Orientation Change Efforts, Adverse Childhood Experiences, and Suicide Ideation and Attempt Among Sexual Minority Adults, United States, 2016–2018. American Journal of Public Health, 110(7), 1024–1030. <https://doi.org/10.2105/AJPH.2020.305637>；Ryan C, Toomey RB, Diaz RM, Russell ST. (2020). Parent-Initiated Sexual Orientation Change Efforts With LGBT Adolescents: Implications for Young Adult Mental Health and Adjustment. Journal of Homosexuality, 67(2), 159–173. <https://doi.org/10.1080/00918369.2018.1538407>；World Health Organization. ICD-10 Version:2019, F66 Psychological and behavioural disorders associated with sexual development and orientation. <https://icd.who.int/browse10/2019/en#/F66>；Opposing view: Sullins DP. (2022). Sexual Orientation Change Efforts Do Not Increase Suicide: Correcting a False Research Narrative. Archives of Sexual Behavior, 51, 3377–3393. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s10508-022-02408-2>；Blosnich JR, Coulter RWS, Henderson ER, et al. (2023). Correcting a False Research Narrative: A Commentary on Sullins (2022). Archives of Sexual Behavior, 52(3), 885–888. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s10508-022-02521-2>
- Notes: Disputed. Sullins 2022 recalculated with the same data, removing suicidal thoughts present before conversion. Calculated this way, conversion was no longer positively associated with suicidal thoughts (OR 0.44, 0.20–0.94). Sullins's institution opposes homosexuality, and the original authors wrote a response. So how much harm it does is still disputed, but no opposing evidence has been seen on "it cannot change orientation". Graded B because the harm figures all come from surveys based on recall. People in long-term distress over this can go to psychiatry or counselling, with the aim of relieving the distress. For what the family should do after a child comes out, see Section 13, entry 6 (if your child says they like people of the same sex). The beneficiaries are you and your family.

### 3. Do not take multivitamins to live longer or prevent cardiovascular disease
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 不要为了长寿或防心血管病吃复合维生素 | 39744bd9 -->
- Cost: Taking them every day adds up to a sum over a year. Taking one takes no time, just one pill. Over time it is easy to become dependent, feeling as if you have left something undone on a day you miss it.
- In plain words: 14,000 male doctors took multivitamins for eleven years. Just as many had major heart and blood vessel events, and just as many died, as those who did not. Looking at 9 trials with over 50,000 people together, no effect on death could be seen either. Not buying them saves 200 to 700 yuan a year. People found to be genuinely deficient in a nutrient, and pregnant women taking folic acid, are not covered by this entry.
- Benefit: The PHS II trial recruited 14,641 male doctors and followed them for 11.2 years (the median: half followed longer, half shorter). Those taking them and those not had the same rate of major cardiovascular events. Major cardiovascular events HR 1.01 (HR is the hazard ratio; 1 means the two sides are the same). 95% CI 0.91–1.10 is this figure's confidence range. Death was also the same, all-cause mortality HR 0.94 (0.88–1.02). The US body that rates preventive measures, USPSTF, pooled 9 randomized controlled trials with 51,550 people in 2022, finding multivitamins unrelated to total mortality. Rated I, meaning the evidence is insufficient to say. It looks worthwhile because "one tablet covers all nutrients" sounds right, and it costs only a few cents a day
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Sesso HD et al. (2012). Multivitamins in the prevention of cardiovascular disease in men: the Physicians' Health Study II randomized controlled trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2012.14805>；US Preventive Services Task Force (2022). Vitamin, Mineral, and Multivitamin Supplementation to Prevent Cardiovascular Disease and Cancer. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.8970>；Gaziano JM, et al. (2012). Multivitamins in the Prevention of Cancer in Men. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2012.14641> (the cancer results in the notes)
- Notes: Disputed: cancer was also examined in the same male doctors. Those taking them had about 8% lower cancer rates (0.86–0.998, P=0.04, barely over the "unlikely to be chance" line). About 12% lower cancer death (0.77–1.01). This range crosses 1, so it may be just chance. The benefit is very small and has only been seen in male doctors. People found to be genuinely deficient, pregnant women (folic acid) and people whose diets are truly very unbalanced are excluded; follow your doctor's advice

### 4. Do not take ordinary fish oil to prevent cardiovascular disease
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 不要为了预防心血管病吃普通鱼油 | 2dcfb80d -->
- Cost: Taking it every day adds up to a sum over a year. Taking it takes no time.
- In plain words: Two large trials recruited 26,000 people and 15,000 people with diabetes respectively. People who took 1 gram of fish oil a day had as many major heart and blood vessel events and deaths as those who did not. "Eating fish is good for you" does not mean "fish oil capsules are good for you". Not buying them saves 400 to 1,000 yuan a year.
- Benefit: The VITAL trial recruited 25,871 people taking 1 gram of fish oil a day, followed for 5.3 years (median). No difference was seen in major heart and blood vessel events or death. Major cardiovascular events HR 0.92 (HR is the hazard ratio; 1 means the two sides are the same). 95% CI 0.80–1.06 is this figure's confidence range. It crosses 1, meaning no difference can be seen. All-cause mortality HR 1.02 (0.90–1.15). The ASCEND trial recruited 15,480 people with diabetes, also 1 gram a day, followed for 7.4 years on average, likewise with no difference. Serious vascular events RR 0.97 (0.87–1.08). All-cause mortality RR 0.95 (0.86–1.05). It looks worthwhile because many people take "eating fish is good for you" to mean "fish oil capsules are good for you"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Manson JE et al. (2019). Marine n-3 Fatty Acids and Prevention of Cardiovascular Disease and Cancer. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1811403>；ASCEND Study Collaborative Group (2018). Effects of n-3 Fatty Acid Supplements in Diabetes Mellitus. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1804989>；Bhatt DL, et al. (2019). Cardiovascular Risk Reduction with Icosapent Ethyl for Hypertriglyceridemia. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1812792> (the high-purity prescription fish oil trial in the notes)
- Notes: Disputed: one trial did work. The REDUCE-IT trial recruited 8,179 people. They already had cardiovascular disease or diabetes, were all on statins, and still had high triglycerides (135–499 mg/dL). They took 4 g a day of a pure-EPA prescription drug, and the event rate was 17.2% versus 22.0%. The risk was about a quarter lower (HR 0.75, 0.68–0.83). But that is a high-dose prescription drug for high-risk patients, not the same as ordinary people taking fish oil supplements. Eating fish itself is not covered here either

### 5. Do not give vitamin D to people who are not deficient in order to live longer
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 不要为了长寿给不缺维生素 D 的人补维生素 D | 03703f2c -->
- Cost: The money is not much; the real cost is thinking you have already taken preventive action.
- In plain words: People not deficient in vitamin D who took it for 5 years had the same numbers of cancer, cardiovascular disease and deaths as those who did not. People found to be genuinely deficient, people with osteoporosis and people who get little sun for long periods are not covered by this entry; they should take it as their doctor advises.
- Benefit: The VITAL trial recruited 25,871 people taking 2000 IU a day, followed for 5.3 years (median). No difference was seen in cancer, heart and blood vessel problems or death. Invasive cancer HR 0.96 (HR is the hazard ratio, 1 means the two sides are the same; 95% CI 0.88–1.06 is the confidence range). Major cardiovascular events HR 0.97 (0.85–1.12). All-cause mortality HR 0.99 (0.87–1.12). The D-Health trial recruited 21,315 Australians aged 60 or over. They took 60,000 IU a month, followed for 5 years. This too showed no difference, all-cause mortality HR 1.04 (0.93–1.18). Death rates were 5.3% versus 5.1%. It looks worthwhile because some studies that only tracked and recorded people without any intervention found that people with low vitamin D died more, which was misread as "supplementing works"
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Manson JE et al. (2019). Vitamin D Supplements and Prevention of Cancer and Cardiovascular Disease. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1809944>；Neale RE et al. (2022). The D-Health Trial: a randomised controlled trial of the effect of vitamin D on mortality. Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-8587(21)00345-4>
- Notes: Disputed: neither trial specifically recruited people who were genuinely deficient in vitamin D, so the conclusion only covers "people who were not deficient to begin with". People found to be genuinely deficient, people with osteoporosis, people who get little sun for long periods and infants should still take it as their doctor advises. Only total mortality, cancer and cardiovascular outcomes were examined here, nothing else

### 6. Do not take antioxidant supplements (beta-carotene, vitamin E, vitamin A) to prevent cancer
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 不要为了防癌吃抗氧化补剂（β 胡萝卜素、维生素 E、维生素 A） | 4fa7d65d -->
- Cost: Taking them every day costs money. For smokers, the real cost is a rising lung cancer risk.
- In plain words: This is not just useless. Pooling 78 trials with nearly 300,000 people, those taking antioxidant supplements actually had slightly higher mortality. In two large trials specifically of smokers, beta-carotene raised lung cancer rates by 18% to 28%. Deaths also rose by 8% to 17%. Smokers and people exposed to asbestos especially should not take them.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooled 78 randomized controlled trials with 296,707 people. For those taking antioxidant supplements, total mortality RR was 1.02 (RR is the risk ratio, 1 means the two sides are the same; 95% CI 0.98–1.05 is the confidence range). Looking only at the more rigorously designed trials less prone to bias, RR 1.04 (1.01–1.07), slightly higher. Beta-carotene alone RR 1.05 (1.01–1.09), vitamin E alone RR 1.03 (1.00–1.05). The ATBC trial recruited 29,133 male smokers taking 20 mg a day; lung cancer was 18% higher (3%–36%) and total mortality 8% higher (1%–16%). The CARET trial recruited 18,314 smokers or people exposed to asbestos. Their lung cancer risk was about 28% higher (RR 1.28, 1.04–1.57). Their risk of death was about 17% higher (all-cause mortality RR 1.17, 1.03–1.33). It looks worthwhile because each step in the chain of reasoning "antioxidants fight ageing, and fighting ageing prevents cancer" sounds like common sense
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Bjelakovic G et al. (2012). Antioxidant supplements for prevention of mortality in healthy participants and patients with various diseases. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007176.pub2>；The Alpha-Tocopherol, Beta Carotene Cancer Prevention Study Group (1994). The effect of vitamin E and beta carotene on the incidence of lung cancer and other cancers in male smokers. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199404143301501>；Omenn GS et al. (1996). Effects of a combination of beta carotene and vitamin A on lung cancer and cardiovascular disease. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199605023341802>
- Notes: The USPSTF is the official US body that rates preventive measures. In 2022 it gave beta-carotene and vitamin E a grade D, meaning it recommends against them. Smokers and people exposed to asbestos have already been harmed by them. Antioxidants naturally present in vegetables and fruit are not included; keep eating them

### 7. Do not buy detox, colon cleanse, enzyme or alkaline water products
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 不要买排毒、清肠、酵素、碱性水这类产品 | 98080ce5 -->
- Cost: These products are not cheap. The kind that "detoxes" by starving you can also drive your blood sugar too low and leave you short of nutrients.
- In plain words: The detox industry is huge, yet clinical evidence supporting it is very scarce, and no one has properly tested the commercial detox diets on sale. When researchers checked whether dietary acidity, alkaline water and cancer are related, they found only 1 qualifying study, which showed no association.
- Benefit: That review concluded that "despite the booming detox industry, there is very little clinical evidence to support the use of these diets", and that "there are no randomised controlled trials of commercial detox diets". Another systematic review specifically examined whether dietary acid load and alkaline water are related to cancer: of 8,278 papers screened, only 1 qualified for inclusion, and it showed no association. The authors wrote that "promotion of the alkaline diet and alkaline water to the public for cancer prevention or treatment is not justified". It looks worthwhile because the two premises "there are toxins in the body that need flushing out" and "an acidic constitution causes cancer" sound a lot like physiology
- Evidence: C
- Sources: Klein AV, Kiat H (2015). Detox diets for toxin elimination and weight management: a critical review of the evidence. J Hum Nutr Diet. <https://doi.org/10.1111/jhn.12286>；Fenton TR, Huang T (2016). Systematic review of the association between dietary acid load, alkaline water and cancer. BMJ Open. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2015-010438>
- Notes: All that can be said is "there is no evidence to support it", so it only gets grade C. Clearing out waste produced by the body's metabolism is already the job of the liver and kidneys. As long as these two organs are not diseased, no additional "detox" is needed

### 8. Do not believe "willpower is like a muscle that gets used up after a while"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
<!-- zh: 不要相信「意志力像肌肉，用一会儿就没了」 | 40e6c9cc -->
- Cost: Free. All that changes is one belief.
- In plain words: The popular claim is: after doing something that takes self-control, you are more likely to slip next, so willpower should be rationed. 23 labs with 2,141 people redid this experiment using the same protocol, and the result was nearly zero. Stop explaining yourself with "I've used up my willpower today", and do not pay for courses teaching you to save willpower.
- Benefit: A multi-lab preregistered replication, 23 labs with 2,141 participants in total, redid the sequential-task paradigm using Sripada and colleagues' standardized protocol: first a task that depletes self-control, then a second task requiring self-control. The pooled result showed a very small ego depletion effect, with a 95% confidence interval including 0 (d = 0.04, 95% CI −0.07 to 0.15). On this basis the authors discussed whether the "self-control is a limited resource" model still holds. Another replication, also with a preregistered protocol, with 36 labs and 3,531 people, found an effect size of d = 0.06, likewise too small to count.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Hagger MS, Chatzisarantis NLD, Alberts H, et al. (2016). A Multilab Preregistered Replication of the Ego-Depletion Effect. Perspectives on Psychological Science, 11(4), 546–573. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616652873>；Vohs et al. (2021). A Multisite Preregistered Paradigmatic Test of the Ego-Depletion Effect. Psychological Science. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797621989733>
- Notes: What is rejected is the model of "self-control as a resource that gets used up", not "people get tired". Too little sleep or working too long reduces efficiency, which is another matter; for sleep, see Section 20, entry 33 (sleep about 7 hours a night). The approaches actually supported by randomized trials are changing your environment and how you phrase plans; see Section 25, entry 13 (move what you want to avoid further away) and Section 25, entry 1 (write it as "at what time, where, and on what cue"). Fixing what you wear and eat is still worth doing because it costs almost nothing. What it saves is those few minutes and a few moments of indecision, not willpower; do not expect it to make your afternoon judgment better. Other entries in this section count money; this one counts time and energy, and the two are not converted.

### 9. Do not count on glucosamine or chondroitin to treat knee arthritis
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 不要指望氨糖（葡萄糖胺）/软骨素治膝关节炎 | ce1ca064 -->
- Cost: Taking them daily adds up to a substantial sum over a year.
- In plain words: People with knee osteoarthritis who took a dummy pill for 24 weeks also had 60% saying the pain eased. Those taking glucosamine or chondroitin were only four or five percentage points more, a gap so small it may be chance. Spending 700 to 1,800 yuan a year basically buys you that 60% placebo effect.
- Benefit: The GAIT trial recruited 1,583 people with knee osteoarthritis for 24 weeks. Of those taking placebo (a dummy pill), 60.1% also said their pain eased. Glucosamine was only 3.9 percentage points higher (P=0.30). Chondroitin 5.3 percentage points higher (P=0.17). The two together 6.5 percentage points higher (P=0.09). These gaps are all too small to rule out chance. The real drug celecoxib, used as a comparison in the same trial, was 10.0 percentage points higher (P=0.008). It looks worthwhile because "raw material to rebuild cartilage" sounds exactly right for the problem, and 60% say it works even on a dummy pill
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Clegg DO et al. (2006). Glucosamine, chondroitin sulfate, and the two in combination for painful knee osteoarthritis. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa052771>
- Notes: Disputed: in the same trial, looking at those in severe pain from the start, the response rate for the two together was 79.2% versus 54.3% for control (P=0.002). But the authors say this was found by combing the data afterwards and can only be treated as a lead, not a conclusion. The approaches with real evidence for knee arthritis are losing weight and strengthening muscles; see Section 20, entry 30 (strength training) and Section 20, entry 25 (keep your BMI at 20–25).

### 10. Do not have a "whole-body PET-CT" or a "tumour marker package" when you have no symptoms
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 不要给没有症状的自己做「全身 PET-CT」或「肿瘤标志物套餐」 | 4f728191 -->
- Cost: A self-paid whole-body PET-CT is not cheap, and it means a dose of ionizing radiation. A tumour marker package costs extra too. The real cost comes later. An "abnormality" is found that turns out to be a false alarm (called a false positive). The follow-up scans, biopsies and surgery that follow are where the money and suffering lie.
- In plain words: When people without symptoms get checked, what is found most often is a false alarm. Over 80% of people having a whole-body CT had at least one "abnormality" found, and nearly 40% were called back for further tests, but most were benign. Women screened with an ovarian cancer marker actually died of ovarian cancer slightly more often, and some had unnecessary surgery because of a false alarm.
- Benefit: The US body that rates preventive measures, USPSTF, gave screening women without symptoms for ovarian cancer with CA-125 and ultrasound a grade D, meaning it recommends against it. The basis was the PLCO trial. Death from ovarian cancer was 0.34% in the screening group and 0.29% in the usual-care group. RR 1.18 (95% CI 0.82–1.71). Screening brought no benefit. The proportion having unnecessary surgery because of false positives was 0.2%–3.25% across trials, and up to 15% of these had serious surgical complications. Another study gave whole-body CT screening to 1,192 people without symptoms. 86% had at least one "abnormality" found, and 37% were advised to have further tests. The original says "most findings were described as benign". It looks worthwhile because "check the whole body at once and catch it early" is so intuitive
- Evidence: A
- Sources: US Preventive Services Task Force (2018). Screening for Ovarian Cancer: US Preventive Services Task Force Recommendation Statement. JAMA (the same recommendation also has an official USPSTF page). <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2017.21926>、<https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/ovarian-cancer-screening>；Furtado CD et al. (2005). Whole-body CT screening: spectrum of findings and recommendations in 1192 patients. Radiology. <https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.2372041741>
- Notes: The tumour marker part is backed by randomized controlled trials. The whole-body CT and PET-CT part only has follow-up records of "what happened to people who had it", without randomized comparison. That evidence is a grade weaker, equivalent to grade B. The few screenings with real evidence, such as for colorectal, cervical and breast cancer, and low-dose chest CT for high-risk people, are another matter; see entry 14 of this section (have the few evidence-based screenings for your age and sex). PET-CT for people who already have symptoms or already have a cancer diagnosis is medical care, not screening, and is a different matter

### 11. Do not count on a fitness band or watch tracking your activity to help you lose weight
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 不要指望手环/手表的运动量化帮你减重 | 02c2ef6c -->
- Cost: The device costs money. And you are distracted every day checking the data.
- In plain words: Overweight people following the same diet and exercise programme for two years lost 3.5 kg if they wore a fitness band. Those without one actually lost 5.9 kg. To lose weight, spending money on a device does not help; what works is the exercise itself.
- Benefit: The IDEA trial recruited 471 overweight or obese adults and followed them for 24 months. Everyone followed the same diet and exercise programme; the only difference was whether they wore a wearable device. The group wearing one lost 3.5 kg (95% CI 2.6–4.5, the confidence range). The group without lost 5.9 kg (5.0–6.8), actually more. The difference between groups was 2.4 kg (1.0–3.7, P=0.002). It looks worthwhile because "you can only manage what you measure" sounds reasonable, and you only buy the device once
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Jakicic JM et al. (2016). Effect of Wearable Technology Combined With a Lifestyle Intervention on Long-term Weight Loss: The IDEA Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2016.12858>
- Notes: This is a single trial, using arm-worn devices from the early 2010s, so it cannot support "all wearables are useless". Exercise itself works; see Section 20, entry 22 (walk 7,000–8,000 steps a day, or accumulate 150–300 minutes of brisk walking a week). - In plain words: Of people with gallstones that have never hurt, about 80% stayed symptom-free over nearly nine years. After a first episode of colic, complications run at 1% to 3% a year. After removal, 10% to 40% of patients still have discomfort. - Cost: Free. All you need to do is ask one more question before increasing the budget: is this extra buying the thing itself, or my position among these people? The hard part is not following along when everyone around you has upgraded.
- In plain words: Comparing people with the same income, the more their neighbours earned, the lower the happiness they reported. This only happened with neighbours of the same education who they often socialized with. So extra money spent to move somewhere everyone is better off than you buys you a step down. But when incomes rise together, people still feel better off; the absolute level still counts.
- Benefit: The US National Survey of Families and Households (NSFH) interviewed the same people in two waves. The author matched these data with local average income. Local average income (PUMA) was estimated from the 1990 census 5% sample. After controlling for own income and a range of personal characteristics, the result was: the higher neighbours' income, the lower self-reported happiness. The effect was large, robust to alternative specifications and highly significant. In the author's words, the drop in happiness from "an increase in neighbours' income" is of the same order of magnitude as from "a decrease in own income of the same size". When comparing the same person with themselves over time (adding individual fixed effects), the coefficient kept its direction and similar size, though significance fell to the 10% level. Restricting comparisons to the same state and the same survey wave (adding state and wave fixed effects) left the results essentially unchanged. This shows it was not caused by "people who move to poorer places being happier to begin with", nor by unmeasured state-level factors. The effect was stronger for people who socialized often with neighbours, and not significant for those who socialized only with friends outside the neighbourhood. Renters and homeowners did not differ, which does not support the explanation that "expensive places have high housing costs, so people have less to spend". Happiness fell only with the income of neighbours of the same education, and barely responded to neighbours of different education. And it was transmitted mainly through "satisfaction with one's material situation", not satisfaction with other areas such as family life. Finally, if own and neighbours' income rise in the same proportion, happiness still rises
