# 15. Parents and older relatives: bringing parents over, medical care during visits, and long-term care insurance

<!-- zh-intro: 6059d6d2 -->

This section covers what foreigners in Japan most often face when looking after their parents: whether parents can come to live in Japan long-term, what to do if they fall ill on a short visit, who can bring parents to live with them, and long-term care insurance. Information is as of 2026-10-03. The three entries on refusing sales pitches, pressure ulcers and cataracts are carried over from the original book's section "older people at home"; the research figures are unchanged, and the mattress has been replaced with a rental under long-term care insurance. If your parents stay in your country of origin and need care from a distance, the other general advice in that section of the original book is also useful.

### 1. Parents on a short visit to Japan have no National Health Insurance and pay medical bills in full, so buy travel medical insurance before they set out
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 父母短期来日本没有国民健康保险，看病要全额自付，出发前买好旅行医疗保险 | 78967fba -->
- Cost: Travel medical insurance is priced by days and coverage; buy it in the country of origin before departure.
- In plain words: National Health Insurance only covers people registered as residents in Japan. Parents visiting on a short-term stay are not registered and are not in National Health Insurance. Medical care in Japan is paid in full, with no 30% share and no high-cost medical expense cap. Before they set out, buy travel medical insurance covering their time in Japan.
- Benefit: The National Health Insurance Act provides that people with an address in a prefecture are insured under National Health Insurance; the enforcement regulations provide that people who are not Japanese nationals and are not "foreign residents" under the Basic Resident Registration Act are excluded. The Immigration Act provides that people with a period of stay of 3 months or less and people on a short-term stay are not mid- to long-term residents issued a residence card
- Evidence: B
- Sources: 国民健康保険法 第五条、第六条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/333AC0000000192> ; 国民健康保険法施行規則 第一条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/333M50000100053> ; 出入国管理及び難民認定法 第十九条の三. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/326CO0000000319>
- Notes: Why grade B: the lack of National Health Insurance follows from the statutes, but how much insurance saves depends on the actual illness, with no statistics. The benefit is rated large because a single hospital stay can cost hundreds of thousands of yen. At the hospital, explain that your parents are short-term visitors, and ask whether they pay in full and whether you can get an estimate first.

### 2. If you hold a work visa, your parents can generally only visit short-term, for up to 90 days, and in principle cannot extend
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
<!-- zh: 持工作签证的人，父母一般只能短期来探亲，最长 90 天，原则上不能延长 | 5e0c980e -->
- Cost: Free. Plan your parents' visit around a maximum of 90 days; visa requirements depend on their nationality, so ask the Japanese embassy or consulate where they live.
- In plain words: Immigration states that, as a rule, foreigners in Japan on work statuses cannot bring their parents to live in Japan. Parents visiting use a short-term stay (短期滞在), with a period of 90, 30 or 15 days. Extensions are only possible in truly unavoidable humanitarian circumstances, so do not count on renewing at the last minute.
- Benefit: The Immigration Services Agency states that, as a rule, the parents of foreigners staying on a work status are not permitted to enter and stay. Short-term stay is a status for short stays in Japan for sightseeing, recreation, visiting relatives and similar, with a period of 90, 30 or 15 days (or a period designated by the Minister of Justice within 90 days). The agency states that those wishing to continue staying on a short-term stay must apply for renewal with documents when, for example, there are truly unavoidable humanitarian circumstances
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国在留管理庁「在留資格「特定活動」（高度専門職外国人又はその配偶者の親・特別高度人材外国人又はその配偶者の親）」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/status/designatedactivities02_00002.html> ; 出入国在留管理庁「在留資格「短期滞在」」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/status/temporaryvisitor.html>
- Notes: The benefit is rated medium because it avoids your parents overstaying and affecting future visits. For holders of status-based visas such as Permanent Resident or Spouse of Japanese National, whether parents can stay long-term must be asked of immigration case by case; this entry draws no conclusion. For the exception for highly skilled professionals, see entry 5 (highly skilled professionals can bring parents to live with them).

### 3. Give older relatives a ready line to refuse with at any time: "I'll go home and talk it over with my family"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 给老人备好一句随时能说出口的推辞：「我要回去和家里人商量」 | f84fa250 -->
- Cost: Free. One conversation with the older person, a few minutes.
- In plain words: Sales pitches and scams rely on on-the-spot pressure, making it awkward to refuse there and then. Agree a line with the older person in advance: "I'll go home and talk it over with my family." They can say it at any time and get a way out on the spot. This works better than reasoning with them afterwards.
- Benefit: Sales pitches and scams both rely on on-the-spot pressure. Agreeing in advance on the line "I'll go home and talk it over with my family" gives the older person a way out. This is more effective than reasoning with them afterwards
- Evidence: C
- Sources: Author's experience; no direct literature
- Notes: Parents visiting Japan who do not understand Japanese find it even harder to refuse door-to-door sales or a phone call pressing them to transfer money. Prepare the line in both Chinese and Japanese. In Japanese they can say 「家族と相談します」 (I will talk with my family). If a contract has already been signed, see Section 10, entry 6 (door-to-door sales can be cancelled unconditionally within 8 days). The line gives the older person a way out so they do not have to stand up to the pressure on the spot; it is not a way of keeping watch on them.

### 4. If someone at home is bedridden long-term, treat pressure ulcers as enemy number one: get a powered air mattress, turn them on schedule, and check the bony areas every day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 家里有人长期卧床，把压疮当头号敌人：上电动气垫床、定时翻身、每天看一遍骨头突出的地方 | 81d89f3b -->
- Cost: People certified as needing care level 2 or above can rent a pressure-relief mattress (床ずれ防止用具) under long-term care insurance, paying 10% to 30%. Turning someone takes only a few minutes. The hard part is doing it every two or three hours, through the night too, which one person cannot sustain alone.
- In plain words: Older people with a pressure ulcer before hip fracture surgery are about 20% more likely to die within 30 days. A powered air mattress cuts new pressure ulcers by about 60%. Turning on schedule, actually carried out, cuts them by about 70%. Do not leave someone lying face down for long. Check the tailbone, heels, hips, shoulders and back every day; if you find redness that does not fade when pressed, contact a nurse or doctor that day.
- Benefit: 19,520 hip fracture surgery patients (US NSQIP 2016 to 2019). Those with a pressure ulcer before surgery had a higher 30-day death rate. After accounting for other factors, the association still held (OR 1.2, 21% higher odds, P=0.004). They were also more likely to have other problems: deep vein thrombosis (OR 1.59), pneumonia (OR 1.39), unplanned readmission (OR 1.43), and 0.4 more days in hospital on average. 324 hospitalized older adults (mean age 86) were followed for a median of 18 months. Pressure ulcers independently predicted long-term death (HR 1.500, 95% CI 1.059 to 2.126, P=0.020). Independently predicting means it predicts on its own after other factors are excluded. 65 randomized trials were analysed together (a network meta-analysis). The comparison was between powered active air-flow support surfaces and standard hospital mattresses (standard hospital support surfaces). Powered active air-flow surfaces are the powered air mattress type. Those using them had a lower pressure ulcer rate (RR 0.42, 95% CI 0.29 to 0.63, moderate certainty). Powered hybrid air surfaces had RR 0.22 (0.07 to 0.66). A Cochrane review of 11 trials with 4,462 people (updated 2026). Turning every 2 hours versus every 4 hours made an unclear difference (RR 1.05, 95% CI 0.79 to 1.39, very low certainty). Comparisons between other turning intervals were equally very uncertain. In a trial of 1,226 ICU patients, wearable sensors giving real-time reminders and visual alerts made 2-hourly turning actually happen. The pressure ulcer rate was lower than with usual care (RR 0.28, 95% CI 0.10 to 0.75, moderate certainty). In a trial of 116 people, the prone (face-down) group had a higher pressure ulcer rate than the supine (lying on the back) group (RR 4.55, 95% CI 2.31 to 8.98). In Japan, long-term care insurance rents 13 types of welfare equipment, including pressure-relief equipment (床ずれ防止用具) and repositioning devices; people at support levels 1 and 2 and care level 1 are in principle not covered by insurance benefits; you pay 10% of the rental, or 20% or 30% above certain income levels
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Porter SB, Pla R, Chow JH, et al. (2022). Preoperative Pressure Ulcers, Mortality, and Complications in Older Hip Fracture Surgery Patients. JAAOS Global Research & Reviews, 6(11). <https://doi.org/10.5435/JAAOSGlobal-D-22-00117>；Ottaviani S, Rondanina E, Longo E, et al. (2026). Impact of pressure ulcers and frailty on long-term mortality: a prospective cohort study of hospitalized older adults. European Geriatric Medicine, 17(2), 665-672. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s41999-025-01331-8>；Shi C, Dumville JC, Cullum N (2018). Support surfaces for pressure ulcer prevention: A network meta-analysis. PLoS ONE, 13(2), e0192707. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0192707>；Latimer SL, Chaboyer WP, Probst S, et al. (2026). Repositioning for pressure injury prevention in adults. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 6, CD009958. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009958.pub4>；厚生労働省 介護サービス情報公表システム「福祉用具貸与」. <https://www.kaigokensaku.mhlw.go.jp/publish/group21.html>
- Notes: There are three reasons for grade B. First, the two mortality studies only show that "people with pressure ulcers fare worse later", which is not the same as "preventing pressure ulcers lowers mortality". Second, the conclusions of the randomized trials on turning intervals are very weak. Third, the air mattress evidence is stronger, but the studies compared hospital bed mattresses, so applying it to home mattresses is an extrapolation. Checking the tailbone, heels, hips, shoulders and back every day for "redness that does not fade when pressed" is routine nursing practice, with no separate literature. Discuss which mattress to rent with the care manager (ケアマネジャー) in charge. For how foreigners join long-term care insurance and use care services, see entry 6 of this section (people 40 and over are also in long-term care insurance). If one leg suddenly swells, treat it as possible deep vein thrombosis; see Section 3, entry 27 (one leg suddenly swells).

### 5. Highly skilled professionals can bring their parents to live with them to look after a child under 7 or a pregnant spouse
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
<!-- zh: 高度专门职的人，为了照顾 7 岁以下的孩子或怀孕的配偶，可以把父母接来同住 | 13cffd90 -->
- Cost: The application itself is free. Prepare proof of household annual income, proof of family relationships, your child's residence card and so on, and apply to immigration.
- In plain words: Holders of the Highly Skilled Professional visa are one of the few exceptions who can bring their parents to live with them. There are three conditions. The parents come to look after your or your spouse's child under 7, or to care for you or your spouse during pregnancy. The parents live with you. At the time of entry, your and your spouse's combined annual income is expected to be 8 million yen or more.
- Benefit: The Immigration Services Agency states that, as one of the preferential measures for highly skilled foreign professionals, the parents (including adoptive parents) of a highly skilled foreign professional or their spouse are permitted to enter and stay, with the status Designated Activities, to raise the professional's or spouse's child (including adopted children) under 7, or to provide assistance, housework and other necessary support to a pregnant spouse or the pregnant professional. Requirements include living with the highly skilled professional who is the applicant's child or child's spouse, and the professional's household annual income (expected) at the time of the applicant's entry being 8 million yen or more. Household annual income is the combined annual remuneration of the professional and their spouse, excluding income of anyone other than the spouse. Parents can also be brought from the home country after the professional has entered Japan
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 出入国在留管理庁「在留資格「特定活動」（高度専門職外国人又はその配偶者の親・特別高度人材外国人又はその配偶者の親）」（accessed 2026-10-03）. <https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/status/designatedactivities02_00002.html>
- Notes: The benefit is rated medium because it lets your parents stay legally long-term and help with childcare. Once the child turns 7 or you no longer live together, the basis for this status is gone, so plan ahead. For how to obtain Highly Skilled Professional status, see the Immigration Services Agency's explanation of the points-based system for highly skilled professionals.

### 6. Foreigners aged 40 and over living in Japan are also in long-term care insurance, and from 65 can use care services when they need care
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
<!-- zh: 住在日本 40 岁以上的外国人也在介护保险里，65 岁起需要照护时可以用介护服务 | 69420cdd -->
- Cost: From age 40, long-term care insurance premiums are paid together with health insurance.
- In plain words: Long-term care insurance (介護保険) applies regardless of nationality. Everyone 65 or over with an address in Japan is insured under it. So are people aged 40 to 64 who are in medical insurance. If your parents live in Japan with a status such as Permanent Resident or Long-Term Resident and are registered as residents, they can apply for care services through the proper procedure when they need care in old age.
- Benefit: The Long-Term Care Insurance Act provides that people aged 65 or over with an address in a municipality are Category 1 insured persons; people aged 40 to under 65 with an address in a municipality who are enrolled in medical insurance are Category 2 insured persons
- Evidence: A
- Sources: 介護保険法 第九条. <https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/409AC0000000123>
- Notes: The benefit is rated medium because it gives access to services such as home care and day care. For specific services and the share you pay, you first need a "care needs certification" (要介護認定); ask the long-term care insurance counter of your municipality. Parents on a short visit have no resident registration and are not in long-term care insurance.

### 7. If an older person's cataracts already affect how they see the road, take them to an eye doctor to assess surgery; if both eyes are affected, do not wait too long for the second
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 老人的白内障已经影响看路，就去眼科评估做手术；两只眼都有的，第二只别拖太久 | b814db6a -->
- Cost: First take the older person to an eye doctor for an examination, and let the doctor decide whether surgery is needed. Cataracts in both eyes are operated on separately. What you pay yourself depends on which artificial lens is chosen and whether it is covered by health insurance; ask before surgery.
- In plain words: Women over 70 who had first-eye cataract surgery promptly had about 34% fewer falls in a year, and fewer had fractures. But the number of people who fell did not go down. For the second eye, no clear difference in falls was seen. Older people who had surgery had about 16% lower hip fracture odds within a year.
- Benefit: A randomized trial recruited 306 women aged over 70 with cataracts. Half had first-eye surgery within about 4 weeks, and the other half waited the usual 12 months. Over a year, those operated on promptly had about 34% fewer falls. The original figure is a rate ratio of falls of 0.66, 95% CI 0.45 to 0.96. Those who fell at least once were 49% in the surgery group and 45% in the waiting group, no fewer. Those who fell twice or more were 18% in the surgery group and 25% in the waiting group. 4 people in the surgery group had fractures (3%), versus 12 in the waiting group (8%). A Cochrane review included this trial and concluded that first-eye surgery in women reduced the number of falls, while second-eye surgery did not. The second-eye randomized trial recruited 239 women aged over 70. The surgery group's fall rate was 32% lower, but the difference may have been chance (rate ratio of falls 0.68, 95% CI 0.39 to 1.19). A 5% sample of US Medicare included 1,113,640 cataract patients aged 65 or over (2002 to 2009). This study only followed and recorded people, with no randomized comparison. Those who had surgery had about 16% lower odds of hip fracture within a year (OR 0.84, 95% CI 0.81 to 0.87). The absolute difference was 0.20 percentage points, about 2 fewer cases per 1,000 people
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Harwood RH, Foss AJ, Osborn F, Gregson RM, Zaman A, Masud T (2005). Falls and health status in elderly women following first eye cataract surgery: a randomised controlled trial. British Journal of Ophthalmology, 89(1), 53-59. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjo.2004.049478> ; Foss AJ, Harwood RH, Osborn F, Gregson RM, Zaman A, Masud T (2006). Falls and health status in elderly women following second eye cataract surgery: a randomised controlled trial. Age and Ageing, 35(1), 66-71. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ageing/afj005> ; Gillespie LD, Robertson MC, Gillespie WJ, et al. (2012). Interventions for preventing falls in older people living in the community. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 9, CD007146. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007146.pub3> ; Tseng VL, Yu F, Lum F, Coleman AL (2012). Risk of fractures following cataract surgery in Medicare beneficiaries. JAMA, 308(5), 493-501. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2012.9014> ; Meuleners LB, Lee AH, Ng JQ, Morlet N, Fraser ML (2012). First eye cataract surgery and hospitalization from injuries due to a fall: a population-based study. Journal of the American Geriatrics Society, 60(9), 1730-1733. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1532-5415.2012.04098.x> ; Meuleners LB, Fraser ML, Ng J, Morlet N (2014). The impact of first- and second-eye cataract surgery on injurious falls that require hospitalisation: a whole-population study. Age and Ageing, 43(3), 341-346. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ageing/aft177> ; Keay L, Ho KC, Rogers K, et al. (2022). The incidence of falls after first and second eye cataract surgery: a longitudinal cohort study. Medical Journal of Australia, 217(2), 94-99. <https://doi.org/10.5694/mja2.51611>
- Notes: Disputed: combining the two randomized trials, the number of people who fell did not clearly decrease (OR 0.81, 95% CI 0.55 to 1.17). In whole-population hospital records from Western Australia, hospitalizations for fall injuries actually rose 27% in the year after first-eye surgery. Between the two operations, such hospitalizations were 2.14 times the pre-surgery level. Before-and-after comparisons like this cannot separate the effect of surgery from the effect of ageing. In another Australian follow-up study, falls dropped most after second-eye surgery. So if both eyes have cataracts, do not wait too long for the second. Between the two operations, take extra care walking and on stairs. Surgery deals with seeing clearly; falling less also depends on Section 1, entry 23 (balance and leg strength). For whether bones can withstand a fall, see Section 1, entry 29 (bone density test). Parents visiting only briefly have no Japanese health insurance, so do not have it done in Japan; see entry 1 of this section (parents on a short visit have no National Health Insurance). The beneficiaries are mainly your parents and grandparents.
