# 24. Don't waste energy

<!-- zh-intro: 5842a974 -->

This section only counts energy and time. It looks at four things: the attention you have available each day, how fast you react, how many mistakes you make, and how long it takes to get back on track after an interruption. All figures are measured directly, from experiments or from records of groups of people followed over time (cohort studies). These figures are not converted into lifespan or mortality. Most entries in this section are evidence grade B. That is normal for this field and does not mean the advice is unsound. Entry 17 (treat pessimistic thoughts as a symptom) and entry 27 (treat "others demand that I be perfect" as a symptom) cite figures on mortality and suicide risk, a different measure from the other entries, and are not compared with them.

This section carries over the original book's section "don't waste energy", with the research figures unchanged; only Chinese national statistics, helplines and references to other sections of the original book were removed. The original entry on "what to expect when dealing with police, doctors and counter staff" was based on Chinese police regulations and was not carried over.

### 1. When doing work that needs continuous thought, block out interruptions, even ones lasting a few seconds
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
<!-- zh: 做需要连续思考的工作时，把哪怕几秒钟的打断也挡在外面 | 4e3c413a -->
- Cost: Free. Close the door, put on headphones, or hang a "do not disturb" sign.
- In plain words: An interruption averaging 2.8 seconds doubles the chance of mistakes in what you do next. A 4.4-second interruption triples it. Field tracking also found it takes an average of 25 minutes to get back to the interrupted task, with two other tasks squeezed in between. Interrupted people work faster, but their stress, frustration and effort are all higher.
- Benefit: An interruption averaging 2.8 seconds doubled the step error rate in a task done step by step in order (a sequential task). A 4.4-second interruption tripled it. This was done in the lab. Another was a field observation of 24 office workers (information workers). They switched work topic every 11 minutes on average. 57% of work topics were interrupted. Getting back to the interrupted work that day took 25 minutes 26 seconds on average. 2.26 other tasks were squeezed in between. A further experiment with 48 people: those interrupted worked faster, but reported higher stress, frustration, time pressure and effort.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Altmann, Trafton & Hambrick (2014). Momentary interruptions can derail the train of thought. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General. <https://doi.org/10.1037/a0030986>；Mark, Gonzalez & Harris (2005). No task left behind? Examining the nature of fragmented work. CHI 2005. <https://doi.org/10.1145/1054972.1055017>；Mark, Gudith & Klocke (2008). The cost of interrupted work: More speed and stress. CHI 2008. <https://doi.org/10.1145/1357054.1357072>
- Notes: The field study divided interruptions into two kinds: other people coming to you, and ones you open yourself. Closing the door only blocks the first kind. The second needs entry 7 (turn off non-essential notifications) and entry 14 (batch your messages) as well.

### 2. No caffeine after 2 p.m.
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
<!-- zh: 下午两点以后不碰咖啡因 | 13288d87 -->
- Cost: Free. People used to an afternoon coffee need a little willpower to go without.
- In plain words: A large coffee (400 mg) 6 hours before bed measurably cuts that night's sleep by over an hour. Yet people's own sleep diaries show no difference at all. An ordinary cup of coffee (107 mg) needs to be finished at least 8.8 hours before bed not to affect sleep. For someone who goes to bed at 11, 2 p.m. is the line.
- Benefit: 400 mg of caffeine 6 hours before bed reduced total sleep time measured by instruments by 1.1 to 1.2 hours compared with placebo. Placebo means a control drink without caffeine. But participants' own sleep diaries reported no difference; they could not notice it themselves. Pooling several studies (meta-analysis): caffeine reduced total sleep time by 45 minutes on average. The proportion of time in bed actually asleep (sleep efficiency) fell by 7%. A cup of coffee (107 mg) needs to be finished at least 8.8 hours before bed not to affect sleep.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Drake, Roehrs, Shambroom & Roth (2013). Caffeine effects on sleep taken 0, 3, or 6 hours before going to bed. Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.5664/jcsm.3170>；Gardiner et al. (2023). The effect of caffeine on subsequent sleep: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Sleep Medicine Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.smrv.2023.101764>
- Notes: The Drake study was done in only one lab, with a large 400 mg dose, about two or three cups of coffee. People who metabolize caffeine slowly need an even earlier cut-off. What less caffeine buys back is sleep time, which adds to entry 10 (sleep 7 to 8 hours a night).

### 3. Go to bed on time; do not stay up for games, short videos or porn
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
<!-- zh: 到点就睡，不为游戏、短视频、色情内容熬夜 | 54c8292a -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is resisting "one more episode" over the long run. Keeping the phone out of the bedroom takes the least effort.
- In plain words: People who use a phone or tablet before bed are more than twice as likely not to get enough sleep, and 2.7 times as likely to be sleepy in the day. The figures are similar even if the device is just in the bedroom and not used. So the simplest approach is to keep the phone out of the bedroom.
- Benefit: People using portable devices before bed were about 2.17 times as likely to get inadequate sleep (OR 2.17). About 1.46 times as likely to have poor sleep quality (OR 1.46). About 2.72 times as likely to be sleepy in the day (OR 2.72). With the device merely in the bedroom and not used, the figures were similar, about 1.79, 1.53 and 2.27 times (OR 1.79/1.53/2.27). This part pooled 20 surveys at a single point in time with 125,198 children and adolescents. Another single-time-point survey: among 844 adults aged 18 to 94, those who used their phone after lights out fell asleep more slowly, spent a lower proportion of time in bed asleep, and were more tired in the day.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Carter et al. (2016). Association Between Portable Screen-Based Media Device Access or Use and Sleep Outcomes. JAMA Pediatrics. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2016.2341>；Exelmans & Van den Bulck (2016). Bedtime mobile phone use and sleep in adults. Social Science & Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2015.11.037>；Grubbs JB, et al. (2018). Pornography Problems Due to Moral Incongruence: An Integrative Model with a Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Archives of Sexual Behavior. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s10508-018-1248-x> (one side of the dispute in the notes)；Prause N, Pfaus J (2015). Viewing Sexual Stimuli Associated with Greater Sexual Responsiveness, Not Erectile Dysfunction. Sexual Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1002/sm2.58> (the other side of the dispute in the notes)
- Notes: Disputed. For the cost of staying up late itself, see entry 10 of this section (sleep 7 to 8 hours a night). There is currently no reliable evidence on whether porn itself affects ordinary people's energy and mental performance. One analysis pooling several studies argues that so-called "porn problems" come more from a mismatch between a person's beliefs and their own behaviour. The problem is not the content itself. Another study pooled data from 280 men. It found that time spent watching was unrelated to erectile function during sex with a partner. This entry is only about porn taking time from sleep.

### 4. Do not work more than 49 hours a week
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
<!-- zh: 每周工作时间不要超过 49 小时 | 8fa9a7fa -->
- Cost: You may earn less overtime pay. You may also need to make the effort to talk to your boss.
- In plain words: Records from British munitions factories in the First World War show that up to 49 hours a week, output grew in step with hours. Beyond 49 hours, each extra hour produced less and less. A 70-hour week produced almost the same as a 56-hour week; the extra 14 hours were wasted.
- Benefit: Output records of British munitions workers in the First World War: up to 49 hours a week, output was proportional to hours. Beyond 49 hours, the growth in output began to slow. It peaked at about 63 hours. Output at 70 hours was hardly different from 56 hours; the extra 14 hours were effectively wasted.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Pencavel (2015). The Productivity of Working Hours. The Economic Journal. <https://doi.org/10.1111/ecoj.12166>
- Notes: The data come from factories a century ago, doing manual work, with mostly female workers. The threshold may not be the same for mental work; the author himself says other kinds of work may be higher or lower. What is reliable is the direction: past a certain point, working more produces almost nothing more. The specific number 49 is not reliable.

### 5. When you feel low, do the highest-value things first: move, get sunlight, sleep on schedule, talk to someone, call a helpline
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
<!-- zh: 情绪低落时先做性价比最高的几件事：动起来、晒太阳、按时睡、找人说、打热线 | 8f494d86 -->
- Cost: Free or very cheap. Brisk walking, jogging or strength training a few times a week. Getting out in daylight. A regular sleep schedule. One phone call when you feel you cannot go on.
- In plain words: For depression, brisk walking and jogging work best, yoga and strength training slightly less, and the harder the exercise the better. Light exposure works very well for seasonal depression and also helps non-seasonal depression. Online self-help cognitive behavioural therapy courses work, and with more severe symptoms it is better with someone guiding you. If you feel you cannot go on, first call the Yorisoi Hotline, where Chinese is spoken.
- Benefit: Pooling 218 randomized controlled trials with 14,170 people (network meta-analysis). The control groups were people doing other activities. Compared with them, the effect size of walking or jogging on depression was g = -0.62 (95% credible interval -0.80 to -0.45). Yoga -0.55, strength training -0.49. The harder the exercise, the better the effect. Pooling several light therapy studies: effect size 0.84 (0.60 to 1.08) for seasonal depression and 0.53 (0.18 to 0.89) for non-seasonal depression. Another analysis pooled individual patient data from each study. It looked at internet-based cognitive behavioural therapy (online self-help courses). Both guided and unguided versions beat control. For moderate to severe cases (PHQ-9 above 9), the guided version was better.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Noetel M, et al. (2024). Effect of exercise for depression: systematic review and network meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-075847>；Golden RN, et al. (2005). The efficacy of light therapy in the treatment of mood disorders: a review and meta-analysis of the evidence. American Journal of Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.162.4.656>；Karyotaki E, et al. (2021). Internet-Based Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Depression: A Systematic Review and Individual Patient Data Network Meta-analysis. JAMA Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2020.4364>
- Notes: These are supporting measures for mild to moderate depression. Moderate to severe cases, or anyone with suicidal thoughts, need medical care; call a helpline first; see Section 1, entry 2 (Yorisoi Hotline). Most exercise trials are small, and cannot hide who is exercising the way drug trials can. The light studies only go up to 2003, also with few people. "Sleep on schedule" and "talk to someone" are the author's experience, without separate literature. Do not treat "move" as a demand on people with depression; walking for ten minutes counts.

### 6. Do not make "how others are doing" daily reading: limit or turn off apps for scrolling through peers' updates
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
<!-- zh: 别把「别人过得怎么样」当每日必读：给刷同龄人动态的应用设限或关掉 | 3f813338 -->
- Cost: Free. Setting app limits or deactivating an account takes a few minutes. The hard part is not opening them again afterwards, a long fight against habit.
- In plain words: People who habitually compare themselves online with those who seem better off tend to feel worse and have lower self-esteem. People who deactivated Facebook for four weeks became slightly happier, but their knowledge of current events also dropped. People who limited three social platforms to 10 minutes a day each saw loneliness and depression fall after three weeks.
- Benefit: Pooling several studies (meta-analysis): 54 independent samples, 94 effect sizes, N = 36,583, using a three-level random-effects model. The average correlation between online upward social comparison and psychological maladjustment was r = 0.330 (95% CI 0.289–0.370, confidence range). Broken down by outcome: socially evaluative negative emotion such as fear of being looked down on 0.438, anxiety 0.382. Depression 0.306, low well-being 0.268, low self-esteem 0.263. Age, cultural background and the year of data collection did not affect the strength of the association. Long-term follow-up studies did not give stronger results than single-time-point surveys either. Separately, a randomized controlled trial with 2,743 US Facebook users. 61% of them were willing to deactivate for less than 102 dollars. They were randomized to a four-week deactivation group or a control group. Deactivation freed up 60 minutes a day for a typical participant. A subjective well-being index rose by 0.09 standard deviations. By component: life satisfaction 0.12, anxiety 0.10, depression 0.09, happiness 0.08. These still held after adjusting for chance from multiple comparisons. The authors gave a reference point: positive psychology interventions. These improve subjective well-being by about +0.34 standard deviations and depression by about -0.23. So this effect is about 25% to 40% of a typical psychological intervention. The costs over the same period: a current events knowledge index fell by 0.19 standard deviations, and political polarization fell by 0.16 standard deviations. After the experiment, the deactivation group still used Facebook 12 minutes a day less than control (0.31 standard deviations, a 23% reduction). Another randomized controlled trial with 143 undergraduates, randomized into two groups. One limited Facebook, Instagram and Snapchat to 10 minutes per platform per day; the other used them as usual. After three weeks, loneliness and depression fell clearly in the limited group. Both groups had lower anxiety and fear of missing out than at the start.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Lei Y, Hu S, Sun Y, Zheng L (2026). "Looking up" linked to feeling down: a meta-analysis of online upward social comparison and psychological maladjustment. Frontiers in Psychology. <https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1825169>；Allcott H, Braghieri L, Eichmeyer S, Gentzkow M (2020). The Welfare Effects of Social Media. American Economic Review, 110(3), 629–676. <https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20190658>；Allcott H, Braghieri L, Eichmeyer S, Gentzkow M (2019). The Welfare Effects of Social Media. NBER Working Paper No. 25514 (the specific figures cited here were checked word for word against this version; the published abstract has no figures). <https://www.nber.org/papers/w25514>；Hunt MG, Marx R, Lipson C, Young J (2018). No More FOMO: Limiting Social Media Decreases Loneliness and Depression. Journal of Social and Clinical Psychology, 37(10), 751–768. <https://doi.org/10.1521/jscp.2018.37.10.751>
- Notes: The "correlation" half only shows two things occurring together and cannot be read as one causing the other; the authors themselves say more long-term studies are needed. The randomized half can speak to cause, but the participants were US Facebook users before the 2018 midterm elections. Applying the conclusions to other platforms is a stretch. The Hunt study had only 143 undergraduates and is a single study. The effect sizes are all small, so do not expect turning off an app to solve mood problems. What to block is the "seeing how others are doing" kind of use; keeping in touch with family and friends, looking things up and job hunting are not included. If you really cannot hold back, start by turning off notifications, "people you may know" and the home feed. For total screen time, see Section 25, entry 10 (set a hard limit on short videos and aimless scrolling) and Section 25, entry 6 (do not watch TV and rolling news). For what to do first when you feel low, see entry 5 of this section (move, get sunlight, sleep on schedule).

### 7. Turn off non-essential notifications, and keep your phone out of sight while working
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
<!-- zh: 关掉非必要通知，工作时把手机放到视线之外 | 251ad56c -->
- Cost: Free. A few minutes changing settings, needing almost no willpower.
- In plain words: When your phone buzzes, even if you do not look at or touch it, your score on an attention task drops. It drops about as much as if you actually took a call or sent a text. Also: with the phone just lying on the desk, even if you resist looking at it, your available mental capacity shrinks. The more dependent on the phone you are, the more it drops.
- Benefit: Merely receiving a notification, without looking or touching, clearly worsened performance on an attention task. The drop was comparable to actually taking a call or texting. This was done in the lab with university students. Two further experiments showed that even when people successfully resisted checking their phone, having their own phone nearby reduced available mental capacity. People most dependent on their phones dropped the most.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Stothart, Mitchum & Yehnert (2015). The attentional cost of receiving a cell phone notification. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance. <https://doi.org/10.1037/xhp0000100>；Ward, Duke, Gneezy & Bos (2017). Brain Drain: The Mere Presence of One's Own Smartphone Reduces Available Cognitive Capacity. Journal of the Association for Consumer Research. <https://doi.org/10.1086/691462>
- Notes: These are all single lab tasks. What happens when this accumulates every day has not been measured. In jobs where you really must be reachable, you can keep notifications on for just a few contacts.

### 8. If you get sleepy in the afternoon, nap for 10 minutes, not half an hour
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
<!-- zh: 下午困了就睡 10 分钟，不要睡半小时 | bca6a716 -->
- Cost: Free. 10 minutes, plus somewhere you can lie down or put your head on the desk.
- In plain words: A 10-minute nap refreshes you right away, and the effect lasts two and a half hours. A 5-minute nap does almost nothing. A 20-minute nap only works after half an hour. After a 30-minute nap, you are groggy at first and perform worse than if you had not slept.
- Benefit: After a night of restricted sleep, a 10-minute nap immediately improved sleepiness, fatigue, vigour and cognitive performance, with some benefits lasting beyond 155 minutes. A 5-minute nap gave almost no benefit. A 20-minute nap's benefits only appeared after 35 minutes. A 30-minute nap was followed first by a period of sleep inertia: performance was worse right after waking, and only improved later.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Brooks & Lack (2006). A brief afternoon nap following nocturnal sleep restriction: Which nap duration is most recuperative? Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/29.6.831>
- Notes: A single lab study, with healthy young participants. If you cannot fall asleep, do not force it. No research supports whether lying with your eyes closed for 10 minutes helps. Naps of over an hour are, on the contrary, linked to higher mortality and coronary heart disease risk; the original book's section "don't die slowly" has a dedicated entry on this.

### 9. To calm down on the spot, do 5 minutes of "cyclic sighing": two inhales, then a long exhale
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 当场想缓过来，用 5 分钟「循环叹息」：吸气两段，呼气拉长 | ea1e10a7 -->
- Cost: Free. 5 minutes a day.
- In plain words: Doing 5 minutes of breathing exercises a day for a month improved mood and slowed breathing more than the same time of mindfulness meditation. Among several breathing methods, "cyclic sighing", which lengthens the exhale, was best. Inhale once through the nose, then take a second small inhale to fill the lungs, then breathe out slowly and fully through the mouth. This is a single trial, with outcomes self-reported, but it costs nothing.
- Benefit: This was a randomized controlled trial done at home, comparing three daily 5-minute breathing exercises with the same time of mindfulness meditation, for 1 month. The three breathing methods were: cyclic sighing (emphasizing a longer exhale), box breathing (equal inhale, hold and exhale), and cyclic hyperventilation with retention (long inhale, short exhale). Analysing everyone's data from every session together (a mixed-effects model), the breathing exercises all beat mindfulness meditation, especially exhale-focused cyclic sighing. It was better both in mood improvement (P<0.05, meaning this difference does not look like chance) and in reduced breathing rate (P<0.05)
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Balban MY, Neri E, Kogon MM, et al. (2023). Brief structured respiration practices enhance mood and reduce physiological arousal. Cell Reports Medicine, 4(1), 100895. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895>
- Notes: Inhale once through the nose, take a brief second small inhale to fill the lungs, then breathe out slowly and fully through the mouth, repeating for 5 minutes. There is only this one trial, done at home, with outcomes reported by participants on questionnaires and few people. It is only slightly better than meditation and solves no problems. But it costs nothing, takes only 5 minutes and can be done anywhere, so it is good value.

### 10. Sleep 7 to 8 hours every night; do not treat 6 hours as enough
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
<!-- zh: 每晚睡够 7 到 8 小时，别把 6 小时当作够用 | a5f1b94b -->
- Cost: Free. You need to set aside 1 to 2 more hours a day for sleep. Going to bed earlier takes a little willpower.
- In plain words: Sleeping only 6 hours a night for two weeks drops mental performance to the level of going two days and nights without sleep. The trouble is you cannot feel it. Self-rated sleepiness rises over the first few days and then stops rising, and people sleeping 6 hours rate themselves as sleepy as those sleeping 4. So saying "6 hours is enough for me" is itself a symptom of sleep loss.
- Benefit: 48 healthy adults aged 21 to 38 slept only 6 hours a night for 14 days in a row. Their mental performance declined cumulatively, to the level of 2 nights without any sleep. Yet their self-rated sleepiness only rose over the first few days, then hardly increased. The 6-hour and 4-hour groups did not differ in self-rated sleepiness. This was a randomized controlled trial (RCT). In addition, short-term sleep loss (acute sleep deprivation, up to 48 hours) had an effect size of g = -0.776 on simple attention lapses and g = -0.125 on reasoning accuracy. This part pooled 70 studies.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Van Dongen, Maislin, Mullington & Dinges (2003). The cumulative cost of additional wakefulness. Sleep. <https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/26.2.117>；Lim & Dinges (2010). A meta-analysis of the impact of short-term sleep deprivation on cognitive variables. Psychological Bulletin. <https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018883>
- Notes: "I don't feel sleepy" is not evidence of enough sleep. People who habitually sleep 6 hours only feel used to it; their performance has not kept up. The pooled studies are about going a whole night without sleep. "Chronically sleeping less" is supported by only one randomized controlled trial. The relationship between sleep duration and risk of death is a separate account; see Section 20, entry 33 (sleep about 7 hours a night).

### 11. Treat "seeing people regularly" as a health expense, rather than only reaching out when you feel bad
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 把「定期和人见面」当成健康支出，别只在心情差时才找人 | 402a8517 -->
- Cost: It costs a little money, plus the time spent meeting. The hard part is taking the initiative to arrange meetings when you feel fine too.
- In plain words: People who rarely see others are about 30% more likely to die in the same period. People who feel lonely are about a quarter more likely, and people living alone about 30%, more so for those under 65. These all come from following people over time, and it may also be that people already in poor health are more likely to live alone.
- Benefit: This is the result of pooling 70 studies that recorded people's situations first and then followed them (meta-analysis), with 3.4 million participants followed for 7 years on average. Social isolation, loneliness and living alone were all associated with higher mortality. After removing confounders such as age and health status, the weighted average odds ratios were: social isolation 1.29, loneliness 1.26, living alone 1.32. Converted, the chance of dying in the same period was about 30%, about a quarter and about 30% higher respectively. The three effect sizes were similar, and larger in people under 65 than in older people
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Holt-Lunstad J, Smith TB, Baker M, Harris T, Stephenson D (2015). Loneliness and social isolation as risk factors for mortality: a meta-analytic review. Perspectives on Psychological Science, 10(2), 227-237. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691614568352>
- Notes: Disputed. These are associations observed by following groups of people, not randomized trials, so cause and effect cannot be separated. People already in poor health are more likely to live alone and socialize less. The authors place social relationships at the same level as smoking and obesity as a health factor, but there is no randomized trial showing that "more socializing lowers mortality". This entry does not conflict with entry 21 of this section (cut back relationships that drain you): that entry cuts relationships that drain you, while this one keeps the few that restore your energy.

### 12. When anxiety affects your life, a mindfulness-based stress reduction course works no worse than a common anti-anxiety drug
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 焦虑到影响生活时，正念减压课的效果不劣于常用的抗焦虑药 | 380c6dec -->
- Cost: The course costs a little money. It runs 8 weeks, with one class a week and daily practice at home. The hard part is practising every day for 8 weeks straight.
- In plain words: People diagnosed with an anxiety disorder who took an 8-week mindfulness-based stress reduction course improved about as much as those taking the anti-anxiety drug escitalopram. Nearly 80% of those on the drug had side effects, and some dropped out because of them. About 15% of the course group had any, and no one dropped out. It has to be the standardized 8-week course; any meditation app does not count.
- Benefit: This trial was done at 3 urban academic medical centres in the US, to see whether the course is worse than the drug (a non-inferiority randomized controlled trial). 276 adults diagnosed with anxiety disorders were randomized 1:1. One group took an 8-week mindfulness-based stress reduction course. The other took escitalopram (flexible dose of 10 to 20 mg). 208 completed the trial. At the end, by the doctor-rated anxiety severity score (Clinical Global Impression of Severity scale), the mindfulness group fell by 1.35 and the drug group by 1.43. The difference was −0.07 (95% CI −0.38 to 0.23, meaning the true difference roughly falls in this range; P=0.65, meaning this difference cannot be told apart from no difference). The lower bound of this range did not cross the pre-set line for "not meaningfully worse" (non-inferiority margin −0.495). Side effects differed greatly: 78.6% of the drug group had at least one trial-related adverse event, and 10 dropped out because of them. In the mindfulness group 15.4%, with no one dropping out because of them
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Hoge EA, Bui E, Mete M, Dutton MA, Baker AW, Simon NM (2023). Mindfulness-based stress reduction vs escitalopram for the treatment of adults with anxiety disorders: a randomized clinical trial. JAMA Psychiatry, 80(1), 13-21. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2022.3679>
- Notes: The trial used the standardized 8-week mindfulness-based stress reduction course (MBSR); any meditation app cannot be equated with it. It applies to people who already meet the diagnostic criteria for an anxiety disorder, who should still see a doctor. If you have kept putting off treatment because of worries about drug side effects, this is an option with comparable strength of evidence.

### 13. Get up at a fixed time, weekends included
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
<!-- zh: 固定起床时间，周末也一样 | cd18ea72 -->
- Cost: Free. Sleeping in less at weekends takes a little willpower.
- In plain words: The fifth of people with the most irregular schedules had body clocks about 2.5 hours later than the most regular fifth. It is like living permanently in jet lag. Students with more regular schedules also got better grades.
- Benefit: 61 university students were recorded for 30 days. The most irregular fifth had body clocks (circadian rhythms) about 2.5 hours later than the most regular fifth. Specifically, melatonin onset times of 00:08 versus 21:32. The sleepiest period was also about 1.8 hours later. More regular schedules went with better academic performance (correlation coefficient r = 0.37).
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Phillips et al. (2017). Irregular sleep/wake patterns are associated with poorer academic performance and delayed circadian and sleep/wake timing. Scientific Reports. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-03171-4>
- Notes: A single study, with few people and no randomized comparison. The researchers estimate that the difference in body clocks mainly came from differences in daily light exposure. So a fixed wake-up time works only together with morning light. After the occasional late night, the fix is to go to bed earlier that night, not to sleep until noon the next day.

### 14. Handle emails and messages in batches at a few fixed times a day

- Cost: Free. It takes some time to tell colleagues how often you reply. Resisting checking email at any moment takes a little willpower.

In the week limited to checking email 3 times a day, daily stress was clearly lower than in the unlimited week (effect size Cohen's d = 0.37). The number of emails received and answered did not differ between the two weeks. Initially, without limits, people checked email 15.5 times a day on average.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Kushlev & Dunn (2015). Checking email less frequently reduces stress. Computers in Human Behavior. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2014.11.005>
- Notes: A single study, with a small-to-moderate effect, measuring stress rather than work done. Instant messaging has not been studied directly; this is an extrapolation.

### 15. Do one thing at a time; do not answer messages during meetings
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
<!-- zh: 一次只做一件事，不要边开会边回消息 | ae671388 -->
- Cost: Free. Resisting answering messages in meetings takes a little willpower.
- In plain words: Every time you switch tasks, you react more slowly and make more mistakes. People who often have several screens open have not learned to do several things at once; they actually do worse on task-switching tests.
- Benefit: After switching tasks, reactions are clearly slower and more error-prone. This part comes from a review of many studies. Another lab study had university students as participants. People who often use several media at once (heavy media multitaskers) were more easily distracted by irrelevant things and irrelevant memories. They actually did worse on task-switching tests.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Monsell (2003). Task switching. Trends in Cognitive Sciences. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S1364-6613(03)00028-7>；Ophir, Nass & Wagner (2009). Cognitive control in media multitaskers. PNAS. <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0903620106>
- Notes: How much switching back and forth costs varies greatly with the work, so no single figure is given here. The Ophir study compared people at a single point in time, so it cannot tell whether "multitasking worsens attention" or "people with poor attention like multitasking".

### 16. When you notice yourself going over the same bad thing again and again, switch to something hands-on

- Cost: Free. The hard part is first noticing that you are going over it again and again (called rumination), and noticing itself takes practice.
- In plain words: Going over the same bad thing again and again does not produce an answer. It only deepens depression, fixes negative thoughts more firmly, makes you worse at solving problems and less able to act, and wears out the people around you who are willing to listen. When you notice yourself going round in circles, go and do something hands-on.
- Benefit: A review of many studies says that repeatedly going over the same bad thing (rumination) deepens depression. It also reinforces negative thinking and weakens problem-solving. It gets in the way of actual action and wears out the people around you who are willing to listen. It is associated with anxiety, binge eating, heavy drinking and self-harm.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Nolen-Hoeksema, Wisco & Lyubomirsky (2008). Rethinking Rumination. Perspectives on Psychological Science. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6924.2008.00088.x>
- Notes: The review only gives a direction, no numbers. "Switch to something" is the author's own approach. All the literature supports is that diverting attention beats continuing to go round in circles. What to switch to has not been compared.

### 17. Treat thoughts like "things are bound to get worse" as a symptom, not a fact
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
<!-- zh: 把「事情肯定会更糟」这类念头当症状看，不当事实看 | b64d1ec4 -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is treating such thoughts as a symptom rather than a fact.
- In plain words: In a twin study followed for 20 years, each step up in pessimism meant about 13% higher chance of dying in the same period. The chance of dying of cardiovascular disease was about 20% higher. Conversely, high optimism scores brought no corresponding benefit. So what needs dealing with is pessimism, not "not being positive enough".
- Benefit: 2,978 Australian twins aged over 50 were followed for 20 years on average, during which 1,068 died. Each 1 standard deviation higher on the pessimism scale meant about 13% higher risk of dying in the same period (hazard ratio 1.134). The confidence range for this figure is 95% CI 1.065–1.207, P=8.85×10⁻⁵. Cardiovascular death was about 20% higher (1.196, 1.045–1.368, P=0.0093). It was unrelated to cancer death. Optimism scores also had no clear association with mortality.
- Evidence: B
- Sources: Whitfield JB, Zhu G, Landers JG, Martin NG (2020). Pessimism is associated with greater all-cause and cardiovascular mortality, but optimism is not protective. Scientific Reports, 10, 12609. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-69388-y>
- Notes: Disputed. This is only observational, without randomization. Some would say "illness comes first, then pessimism"; the authors counter that if so, cancer death should also be associated. But unmeasured factors are still possible. Optimism has no protective effect, so there is no need to force yourself to "think positive". What to do is not to treat pessimistic expectations as accurate predictions of the future.

### 18. When angry, leave the scene first, and treat the other person like the weather rather than an enemy

- Cost: Free. In the moment, hold back from answering back, turn and walk away, and decide later whether to deal with it. This takes some practice.
- In plain words: Reinterpreting what just happened, or putting yourself in the other person's shoes, both genuinely lower the emotion, the latter more so. Forcibly suppressing it without showing it does not work. Also, people who punished someone who offended them kept going over it for longer afterwards; those who did not retaliate moved on faster. People overestimate in advance how satisfying revenge will be.

Distraction d = 0.27. Suppressing feelings did not work (d = -0.04). People overestimated in advance the satisfaction revenge would bring.

Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. <https://doi.org/10.1037/a0012165>
- Notes: The pooled part measures intensity of emotion, not energy, with small to moderate effects. The revenge part is a single set of experiments, hence grade B. The "empty boat" is a parable from Zhuangzi's "The Mountain Tree": you do not get angry when an empty boat drifts into you, only when there is someone in it, though the bump is the same. ### 19. When angry, do not vent by smashing things, hitting a punchbag or going for a run; slow your body down first

<!-- zh: 生气时别靠砸东西、打沙袋、跑一圈来发泄，先让身体慢下来 | e84e1fe7 -->
- Cost: Free. In the heat of anger, spend a few minutes doing something that slows your heart: breathe slowly, sit down, be still. The hard part is that venting is exactly what you most want to do at that moment.
- In plain words: Venting methods that make the body more aroused, such as hitting a punchbag, throwing things or going for a run, do not reduce anger overall. Methods that calm the body, such as slow breathing, mindfulness and meditation, clearly reduce anger and the urge to lash out.
- Benefit: Pooling 154 studies (meta-analysis), with 184 samples and 10,189 people. Methods that calm the body, such as deep breathing, mindfulness and meditation, reduced anger and aggression. Effect size g = -0.63, 95% CI -0.82 to -0.43 (confidence range). This result was stable across sex, ethnicity, age and culture. Methods that make the body more aroused, such as hitting a punchbag, jogging and cycling, did not work overall. Their effect size was g = -0.02, 95% CI -0.13 to 0.09. The authors concluded that venting anger or going for a run are not effective ways to cool down.
- Evidence: A
- Sources: Kjærvik SL, Bushman BJ (2024). A meta-analytic review of anger management activities that increase or decrease arousal: What fuels or douses rage? Clinical Psychology Review. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2024.102414>
- Notes: What was measured is anger and aggression, not energy. Recording it under time is the author's classification: one fewer argument means one fewer mess to clean up. For what to do on the spot, see entry 18 of this section (when angry, leave the scene first). Running is not useless; over the long run it helps low mood; see entry 5 of this section (move, get sunlight, sleep on schedule). It is just not suited to cooling down on the spot. In the heat of anger, do not drive, send messages or make decisions. If someone is hitting you, leave first and call the police. Then what needs handling is not your emotions; see Section 11, entry 2 (escaping domestic violence).

### 21. Cut back on relationships that drain you, and learn to refuse requests you do not want to take on

### 27. Treat "others demand that I be perfect" as a symptom, not a fact

- Cost: Free. The hard part is treating such thoughts as something to deal with, rather than as an accurate measure of what others really demand.
- In plain words: People who constantly feel "those around me demand that I be perfect, and if I fail I'll be rejected" are more prone to suicidal thoughts. "I demand perfection of myself" is much more weakly linked to suicidal thoughts, about a third of the former. After removing thoughts already present at the start, the former feeling is still linked to more such thoughts later, though not by much.

Perfectionistic concerns is a composite including socially prescribed perfectionism, concern over mistakes, doubts about actions and the gap between reality and standards. Socially prescribed perfectionism alone was also r = .28 (.25–.32, 30 studies, 3,640 people). - Notes: Why grade B: most included studies are one-off questionnaires that cannot show which came first. The correlation is moderate (r about .28), explaining only a few to ten percent of the variation.
